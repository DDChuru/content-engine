## The causal spine

The syllabus asks for two things about each factor: **investigate** it (a design with a measured rate) and **explain** it (a cause at the active site). One sentence carries both factors, and 3.2.1b reuses it word for word:

> **The rate depends on how often substrate molecules meet a working active site and form enzyme–substrate complexes per unit time, and on whether the active sites stay functional.**

**Temperature.** Measured on the gas-syringe rig at five maintained water-bath temperatures, enzyme and substrate equilibrated separately for the same ten minutes, initial rate from each progress curve's t = 0 tangent, mean of three. Below the optimum, more kinetic energy → more collisions and more effective collisions per unit time → more enzyme–substrate complexes per unit time → higher rate. Above the optimum, increasing denaturation: hydrogen and ionic bonds between R groups break, the tertiary structure and the active-site shape change, the substrate is no longer complementary, fewer functional active sites, fewer complexes per unit time. Peptide bonds are not broken. The particles do not lose kinetic energy when the rate falls; low activity in the cold is not denaturation.

**pH, using buffer solutions.** Measured by the S22/33 Q1(a) disc method with the buffer pH of the enzyme-soaking mixture varied across five values; three whole-second rise times; mean; 1/mean rise time as a relative rate proxy. Away from the optimum, R groups at the active site gain or lose hydrogen ions, their charges change, ionic and hydrogen bonds are altered, the active site changes shape, fewer complexes per unit time. A modest change need not mean irreversible unfolding; extreme pH can denature.

**An optimum belongs to one enzyme under stated conditions**: this catalase preparation in these set-ups; a protease around pH 6.1 in S24/32; a lipase at pH 8 in W20/21. No sentence says or implies that enzymes in general work best at 37 °C or at pH 7. No Q10 rule.

**What the mark schemes credit, quoted:** [S22/33 Q1(b)(v), MS p.6] "correct reference to increase in kinetic energy ; more effective collisions ; more enzyme substrate complexes ; active site changes shape ; enzyme denatured ; fewer enzyme substrate complexes ;". [S24/32 Q1(b)(ii), MS p.7, any three] "optimum pH around 6.1 ; (away from optimum) shape of the active site is changed ; (from pH1.8 to pH6.1) more enzyme substrate complexes / (from pH6.1 to pH8.5) less enzyme substrate complexes / (at optimum pH) maximum number of enzyme substrate complexes ; low activity at extreme pH ;". [S22/33 Q1(a), MS p.6] "oxygen is released (causing the discs to rise) ;", "records three times for each pH ;", "records a mean time for each pH ;", "results recorded as whole seconds ;", "use boiled enzyme ;", "contaminated with enzyme" / "use fresh hydrogen peroxide for each test". Validation without quotation: specimen P1 Q15, key C (above the optimum temperature: R-group bonds broken, tertiary structure altered, active site no longer complementary; weights S-B); S22/33 Q1(a)(vii), optimum pH located by values either side (weights ledger); W20/21 Q2(b)(iii), a lipase optimum of pH 8 among the credited points (weights ledger). So the spine is what is credited: kinetic energy, effective collisions, complexes, active-site shape change, denatured, fewer complexes; an optimum read from the data; low activity at extreme pH.

**The handle:** *press studs, not stitches.* The fold is held by press studs (hydrogen and ionic bonds between R groups); the chain is stitched (peptide bonds). Heat pops the studs; the stitching stays. Converted at once in the same beat: *bonds between R groups break, peptide bonds do not, and the enzyme is denatured.* The handle is never the exam answer.

**Typicality rules applied:** optima are stated for this catalase in this set-up, with the range located by the values either side, never as a universal temperature or pH. "Held partly by hydrogen bonds and ionic bonds" (other interactions exist; the syllabus depth names these two for pH and the plan names them for temperature). "Some [R groups] carry charges". "A modest change … need not unfold"; "activity can return"; "enough bonds can break". "Collects no measurable gas" (not "no gas"). The hook's claims are hedged: "much of your chemistry", "one way it keeps". The word *denatured* is used throughout; the rejected vocabulary for heat damage is neither spoken nor shown.

Bubbles gather on the disc; it lifts and rises; **timer stops as the disc reaches the surface**. Times recorded in **whole seconds**.

**`EnzymeActiveSiteModel` (published by 3.1.1-2; `rest-lk` default; caption *schematic; not a real protein shape*).** For this lesson the silhouette shows its faint backbone line (the 3.1.1-2 Beat 6 trace) with the 2.3.3 bond legend: **four dashed links (hydrogen bonds)** and **two dotted links (ionic bonds)** between loops of the backbone, one dotted link lying across the cleft wall; the two R-group stubs it joins at the cleft carry **+** and **−** tags.
- **`denatured` motion (first specified here; SHARED-SPECS §5):** a small thermometer glyph beside the model rises; the model jitters slightly faster; the dashed and dotted links **vanish one by one** (about 0.25 s apart, cue-synced); the silhouette **loosens** (loops drift apart over about 0.8 s); the cleft **distorts**; the backbone line stays **continuous, never cut**, and is traced once with the tag **peptide bonds intact**. A substrate then arrives, **rocks and fails to seat**, and drifts away. Caption word **denatured**.
- **`ph-shifted` sub-state (published here, reversible):** the **+**/**−** tags on the R groups lining the cleft change (one **−** becomes neutral, one neutral stub gains **+**); the dotted ionic link across the cleft wall breaks; the cleft shape **shifts** over about 0.6 s; the silhouette stays tight and the other links stay. At an **extreme pH** the model runs the full `denatured` motion instead.
- **Cold state (no new asset):** `rest-lk`, every link present, substrate tokens moving slowly; tag **not denatured**.
