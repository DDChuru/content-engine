## The causal spine

The syllabus asks for two things about each factor: **investigate** it (a design with a measured rate) and **explain** it (a cause at the active site). One sentence carries both factors, and 3.2.1b reuses it word for word:

> **The rate depends on how often substrate molecules meet a working active site and form enzyme–substrate complexes per unit time, and on whether the active sites stay functional.**

**Temperature.** Measured on the gas-syringe rig at five maintained water-bath temperatures, enzyme and substrate equilibrated separately for the same ten minutes, initial rate from each progress curve's t = 0 tangent, mean of three. Below the optimum, more kinetic energy → more collisions and more effective collisions per unit time → more enzyme–substrate complexes per unit time → higher rate. Above the optimum, increasing denaturation: hydrogen and ionic bonds between R groups break, the tertiary structure and the active-site shape change, the substrate is no longer complementary, fewer functional active sites, fewer complexes per unit time. Peptide bonds are not broken. The particles do not lose kinetic energy when the rate falls; low activity in the cold is not denaturation.

**pH, using buffer solutions.** Measured by the S22/33 Q1(a) disc method with the buffer pH of the enzyme-soaking mixture varied across five values; three whole-second rise times; mean; 1/mean rise time as a relative rate proxy. Away from the optimum, R groups at the active site gain or lose hydrogen ions, their charges change, ionic and hydrogen bonds are altered, the active site changes shape, fewer complexes per unit time. A modest change need not mean irreversible unfolding; extreme pH can denature.

**An optimum belongs to one enzyme under stated conditions**: this catalase preparation in these set-ups; a protease around pH 6.1 in S24/32; a lipase at pH 8 in W20/21. No sentence says or implies that enzymes in general work best at 37 °C or at pH 7. No Q10 rule.

**What the mark schemes credit, quoted:** [S22/33 Q1(b)(v), MS p.6] "correct reference to increase in kinetic energy ; more effective collisions ; more enzyme substrate complexes ; active site changes shape ; enzyme denatured ; fewer enzyme substrate complexes ;". [S24/32 Q1(b)(ii), MS p.7, any three] "optimum pH around 6.1 ; (away from optimum) shape of the active site is changed ; (from pH1.8 to pH6.1) more enzyme substrate complexes / (from pH6.1 to pH8.5) less enzyme substrate complexes / (at optimum pH) maximum number of enzyme substrate complexes ; low activity at extreme pH ;". [S22/33 Q1(a), MS p.6] "oxygen is released (causing the discs to rise) ;", "records three times for each pH ;", "records a mean time for each pH ;", "results recorded as whole seconds ;", "use boiled enzyme ;", "contaminated with enzyme" / "use fresh hydrogen peroxide for each test". Validation without quotation: specimen P1 Q15, key C (above the optimum temperature: R-group bonds broken, tertiary structure altered, active site no longer complementary; weights S-B); S22/33 Q1(a)(vii), optimum pH located by values either side (weights ledger); W20/21 Q2(b)(iii), a lipase optimum of pH 8 among the credited points (weights ledger). So the spine is what is credited: kinetic energy, effective collisions, complexes, active-site shape change, denatured, fewer complexes; an optimum read from the data; low activity at extreme pH.

**The handle:** *press studs, not stitches.* The fold is held by press studs (hydrogen and ionic bonds between R groups); the chain is stitched (peptide bonds). Heat pops the studs; the stitching stays. Converted at once in the same beat: *bonds between R groups break, peptide bonds do not, and the enzyme is denatured.* The handle is never the exam answer.

**Typicality rules applied:** optima are stated for this catalase in this set-up, with the range located by the values either side, never as a universal temperature or pH. "Held partly by hydrogen bonds and ionic bonds" (other interactions exist; the syllabus depth names these two for pH and the plan names them for temperature). "Some [R groups] carry charges". "A modest change … need not unfold"; "activity can return"; "enough bonds can break". "Collects no measurable gas" (not "no gas"). The hook's claims are hedged: "much of your chemistry", "one way it keeps". The word *denatured* is used throughout; the rejected vocabulary for heat damage is neither spoken nor shown.

## The models, specified once

**`GasSyringeRig` + `WaterBathRig` (published by 3.1.3; reused, with an added three-way tap and the added states below).** Per SHARED-SPECS §5: conical flask with **10.0 cm³ hydrogen peroxide solution, 0.20 mol dm⁻³**; a **small test tube standing upright inside it** holding **5.0 cm³ yeast suspension** (made up in *buffer solution, pH 7.0*; swirled before every aliquot is measured); bung with delivery tube **seated before mixing**; a **three-way tap** on the delivery line between the flask and the syringe (added here; positions *vent* = flask open to the air, syringe shut off; *collect* = flask to syringe, vent shut); **gas syringe** clamped horizontally. Labels as text nodes: *conical flask*, *hydrogen peroxide solution*, *small tube: yeast suspension (catalase)*, *bung*, *delivery tube*, *three-way tap*, *gas syringe*, *water bath*, *thermometer*. The flask stands in a **thermostatically controlled water bath** with the thermometer in the bath water; the syringe sits above the bath.
- **Added state `equilibrate-separate` (published here):** bung seated, tap set to **vent**, so gas that expands or contracts as the flask reaches the bath temperature escapes or enters through the vent and is not collected by the syringe; the flask in the bath with a **10:00 countdown** on a bath timer; small heat-shimmer ticks on both the peroxide and the inner tube, the two liquids visibly **not in contact** for the full ten minutes. **Each temperature is a separate run**: its own flask, own inner tube, own bath held at that temperature (10, 20, 30, 40 or 50 °C), own 10:00 countdown; one flask never changes temperature during an equilibration. The 10 °C bath is drawn with its cooling unit and the bath water cooled (small tag *cooled bath*).
- **Baseline (end of equilibration, published here):** with the countdown at 0:00 and the tap still set to **vent**, the plunger is set to **0 cm³**; the tap is then turned to **collect**, closing the vent; tag *baseline 0 cm³ set after equilibration; collection path gas-tight*. The liquids are still apart. After mixing, the system is never opened and the plunger is never pushed back.
- **Mixing (handling contract):** a hand grips the flask **by its neck**, tilts it about 50° from upright; the inner tube topples against the flask wall so that **its mouth is below its base** and the suspension runs out of its lip into the peroxide; liquid surfaces stay level; the peroxide never reaches the delivery tube. **On the frame the suspension first touches the peroxide, the stopwatch starts from 0 s and collection starts (t = 0)**; the stopwatch is already running when the flask returns upright into the bath, and it is never reset; the plunger moves out smoothly as gas collects.
- **Control (enzyme-free, matched):** the same rig, the same sequence (vent, equilibration, baseline, tilt) and the same thermal history, with **5.0 cm³ of the same enzyme-free buffer solution, pH 7.0** (the suspension's vehicle, with no yeast) in the inner tube in place of the yeast suspension; label *enzyme-free buffer, pH 7.0, in place of yeast suspension*.

**`CatalaseDiscRig` (published by 3.1.3 as a transfer example; this lesson runs the pH series on it).** The S22/33 Q1(a) design, per the plan's disc-rise paragraph. All tubes stand **in a rack in a glass-sided water bath at 30 °C** (thermometer in the bath).
- **Soaking mixtures (`BufferedTubeRig`, five-buffer state published here):** five labelled tubes, each **2.0 cm³ yeast suspension + 8.0 cm³ buffer solution**, labels *buffer solution, pH 5.0 / 6.0 / 7.0 / 8.0 / 9.0*; enzyme volume, buffer volume, total volume and standing time (10 min) the same in every tube. For this series the yeast is suspended in distilled water, so the only buffer in each soaking mixture is the one whose pH is varied (see *Datasets*). Every pipette delivery is made with the **tip held just above the receiving tube's mouth, not touching**. A sixth tube is the control: **2.0 cm³ boiled yeast suspension + 8.0 cm³ buffer solution, pH 7.0**; the boiled suspension is taken from the boiling bath with its tube held **in a test-tube holder** and cooled to 30 °C in the assay bath before it is measured; it then stands the same 10 min at 30 °C and follows exactly the same mixing, soaking, draining, release and endpoint procedure as the five treatments, with the **120 s** observation limit. The label is the buffer's pH; nothing claims a measured pH of the peroxide tube.
- **Mixing before each disc:** each soaking mixture (the five treatments and the boiled control) is **mixed gently immediately before each disc goes in** (tube lifted from the rack and swirled in a small circle, then returned), with the same timing in every tube; tag *mixed just before each disc*. This is separate from swirling the gas-rig stock.
- **Discs:** identical filter-paper discs from one hole punch; each soaks for **60 s**, lifted with **forceps**, drained for 5 s held above its soaking tube (not touching). **Forceps are rinsed** in a beaker labelled *distilled water: rinse* and blotted **between treatments**, to avoid carrying enzyme or buffer from one condition to the next.
- **Peroxide tubes:** boiling tubes of the same hydrogen peroxide solution, filled to the **same marked line** (same concentration, volume and depth). **A fresh tube for every disc**; used tubes move to a *used* rack.
- **Release:** long forceps carry the disc down inside the tube to the bottom; the forceps open, the disc is released flat, the forceps are withdrawn; **timer starts at release**, the plan's fixed starting position for a rise time. The disc meets the peroxide during the descent, so the descent is drawn brief and identical (about 1 s) for every disc, the control included. Bubbles gather on the disc; it lifts and rises; **timer stops as the disc reaches the surface**. Times recorded in **whole seconds**.

- **`denatured` motion (first specified here; SHARED-SPECS §5):** two parts. The **heat trigger**, used only for temperature: a small thermometer glyph beside the model rises and the model jitters slightly faster. The **core motion**, used for temperature and for pH: the dashed and dotted links **vanish one by one** (about 0.25 s apart, cue-synced); the silhouette **loosens** (loops drift apart over about 0.8 s); the cleft **distorts**; the backbone line stays **continuous, never cut**, and is traced once with the tag **peptide bonds intact**. - **`ph-shifted` sub-state (published here, reversible):** the **+**/**−** tags on the R groups lining the cleft change (one **−** becomes neutral, one neutral stub gains **+**); the dotted ionic link across the cleft wall breaks; the cleft shape **shifts** over about 0.6 s; the silhouette stays tight and the other links stay. The pH states are **qualitative**: a small state tag beside the model reads *near this enzyme's optimum*, *away from the optimum* or *extreme pH*, with no number (no pH threshold is specified; the measured pH plot and its 7–9 bracket stay a separate panel). At *extreme pH* the model runs the `denatured` **core motion only** (link loss, unfolding, cleft distortion, backbone continuous, substrate fails to seat), triggered by **H⁺** tokens at the stubs, **without** the thermometer glyph or the faster thermal jitter.
- **Cold state (no new asset):** `rest-lk`, every link present, substrate tokens moving slowly; tag **not denatured**.
- Motion contract: all of this is non-covalent **MOTION**. No covalent bond is made or broken on the model in this lesson; the backbone line is continuous in every frame.
