## The causal spine

One idea carries the lesson: **the DNA is already copied before mitosis starts; mitosis is the orderly sharing-out of those copies, so that each new nucleus gets one copy of every chromosome, and the envelope, the spindle and the cell surface membrane each do a job that makes the sharing possible.**

> **Chromosomes replicated in the S phase of interphase condense and become visible in prophase, each as two sister chromatids joined at a centromere; the nucleolus disappears, the spindle forms from the poles and the nuclear envelope breaks down into fragments. In metaphase, spindle fibres attach at the centromeres and the chromosomes line up at the equator. In anaphase, the centromeres divide and the sister chromatids separate, each now a daughter chromosome, pulled to opposite poles as the attached fibres shorten. In telophase, the daughter chromosomes uncoil, a nuclear envelope re-forms around each group, a nucleolus reappears in each new nucleus and the spindle breaks down. Cytokinesis then divides the cytoplasm: in an animal cell the cell surface membrane is drawn in as a cleavage furrow; in a plant cell vesicles fuse at the equator into a cell plate that becomes the new cell walls. Plant cells typically have no centrioles and still form a spindle.**

- **animal**: round cell (diameter about 620 px) bounded by the **cell surface membrane** (single line); a central nucleus bounded by the **nuclear envelope**, a double line; a **nucleolus**, a darker grey disc inside the interphase nucleus; two **centrosomes**, each a small pale halo with a **pair of centrioles** (two short perpendicular cylinders), lying together just outside the envelope at upper left in `interphase`.
- **plant**: rectangular **cell wall** (about 660 × 440 px, a pale grey-olive band) with the **cell surface membrane** a single line just inside it; the same nucleus, envelope and nucleolus; **no centrioles**; the spindle poles are broad, unmarked regions at left and right.

Transitions (every one continuous MOTION; never a cut between stills):
- **Condensation** (`interphase` → `prophase-early`): each chromosome coils from `replicated-extended` to `replicated-condensed` over about 3 s (shortening and thickening), the two sister chromatids resolving side by side, joined at the centromere. No replication is drawn here; the chromatids are already two.
- **Nucleolus**: fades and shrinks to nothing over about 1.5 s during `prophase-early`; in `telophase`, a small disc grows back to full size inside **each** new nucleus. (Topic 1 recall only; no ribosome content.)
- **Centrosomes** (animal): move apart around the envelope to the left and right poles over about 2 s in `prophase-late`.
- **Spindle**: Spindle microtubules grow from the animal centrosomes or the plant's broad pole regions. While the nuclear envelope is intact, keep them outside it. After envelope breakdown, chromosome-attached fibres reach the centromere regions, with opposite-pole attachments to the two sister chromatids. Distinguish these from any fibres overlapping elsewhere in the spindle. (Drawn as thin grey lines.)
- **Envelope breakdown**: the double line breaks into about twelve short double-line fragments that drift outward and disperse into the cytoplasm (`prophase-late`); in `telophase`, fragments gather and join around each group of daughter chromosomes, closing into a complete double line (re-formation as motion).
- **Alignment** (`metaphase`): chromosomes are drawn to the equator one at a time until all four centromeres sit on it, each chromatid facing a different pole.
- **Separation** (`anaphase`): the `replicated → separated` switch happens in **one rendered frame** at the narration's words *the centromeres divide*; on that frame each separated unit is relabelled *daughter chromosome* and the whole-cell count strip updates to **8 daughter chromosomes · 8** (DNA-molecule count unchanged); the attached fibres then shorten and the daughter chromosomes MOVE to opposite poles, **centromere leading, arms trailing** (small type *in this schematic the arms trail behind the leading centromere; real chromosomes may look U- or V-shaped*).
- **Decondensation** (`telophase`): daughter chromosomes uncoil back towards the extended thread over about 3 s; the spindle fibres shorten back and fade (spindle breakdown as motion).
- **Cytokinesis, animal**: the cell surface membrane is drawn inwards around the equator as a **cleavage furrow** that deepens until the cell is pinched into two round daughter cells.
- **Cytokinesis, plant**: small pale **vesicles** travel to the equator and gather there; neighbouring vesicles fuse, forming a **cell plate** from the centre outwards until it meets the old wall; the plate thickens into the **new cell walls** with a cell surface membrane on each side.
