## The causal spine

One idea carries the lesson: **a telomere is a long run of repeated, non-coding DNA at each chromosome end, so when repeated rounds of replication leave the chromosome's end shorter, what is lost is telomere, not genes.**

> **In typical dividing somatic cells, chromosome ends can shorten over successive rounds of DNA replication, which happens in the S phase of interphase. Telomeres are long stretches of a short, repeated, non-coding DNA sequence at the ends of chromosomes, lying beyond the genes, so shortening initially removes telomeric sequence rather than genes near the ends. This protects genes during repeated replication. Some cells maintain their telomeres.**

Bounds, from the plan (MF3): the spine does not say that telomeres prevent all DNA loss or all DNA damage; nothing is said about what happens when a real telomere runs out; no telomerase mechanism, no ageing claim, no Hayflick limit, no end-replication mechanism (the general replication process is 6.1.4's).

**What the mark schemes credit, quoted.** No telomere marking point is held verbatim in this run's evidence. What the plan check verified in the PDFs (cited by demand, never shown as Cambridge's wording): **s21_22 Q1(b), QP p3 / MS p8** — a 4-mark cloze of which only part D, 1 mark, is Topic 5: D supplies repeated end sequences permitting replication without gene loss, and the answer is telomeres (A08; A–C are Topic 6 replication chemistry). **s21_12 Q19, key A** — telomere shortening after more divisions. **s22_12 Q18, key A** — telomere function/shortening, with maintained telomere length in stem and cancer cells supplied as context (weights: supplied context tests existing Topic 5 knowledge and does not make the supplied names required recall). The command-word guidance for *outline* is the Learner Guide's “Detail is not required.” [Learner Guide PDF p15, via EXAMINER-INSIGHT §4] **PDF-VERIFIED (plan check)**; it governs exam answers, not how fully we explain, and is not permission to omit the essential link. G05's own assessment, shown as **G05's summary** and never as Cambridge's wording: *G05: "this does not establish a complete mechanistic rubric for telomere function"*. So the spine is what the verified demands test: telomeres named as the repeated end sequences, shortening with more divisions, and the function of keeping genes during replication.

### `TelomereEndModel` (published here; SHARED-SPECS §5, identical)

- **Geometry.** One chromatid end at Z2-like scale: a plain two-strand helix strip (label *DNA*; no bases), two **gene bands** (white bands with a thin dark outline, each labelled *gene*), then the telomere drawn as **grey schematic blocks** running to the tip. The run carries the bracket label *telomere: repeated, non-coding DNA* and the optional label *repeat in humans: TTAGGG* **on the run, not on one block**. Small type on the run: *grey blocks: arbitrary schematic lengths, not individual TTAGGG repeats or a measured loss per division*. The tip is labelled **end of chromosome**. Caption, whenever shortening is shown: *schematic shortening; amount not to scale*.
- **Replication-round counter** (top right): counts rounds of replication (0, 1, 2, 3); it is not a chromosome count and has no compartment tag.
- **Motion contract (real state).** Each round the parent molecule is copied: the **daughter molecule is drawn growing along the template** from the gene end towards the tip, its gene bands appearing at the same positions as it passes (identical), and it **stops short** of the template's end (MOTION). The template then fades and the daughter slides into its place to be the template of the next round. The daughter's endpoint is marked with a thin tick (*end after round 1*, *2*, *3*); a dashed guide marks the **starting end**. Gene bands are never touched in the real state. **No block is ever detached or cut off an intact chromosome**; a shorter daughter is simply drawn shorter, and its endpoint need not fall on a block boundary. No bond is drawn making or breaking; no enzyme, primer or telomerase is drawn.
- **Thought-experiment state `no-telomere`.** Caption **thought experiment — not a real chromosome** is on screen for the whole time this state is shown, and the model's frame becomes a dashed outline in the house accent (not terracotta). The grey run fades away (motion), leaving the strip ending a short distance beyond gene 2; its own round counter runs; over successive rounds the shortening endpoint reaches the end gene band and the daughter's copy of that band ends at the endpoint (ringed). Then the model **returns to the real state** (the grey run and the Beat 5 ticks fade back in, motion), and the caption clears on the frame the real state is complete.
- **Colours.** DNA strip dark; gene bands white with dark outline; telomere grey; highlights and rings in the house accent. No terracotta.
