## The causal spine

One idea carries the lesson: **a telomere is a long run of repeated, non-coding DNA at each chromosome end, so when, in typical dividing somatic cells, repeated rounds of replication leave the chromosome's end shorter, what is initially lost is telomeric DNA, not nearby genes.**

> **In typical dividing somatic cells, chromosome ends can shorten over successive rounds of DNA replication, which happens in the S phase of interphase. Telomeres are long stretches of a short, repeated, non-coding DNA sequence at the ends of chromosomes, lying beyond the genes, so shortening initially removes telomeric sequence rather than genes near the ends. This protects genes during repeated replication. Some cells maintain their telomeres.**

Bounds, from the plan (MF3): the spine does not say that telomeres prevent all DNA loss or all DNA damage; no telomerase mechanism, no ageing claim, no Hayflick limit, no end-replication mechanism (general semi-conservative replication and its enzymes are 6.1.4's; the detailed problem of copying chromosome ends is outside this lesson's required mechanism). Round-1 check M2: teach the bounded examined conclusion that a cell may stop dividing if its telomeres become too short. Do not teach an ageing theory, a numerical division limit, telomerase biochemistry, or the detailed cellular response to an unprotected chromosome end. That conclusion is spoken once, in Beat 8, attached to S22/12 Q18; the model is never animated towards a stop.

- **Geometry.** One chromatid end at Z2-like scale: a plain two-strand helix strip (label *DNA*; no bases), two **gene bands** (white bands with a thin dark outline, each labelled *gene*), then the telomere drawn as **grey schematic blocks** running to the tip. The whole-run TTAGGG label and the block note are kept on every zoom and replay of the model that shows the grey run (round-1 check, should-fix 4); no highlight or animation may imply that one grey block is one six-base repeat.
- **Replication-round counter** (top right): counts rounds of replication (0, 1, 2, 3); it is not a chromosome count and has no compartment tag.
- **Motion contract (real state).** Each round the parent molecule is copied: the **daughter molecule is drawn growing along the template** from the gene end towards the tip, its gene bands appearing at the same positions as it passes (identical), and it **stops short** of the template's end (MOTION). The template then fades and the daughter slides into its place to be the template of the next round. The daughter's endpoint is marked with a thin tick (*end after round 1*, *2*, *3*); a dashed guide marks the **starting end**. Gene bands are never touched in the real state. **No block is ever detached or cut off an intact chromosome**; a shorter daughter is simply drawn shorter, and its endpoint need not fall on a block boundary. No bond is drawn making or breaking; no enzyme, primer or telomerase is drawn. Small type on the model whenever copying runs (round-1 check, should-fix 3): *This comparison shows endpoint lengths across successive replication rounds, not strand inheritance or a molecular replication mechanism.* Copying stays progressive and the endpoints shorter; the intact parent beside the growing second strip is not a diagram of conservative DNA replication.
