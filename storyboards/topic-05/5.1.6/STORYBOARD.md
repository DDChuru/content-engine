## The causal spine

One idea carries the lesson: **mitosis is not the fault; losing control of it is.** In a healthy adult tissue that is maintaining its size, genes control when cells divide, so the cells made by mitosis replace the cells lost. A mutation in a gene that controls cell division can remove that control in one cell; the mutated gene is copied with the rest of the DNA and inherited by its daughter cells, which also divide without control; repeated, uncontrolled mitosis builds a mass of abnormal cells, a tumour.

> **A mutation in a gene that controls cell division (one typical cause: ultraviolet radiation in sunlight, in skin cells) can cause loss of control of the cell cycle, so the cell divides repeatedly by mitosis without control. Its daughter cells inherit the mutation and also divide without control, producing a mass of abnormal cells: a tumour. Usually more than one such mutation has accumulated in the same cell line first. A benign tumour typically stays in one place and does not invade surrounding tissue; a malignant tumour (a cancer) invades surrounding tissue, and cells can break away, travel in the blood or lymph and form secondary tumours.**
