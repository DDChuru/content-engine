Bill of Health

Every site gets one Bill of Health per day. Seven things every shift must close before the day ends, three that carry over until someone resolves them. It is written once, in the moment, by the people doing the work — and it cannot be rewritten tomorrow. That is not a limitation. That is what makes it evidence.

Audience: site managers, shift supervisors, SHEQ and the regional champions who will lead on-site training. Daniel's voice. Landscape 1920×1080 with a phone-framed screen and zoom crops, Ecowize bookends. Target 4:40. Spoken numbers written as words for the voice engine.

DUE DILIGENCE
"Show me" beats "tell me."

FSSC 22000 · retailer audits · Regulation R638

The defence is that you took all reasonable steps — and the proof is the record made at the time.

A record that can be edited later is worth less than one that cannot.

10

Why an auditor cares

3:40 – 4:08
FULL-FRAME CARD · FOOD SAFETY DUE DILIGENCE
DanielHere is why that matters beyond the site. In food safety, when something goes wrong, the question is not whether you had a system — it is whether you exercised due diligence: all reasonable steps, taken at the time, and recorded at the time. An auditor from a retailer, or under FSSC, or the health department, does not ask you to tell them about last Tuesday. They ask you to show them. A record you could have edited afterwards proves very little. A record nobody could touch after the shift proves that the work happened when you said it did. The Bill of Health is that record.
Shot: second full-frame card, three audit contexts named on screen. Keep it factual: "due diligence" is the standard of care and the defence; do not name a specific section of law on screen or in voice.
