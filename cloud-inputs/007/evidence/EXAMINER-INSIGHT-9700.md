## 2. How students actually fail

Only text explicitly introduced as **verbatim examiner quotation** is quoted from commentary. Surrounding descriptions are **our paraphrase**. Short excerpts are intentional; the linked pages provide their complete context.

| Failure type | Verbatim examiner quotation | What the evidence means in its specific question — paraphrase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Imprecise wording | **“not specific”** — June 2024, P52 key messages. [R24 p.54][R24-54] | An unspecified amount does not establish whether mass or volume is controlled. A practical gate should request the quantity, unit and operation. |
| Wrong biological term | **“contract and relax”** — March 2023, P22 Q5(c), terms incorrectly applied to elastic tissue. [RM23 p.7][RM23-7] | Elastic tissue stretches and recoils; smooth muscle contracts and relaxes. Knowing both word pairs is insufficient if they are assigned to the wrong material. |
| Missing causal link | **“did not go on to explain”** — E2 Q3(c). [E2 p.27][E2-27] | Knowing that the smallpox virus did not mutate was insufficient without connecting this to continued use of one vaccine. A fact and its practical consequence are distinct parts of the answer. |
| Describing instead of explaining | **“descriptions of the graph rather than explanations”** — June 2024, P42 Q10(b). [R24 p.45][R24-45] | Reporting a glucose change cannot replace the glucagon–liver–glucose-restoration mechanism. The requested interval was 15–70 minutes. |
| Misreading the command word | **“Misreading the command word”** — same question. [R24 p.45][R24-45] | Identify both the required operation and its scope before writing. A scientifically correct answer to a different operation can still miss the available points. |
| Using the wrong biological level | **“This did not answer the question.”** — March 2023, P22 Q6(a). [RM23 p.7][RM23-7] | Candidates described collagen molecules where the question required collagen fibres. A correct smaller-scale account does not necessarily answer a larger-scale structure–function question. |

A particularly economical diagnostic example is the distinction between a term and an example: E2 Q1(C) required globular, while some candidates named haemoglobin. This is a category error, not simply insufficient detail. E2 also records cytokinin being read as cytokine, causing an irrelevant immune-response account. [E2 pp.9–10, 47][E2-9]

**Design inference:** store failure labels separately for wrong entity, wrong process, missing relationship, wrong evidence, wrong command and wrong scope. Do not turn every examiner criticism into a global banned-word rule or treat every irrelevant sentence as cancellation of an otherwise correct answer.

## 3. What a good answer looks like: actual contrasts

These are paraphrases of commentary, not newly invented model answers or transcriptions of student handwriting. Grade labels in E0 describe the selected historical examples, not current grade thresholds.

| Same question | Stronger response | Weaker response and concrete distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E2 P22 Q2(a), xerophyte image | Identifies a visible additional adaptation and connects hairs to humid air and a reduced gradient; **1/1 + 2/2**. [p.11][E2-11] | Repeats an adaptation already supplied, then offers a generic leaf explanation; **0/1 + 0/2**. [p.13][E2-13] **Check both selection and explanation.** |
| E0 P3 Q2(a), plan drawing | Grade-A example distinguishes a tissue plan from cells, but still loses credit for drawing the wrong extent. [PDF p.87, printed p.85][E0-87] | Grade-E example includes cells in the plan and confuses epidermis/endodermis. [PDF p.96, printed p.94][E0-96] **Scientific knowledge and compliance with drawing instructions need separate checks.** |
| E0 P4 Q7(c), auxin graph | Full marks include both description and explanation. [PDF p.125, printed p.123][E0-125] | Grade-C example obtains only one explanation mark and compares the wrong curves. **A mechanism cannot compensate for missing or incorrect data description.** |
| E0 P5 Q1(a)(ii), leaf investigation | Maximum-mark example supplies a method for each measured feature. [PDF p.149, printed p.147][E0-149] | Grade-E example changes the stated same-plant comparison into different plants/species and lists apparatus without a coherent method. [PDF p.153, printed p.151][E0-153] **The plan must preserve the question's experimental comparison.** |

**Suggested gate format, our inference:** present the same scientific information with different commands, or supply two short responses containing similar facts. Ask the learner to identify the missing relationship, repair it, and justify why the repair answers the actual question. This tests selection and application rather than only recognition of keywords.

## 4. Command words: current definitions and answer demands

The 2025–2027 syllabus explicitly makes command meanings subject-context dependent. The following definitions are **paraphrases** of its table, not Cambridge quotations. [Syllabus PDF p.50][SYL-50]

| Command | Cambridge's distinction — paraphrase | Proposed observable answer requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Describe** | Present characteristics, main features or relevant points. | For data, identify the relationship/trend and support it with appropriate values and units. For a structure, describe the requested structure at the requested level. Do not substitute a mechanism for the requested description. |
| **Explain** | Make reasons, purposes and relationships clear, including how/why and relevant evidence. | Link cause → biological process → consequence in context. For a changed condition, state what increases, decreases or stops. |
| **Suggest** | Apply knowledge to a situation that permits a range of valid proposals. | Use the supplied evidence and biology to make a defensible proposal. It is neither a demand for one memorised sentence nor permission for arbitrary guessing. |
| **Compare** | Identify or comment on similarities and/or differences. | Pair the same feature for both objects. Follow any explicit restriction: both similarities and differences, or differences only. |

The **and/or** in compare matters. June 2024 P22 Q3(b) required both, whereas Q4(d) required differences; a list about just one condition was insufficient. [R24 p.12][R24-12] E0 P4 Q7(c) demonstrates that a combined describe-and-explain instruction needs evidence of both operations. [E0 p.125][E0-125]

For **suggest**, the Learner Guide directs learners to apply relevant prior information; its example also restricts the number of suggestions requested. [LG p.15][LG-15] For **outline**, its **verbatim guidance** is **“Detail is not required.”** This is guidance for the illustrated outline task, not permission to omit essential steps from every experimental plan. [LG p.15][LG-15]

**Implementation inference:** each gate should specify command, biological subject, comparison partner, evidence source, requested interval, exclusions and number of responses. Test those dimensions explicitly before escalating content depth.

## 5. Depth signals and defensible ceilings

These are **question-local boundaries**, not universal word counts or a ceiling on the entire topic.

| Source | Evidence — paraphrase | Consequence for a proposed gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E2 Q2, p.11; Q4(a), p.31. [E2][E2-31] | Full marks were possible using hairs instead of the more precise trichomes; the closed-circulation answer earned its marks without the suggested fuller vessel list. | Keep examiner suggestions for improvement separate from minimum full-credit requirements. |
| LG Q3(c), p.19. [LG][LG-19] | A missing specification of timed/regular sampling lost one possible point, yet other points yielded **3/3**. | Do not silently turn every optional marking point into a compulsory condition. A timing-only diagnostic can still assess that missing skill separately. |
| June 2024 P23 Q5(a), p.21. [R24][R24-21] | Detailed intracellular events were not required at AS level in this signalling question. | Require the relevant signal/target/response sequence; do not automatically demand an A Level biochemical cascade. |
| March 2023 P52 Q1(b)(ii), p.14. [RM23][RM23-14] | The question expressly excluded glucose-assay and colorimeter-use details, but candidates still supplied them. | Encode explicit exclusions. More procedural detail can be irrelevant rather than evidence of greater mastery. |
| June 2023 P21 Q1(b)(ii), p.11. [R23][R23-11] | Generic enzyme-action accounts did not supply the requested protein-breakdown chemistry. | Stop at the mechanism the question actually asks about; extra familiar material cannot replace it. |
| E0 P5 Q2(b)(iii), PDF p.176 / printed p.174. [E0][E0-176] | An unnecessary explanation was followed by a contradiction that prevented credit for the initially correct trend. | Teach stopping after a sufficient, coherent answer. Extra content is not inherently penalised; contradiction is the problem here. |

**Do not infer a fixed sentence-per-mark algorithm.** Separate three things in our product: the original question's full-credit threshold, a narrower diagnostic requirement deliberately chosen by us, and optional enrichment. Older exemplars cannot establish current syllabus coverage or universal lexical acceptance.

### 6.1 A correction to existing source interpretation

**G01's first credit row misidentifies `s23_21 Q1(a)(i)` as an animal-cell task.** The archived [QP PDF p.2](pastpapers/2023/June/9700_s23_qp_21.pdf#page=2) explicitly presents a **plant stem cell** and asks for evidence distinguishing it from **leaf mesophyll**. The [June 2023 report, P21 Q1(a)(i), PDF p.11][R23-11] confirms that context. Correct the task description before reusing that gate; retain its valid general demand for observable evidence. This is a source-mapping correction, not a newly discovered student misconception. [Existing G01][G01]
