## 1. Cell Structure

**Confidence: Medium.** Ten leaves across two topics. The microscopy descriptions are unusually explicit about the operations taught; organelle-detail limits are less certain. Evidence anchors: AL 1.1.1–1.1.5 and 1.2.1–1.2.5.

**Difficulty and type of content.** Recall and structure–function explanation dominate the cell material. Microscopy adds practical technique, micrograph interpretation and calculation. The main demand appears to be distinguishing structures and choosing a measurement relationship while keeping units consistent, rather than advanced mathematics.

**Scope in one lesson.** Slide preparation and biological drawing share one leaf; magnification, graticule calibration, resolution and actual-size calculation have separate leaves. Organelles/functions form a broader leaf, while cell comparisons, ATP and viruses each have their own. Treat these as evidence that a measurement note need not become a survey of all cell biology.

**What is deliberately kept simple — inferred limit.** Keep microscopy at specimen observation, image interpretation and the named measurement operations. Keep ATP here at cellular work, breakdown and regeneration, without importing the later respiration section.

**Where they stop.** The described measurement endpoints are image/actual size, magnification and calibrated graticule divisions. The exact organelle list, electron-microscope detail and example complexity are unknown; a closer look would be needed to specify them.

**Flags for gating.** **Calculation:** rearrangement, microscopy units, scale bars and calibration. **Practical/data:** temporary slides, stains, coverslips, drawings and micrographs. These are distinct demands, not one undifferentiated recall task.

**DO-NOT-ADD:**

- Do not add optical-equation derivations, electron-optics engineering, exhaustive organelle biochemistry or viral replication pathways.
- Do not combine every measurement method with every cell type simply to make a lesson comprehensive.

## 2. Biological Molecules

**Confidence: Medium.** Nineteen leaves across four topics. Named molecular features and test purposes are well described; chemical and numerical depth is not. Anchors: AL 2.1.1–2.1.3, 2.2.5–2.2.8 and 2.3.1–2.4.2.

**Difficulty and type of content.** Recall and molecular structure–function explanation dominate, with a distinct practical testing strand. This is already molecular content: glucose forms, bonds, protein organisation and water polarity are supported. “Simple” must not mean removing those explanations. The difficulty is connecting a limited structural feature to a biological consequence.

**Scope in one lesson.** The map separates a food-test overview, a Benedict's concentration investigation and a non-reducing-sugar test. It also separates individual storage/structural molecules, protein structure, protein examples and water properties. One molecule or one test purpose is the likely bounded unit; nineteen leaves are not a licence to teach the entire chemistry of biomolecules at once.

**What is deliberately kept simple — inferred limit.** Explain condensation/hydrolysis and the named bonds through their biological roles. Keep concentration work tied to the described comparison standards, colour measurements and dilutions.

**Where they stop.** Descriptions reach protein side-chain interactions, haemoglobin subunits and collagen organisation. Exact structural formula requirements, named sugar examples and dilution arithmetic are unknown. Do not infer them from general chemistry knowledge.

**Flags for gating.** **Practical:** Benedict's, iodine, emulsion, biuret and hydrolysis/neutralisation. **Data/potential calculation:** concentration comparison and dilution; the description does not establish the number or complexity of numerical steps.

**DO-NOT-ADD:**

- Do not add reagent reaction mechanisms, extensive stereochemistry, amino-acid catalogues, protein-folding thermodynamics or extra named biomolecules.
- Do not inflate haemoglobin structure into the later gas-transport lesson.

## 3. Enzymes

**What is deliberately kept simple — inferred limit.** Use the described substrate–rate graphs to locate Vmax and interpret Km as an affinity measure. The description supports graph use, not a mathematical derivation of enzyme kinetics. Colorimetry concerns calibration and following a colour change, not instrument theory.

**Where they stop.** The map explicitly reaches competitive/non-competitive inhibition, end-product control and alginate immobilisation. Exact rate calculations, numerical graph demands, assay controls and experimental timings remain unknown. Do not invent a worked-example standard.

**Flags for gating.** **Practical:** catalase oxygen production, amylase starch disappearance, colorimetry and free/immobilised comparison. **Data:** rate curves, saturation and Vmax/Km. Numerical extraction may require gating, but a full equation-solving exercise is not established by the description.

**DO-NOT-ADD:**

- Do not add Michaelis–Menten derivations, reciprocal-plot methods, catalytic residue chemistry, extra inhibition classes or industrial case-study catalogues.
