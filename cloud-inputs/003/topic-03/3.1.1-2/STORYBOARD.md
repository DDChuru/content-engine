## The causal spine

Two facts carry the lesson, both in the syllabus's own words: an enzyme is a **globular protein that catalyses reactions** inside or outside the cell, and it works through an **active site**, an **enzyme–substrate complex**, **lowering of activation energy** and **specificity**, under two named hypotheses.

> **An enzyme is a globular protein and a biological catalyst. A small region of its tertiary structure, the active site, is complementary in shape to its substrate, so the enzyme is specific. The substrate binds by temporary bonds to form an enzyme–substrate complex; inside the complex the reaction happens with a lower activation energy, so it is faster; the products leave and the enzyme is unchanged and used again. In the lock-and-key hypothesis the active site is complementary before binding; in the induced-fit hypothesis the active site is not fully complementary at rest, changes shape to fit the substrate as it binds, and returns to its original shape after the products leave.**

**What the mark schemes credit, quoted:** [s24_23 Q4(b), MS p.9] "laccase uses induced fit ;", "idea that active site shape not fully complementary to (shape of) monolignols ;", "active site changes shape to, fit / bind to, monolignols ; AW", "becomes fully complementary ;", "forming enzyme-substrate complex ;", "lowers, activation energy / energy of activation;", "laccase returns to original shape, after product leaves active site / for re-use / AW ;", AVP "suggestion that monolignols are held in place by (temporary) H bonds"; "max 4 if no reference to laccase / monolignols". [s24_22 Q3(a), MS p.13] "active site with a specific shape as part of a lysozyme molecule drawn and active site labelled or included in an annotation ;", "complementary shaped peptidoglycan drawn and, peptidoglycan / substrate, labelled or included in an annotation;", "enzyme-substrate complex drawn and labelled ;", "lysozyme with same shaped active site and, two products / NAM and NAG drawn (formed from hydrolysed peptidoglycan) ;", "max 2 if no ref. to specific example". [w20_21 Q2(b)(i), MS p.7] "macromolecule and extracellular enzyme ;". [s21_12 Q13, key B] the arrow between the two peaks is the reduction in activation energy. [s21_12 Q12, key A] the suggestion that *cannot* be correct is the one saying the molecule "is not the same shape as the active site". So the spine is exactly what is credited: the site, the complementary substrate, the complex written in full, the lowered barrier, the unchanged enzyme, and two hypotheses with two names.

**The handle:** *lock and key* for specificity (the subject's own analogy). Converted at once: *the active site is complementary in shape to its substrate, so the enzyme is specific: it acts on a particular substrate or a limited group of related substrates.* The handle is never the exam answer; the drawing is labelled with the biological names, not "lock" and "key" (the s24_22 scheme caps an unlabelled lock-and-key drawing at 1).

**Typicality rules applied:** "enzymes are globular proteins" is said as the syllabus says it; no ribozyme detour and no count of enzymes. Catalase, amylase and lipase are the examples because the topic's own investigations use them; "some enzymes … others" rather than a universal split. Activation energy is a barrier on our own energy-against-reaction-progress graph (S21/12's figure labels its axis "time"; ours is not claimed as a reproduction). Lock-and-key is "the simpler model", not "wrong": the June 2024 lysozyme question credited lock-and-key diagrams. E35's ruling is stated as local to the 2016 branching-enzyme question and explicitly bounded by S24/23's credit for "lowers activation energy". Secretion is named; exocytosis is Topic 1/4 and is not taught. No catalytic-residue chemistry, no transition-state theory, no cofactor lesson.

## The models, specified once

**`EnzymeActiveSiteModel` (MODEL tag on every frame; published here, reused L2–L6).** A globular enzyme silhouette in base ink, a soft rounded blob with one addressable **active-site cleft** on its upper right. Caption on first appearance and whenever the model is the main subject: *schematic; not a real protein shape*. Labels are SVG text nodes: **enzyme**, **active site**, **substrate**, **enzyme–substrate complex**, **products**.

States, each addressable by id:
- **`rest-lk`** (lock-and-key resting state): the cleft is drawn already complementary to the substrate outline, a fixed notch. Used in Beats 7, 8, 9, 12, 16, 17, 18 and as the default state.
- **`rest-if`** (induced-fit resting state): a **separate drawing**, the cleft slightly wider and shallower than the substrate outline, visibly *not fully complementary*. Used only in Beat 13 and the Beat 17 recap. Never produced by squeezing `rest-lk`; the two resting states are two assets.
- **`bound`**: substrate seated in the cleft; a soft bracket around the pair labelled **enzyme–substrate complex**; three short dashed ticks between substrate and cleft wall labelled *temporary bonds (e.g. hydrogen bonds)* in the 2.3.3 dashed convention. In the induced-fit sequence `bound` is reached by the cleft **closing around** the substrate over ~0.6 s (motion), then returning to `rest-if` after release; in the lock-and-key sequence the cleft does not move.
- **`products`**: the substrate outline replaced by two smaller outlines leaving the cleft; the enzyme silhouette unchanged.
- **`inhibitor-competitive`**, **`inhibitor-noncompetitive`**, a **second binding site** on the lower left, and two inhibitor shapes: **published here, not shown in L1**; specified and first used in L5.
- **`denatured`** (cleft distorted, silhouette loosened, backbone intact): **published here, not shown in L1**; first used in L4a.
- **Substrate outlines:** a generic substrate (a rounded wedge with one notch, complementary to `rest-lk`); two **non-fitting** outlines (a wedge with the notch on the wrong side; a rounder blob) for Beat 7; the lysozyme-question set for Beats 16 and 18 (labelled *peptidoglycan*, products *NAM* and *NAG*, drawn as schematic pieces, no molecular structure).

**Motion contract (non-covalent events, Beats 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 16, 18 and every replay):** approach, seating, failure to seat, the induced-fit shape change, and product release are **motions**: the substrate travels into the cleft over ~0.8 s; a non-fitting outline reaches the cleft, rocks once and slides away; the bound state holds; products drift out. No bond lines are drawn for these events except the dashed temporary-bond ticks, which appear only in `bound`.

**`EnergyProfileGraph` (Beat 10, recalled by label in L4a).** Axes: **energy** up, **progress of reaction** along (our axis; caption *our axis; S21/12's figure labels its axis "time"*). A horizontal reactant level at left labelled **substrate**; a horizontal product level at right labelled **product**, drawn a little lower in this example (small type: *in this example the product level is lower; the enzyme does not change either level*). Two curves from the same reactant level to the same product level: **without enzyme**, the higher peak; **with enzyme**, the lower peak. Two vertical brackets from the reactant level: **activation energy without enzyme** and **activation energy with enzyme**. A third bracket between the two peaks labelled **reduction in activation energy**, drawn in the accent. No transition-state label, no molecular drawing on the graph.

**Atom-resolved reaction (Beat 9 only; `Hydrolyse` replay from 2.2.6, as specified under §3.1.3 of the plan).** Substrate: the checked four-glucose α-1,4 segment (C₂₄H₄₂O₂₁) from 2.2.5/2.2.6, drawn in an inset labelled *atom-resolved substrate (MODEL)* while the schematic model holds the `bound` state. At the middle bridge: **Og** is the bridge oxygen, **Cp** the partner glucose C4, **Ow** the water oxygen, **Ht** the water hydrogen transferred to Og, **Hr** water's retained hydrogen.

| Operation | Edges |
|---|---|
| Delete | Og–Cp; Ow–Ht |
| Create | Og–Ht; Cp–Ow |
| Retain | C1–Og; Ow–Hr; every other atom and spectator bond |

Bond-order sums before → after: glucose C1 4→4; Cp 4→4; Og 2→2 (C1 + Cp → C1 + Ht); Ow 2→2 (Ht + Hr → Cp + Hr); Ht 1→1; Hr 1→1. Whole-scene balance C₂₄H₄₂O₂₁ + H₂O → 2 C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ (C24/H44/O22 both sides). Only one O–H edge is newly created; the second product hydroxyl retains Ow–Hr. **Net-transformation contract:** the water model and the segment are fully bonded while the water approaches; the complete bond graph switches in **one rendered frame**; no crossfade or overlapping old/new bond sets; no free H or OH species; the caption **net reaction; not a stepwise mechanism** is visible from before the water approaches until the end of the product hold; valence audit (C 4, O 2, H 1) on every rendered state. The schematic `products` state on the main model switches in the same frame as the inset's graph switch. Nothing else in this lesson makes or breaks a covalent bond.

### BEAT 10 · Why it is faster: activation energy · 6:40–7:40
**Narration:**
> Why is it faster? Here is an energy profile for this reaction: energy up the side, the progress of the reaction along the bottom. The substrate starts here; the product ends here, in this example a little lower; and in between there is a hill. To react, the substrate has to get over that hill, and its height is the activation energy, the energy that has to be put in before the reaction can go. The enzyme lowers the activation energy: same start, same finish, a lower hill. The gap between the two peaks is the reduction, not either hill on its own. A lower hill means many more molecules get over it in a given time, so the rate goes up.
