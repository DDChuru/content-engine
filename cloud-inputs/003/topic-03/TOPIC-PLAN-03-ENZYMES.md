# Topic 3 — Enzymes: teaching plan

Prepared 21 September 2026; rework 1 after `TOPIC-03-PLAN-CHECK.md`, 21 September 2026. **Planning only: 8 syllabus outcomes, proposed 61:30, 96 macro beats including 11 evidenced error beats, delivered as 7 lessons.** The [evidence weights and full QP/MS ledger](TOPIC-03-WEIGHTS.md) are the audit trail for incidence, overlapping marks, selected errors and time. This plan follows the Topic 2 documents' method and tone, with the current [VIDEO-STRUCTURE.md](VIDEO-STRUCTURE.md) requirements (five-move error beat at ~45 s, "say the typical thing as typical", the multi-fault marker rule, and the 21 September section "When covalent bonds change: every frame must be valid chemistry") taking precedence over older conventions.

## Scope, evidence and authoring rules

The syllabus sets **scope**; we write the explanation; mark schemes **validate** ([CONTENT-ARCHITECTURE.md](CONTENT-ARCHITECTURE.md)). The [topic introduction, p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20): "Enzymes are essential for life to exist. The mode of action of enzymes and the factors that affect their activity are explored in this topic. Prior knowledge for this topic is an understanding that an enzyme is a biological catalyst that increases the rate of a reaction and remains unchanged when the reaction is complete. There are many opportunities in this topic for candidates to gain experience of carrying out practical investigations and analysing, interpreting and evaluating their results." Read alongside [the depth index and verbatim outcomes](SYLLABUS-9700-DETAIL.md), [examiner insight](EXAMINER-INSIGHT-9700.md), [G03 criteria](GATE-CRITERIA-9700-03-ENZYMES.md) and [calibration §3](COMPLEXITY-CALIBRATION-9700-BIOLOGY.md).

There are **8 outcomes**: four on mode of action and measurement, four on factors. **3.1.2 is the only outcome with an explicit depth qualifier ("including the lock-and-key hypothesis and the induced-fit hypothesis"): a floor, not a ceiling.** No Topic 3 outcome uses "limited to" or "not expected"; every other boundary below is our editorial allocation from the command word and neighbouring outcomes, not a fabricated syllabus prohibition. Calibration §3's DO-NOT-ADD list (no algebraic Michaelis–Menten derivation, no reciprocal plot, no catalytic-residue chemistry, no extra inhibition classes, no industrial catalogue) is adopted.

MS excerpts are exact wording, whitespace and subscript typography normalised; A/R/I are local to their question, and "I" means the response earns nothing at that point, not that it is false. A one-letter MCQ key validates the selected option only. Where no direct prose rubric or examiner diagnosis was located, that is said. Evidence gaps do not remove syllabus content.

**Timing:** 120 words per minute of final video, the effective rate; prescribed reads and holds are not added again. Each outcome's allocation includes **45 seconds of spoken talk-through per evidenced error**. The 11 selections are owned once in the weights register; a recalled error in a later lesson is not a second COMMON MISTAKE beat. All five moves apply; when a written answer has several faults the marker clears only on the completed correct frame.

**Chemistry on screen (the 21 September VIDEO-STRUCTURE rule, applied per lesson below).** Substrate approaching and entering an active site, an active site changing shape around a substrate (induced fit), an inhibitor seating in or beside the active site, a substrate failing to seat, and a protein unfolding at high temperature or extreme pH are **non-covalent events and are animated as MOTION**. Any bond made or broken at the active site is a **net transformation**: two complete keyframes, the complete bond graph switching in one rendered frame, no crossfade, the caption "net reaction; not a stepwise mechanism" visible throughout and on every replay, the storyboard listing bonds deleted and created by atom name (water counts), and a valence audit on every rendered state. The two atom-resolved reactions in this topic are specified once, under 3.1.3, and replayed elsewhere by reference: **amylase** reuses the checked `Hydrolyse` spec from `topic-02/2.2.6/STORYBOARD.md` by name; **catalase** has its own edge list. Enzyme lessons do not draw catalytic-residue chemistry or transition states; the energy-profile diagram is a graph, not a molecular drawing.

**Rate vocabulary, held constant across the topic.** *Initial rate* is the gradient of the tangent at t = 0 on a progress curve, or of a genuinely straight initial section. *Average rate* is change divided by a whole time interval (S23/34's 5.4 g min⁻¹ is one). *1/t* for a fixed endpoint under comparable conditions is a relative rate proxy with units s⁻¹ when t is in seconds; it is not a concentration-per-time measurement and not automatically an initial rate. Substrate disappearance is reported as the positive magnitude of a negative gradient. Each planned experiment below states which of these it yields.

**Visual integrity:** enzyme, substrate, active site and inhibitor are drawn **MODELS**, labelled so ("schematic; not a real protein shape"). Rate graphs are drawn with labelled axes and units from the cited question; points made for teaching are labelled schematic, not measured data. Practical demonstrations use labelled apparatus and schematic result swatches; real footage remains a production dependency, never a fabricated photograph. Cambridge artwork is never reproduced; question text is quoted verbatim. Safety and handling are specific to the chosen reagents (hydrogen peroxide irritant; buffers; iodine), as Topic 2 did, not a generic practical-skills chapter.

## Outcomes in syllabus order

### 3.1.1

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> state that enzymes are globular proteins that catalyse reactions inside cells (intracellular enzymes) or are secreted to catalyse reactions outside cells (extracellular enzymes)

**Commands / content type:** state. **SINGLE STATEMENT / CLASSIFICATION**. Budget **1:45**, 4 beats, **1 error (E32)**. Delivered as the opening chapter of L1.

**What we teach:** Enzymes are globular proteins (recall 2.3.4's *generally soluble, physiological roles* by label). Two classes by where they act: intracellular (catalyse reactions inside cells, e.g. catalase) and extracellular (secreted, catalyse reactions outside cells, e.g. amylase in saliva, lipase in the intestine), using the enzymes the topic's own investigations use. Say "enzymes are globular proteins" as the syllabus does; no ribozyme detour. Secretion is named, not expanded into an exocytosis mechanism.

**Exact mark-scheme keys:** [W20/21 Q2(b)(i), MS p7](pastpapers/2020/November/9700_w20_ms_21.pdf#page=7): "macromolecule and extracellular enzyme ;" for lipases "released by exocytosis from intestinal epithelial cells". [ECR Paper 2 Q1 D, p7](cambridge-official/9700-ecr-paper2.pdf#page=7): the required term for enzymes that function outside cells is "extracellular".

**Scope ceiling and boundaries:** the statement only; exocytosis is Topic 1/4; 3.1.2 owns mechanism.

**Visual needs:** a cell outline with one enzyme model inside and one outside, labelled; no organelle detail.

### 3.1.2

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> explain the mode of action of enzymes in terms of an active site, enzyme–substrate complex, lowering of activation energy and enzyme specificity, including the lock-and-key hypothesis and the induced-fit hypothesis

**Commands / content type:** explain. **MECHANISM**. Budget **10:45**, 16 beats, **3 errors (E33–E35)**.

**What we teach:** The active site is a small region of the enzyme's tertiary structure whose shape (from R-group interactions, recalled from 2.3.3) is complementary to its substrate; that is specificity ("complementary", not "the same shape"). Substrate binds by temporary non-covalent bonds to form an enzyme–substrate complex; the reaction happens; products leave; the enzyme is unchanged and re-used. **Activation energy is the energy barrier from reactants to the peak of the energy profile.** The enzyme lowers it: show the catalysed and uncatalysed barriers from the same reactant level; their difference is the reduction in activation energy, not either activation energy itself; reactant and product levels are unchanged (the S21/12 Q13 form; the paper's own figure labels its horizontal axis "time", ours is labelled reaction progress and is not claimed as a reproduction). Two hypotheses, kept as two names and two drawn states: lock-and-key (active site complementary before binding) and induced fit (active site **not fully complementary** at rest; changes shape on binding, becoming fully complementary; returns to its original shape after the product leaves). Induced fit is the currently accepted refinement; lock-and-key is not called "wrong": the June 2024 lysozyme question asked for lock-and-key diagrams and credited them. "Changes shape to fit/bind" is credited wording (S24/23 Q4(b)); the substrate does not acquire an active site, and an enzyme does not reshape to fit every molecule.

**Exact mark-scheme keys:** [S24/23 Q4(b), MS p9](pastpapers/2024/June/9700_s24_ms_23.pdf#page=9): "laccase uses induced fit ;", "idea that active site shape not fully complementary to (shape of) monolignols ;", "active site changes shape to, fit / bind to, monolignols ;", "becomes fully complementary ;", "forming enzyme-substrate complex ;", "lowers, activation energy / energy of activation;", "suggestion of how activation energy is lowered ;", "laccase returns to original shape, after product leaves active site / for re-use / AW ;", AVP "monolignols are held in place by (temporary) H bonds"; "max 4 if no reference to laccase / monolignols". [S24/22 Q3(a), MS p13](pastpapers/2024/June/9700_s24_ms_22.pdf#page=13): "active site with a specific shape as part of a lysozyme molecule drawn and active site labelled … ; complementary shaped peptidoglycan drawn and, peptidoglycan / substrate, labelled … ; enzyme-substrate complex drawn and labelled ; lysozyme with same shaped active site and, two products / NAM and NAG drawn … ; AVP ; e.g. water shown". Keys: S21/12 Q12 A (the suggestion that **cannot** be correct), Q13 B, S23/12 Q13 A, S24/12 Q8 A; S21/22 Q5(d)(i) active-site alternatives (MS p18).

**Scope ceiling and boundaries:** no catalytic-residue chemistry, no transition-state theory, no cofactor lesson (laccase's copper AVP is a suggestion mark). Denaturation is taught under 3.2.1. The active-site model is the shared asset for the topic.

**Chemistry on screen:** binding, induced fit and product release are motion, using labelled schematic shapes. If the default reaction is atom-resolved, reuse the complete four-glucose α-1,4 segment plus water and its checked internal-hydrolysis products from 2.2.6, with the full named-edge exchange specified for L2. Do not invent an isolated bond-deletion reaction for the generic silhouette.

**Visual needs:** `EnzymeActiveSiteModel`; `EnergyProfileGraph`.

### 3.1.3

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> investigate the progress of enzyme-catalysed reactions by measuring rates of formation of products using catalase and rates of disappearance of substrate using amylase

**Commands / content type:** investigate. **PRACTICAL / DATA**. Budget **10:15**, 15 beats, **1 error (E42)**.

**What we teach.** Two named investigations, each with a definite measurement.

*Catalase, product formation:* hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen. Chosen design: use measured aliquots of one well-mixed catalase preparation and hydrogen peroxide at fixed concentrations and volumes, buffered pH and maintained temperature. Keep the reactants separate inside the gas-tight vessel already connected to the gas syringe; tip to mix and start timing at mixing, without opening the system. Record the volume of gas collected at stated regular times. This is a time-series adaptation of S23/51 Q1(d), whose original reading was gas collected after one minute. Label the measured y-axis “volume of gas collected / cm³”; oxygen is the product of the catalase reaction, but collection alone does not establish that a yeast preparation's collected gas is pure oxygen. Include a matched enzyme-free background control. Estimate the initial gas-collection rate from the tangent at t = 0, or a genuinely straight initial section, with rise/run and units cm³ s⁻¹. Use the same collection-start specification in L4a and the L4b peroxide-dilution experiment. The disc-rise method (S22/33, S23/51) is taught as a **transfer example**: time for a catalase-soaked disc to rise is a fixed endpoint, so **1/t is a relative rate proxy**, not a progress curve.

*Amylase, substrate disappearance:* starch → maltose. **Chosen design:** amylase and starch mixed at a maintained temperature and buffered pH; take equal samples at stated regular times into equal portions of iodine solution. In the usual iodine starch test, disappearance of the blue-black result leaves the iodine's yellow-brown colour; it does not require a literally colourless sample (the syllabus's ordinal-data example on [p61](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=61) says "colourless" and is quoted as such where used, not made the universal observation). The timed iodine samples show the progression of starch disappearance. Record the first sample that no longer gives blue-black with iodine; use the same sampling interval and endpoint criterion throughout. This gives an endpoint time and a 1/t relative-rate proxy, not a quantitative starch-concentration curve. A falling substrate-concentration curve may be shown separately as a labelled schematic to explain a negative gradient, but numerical substrate concentrations or initial rates are not inferred from the by-eye swatches.

*Reading rates:* a product–time graph rises and a substrate–time graph falls; a rate–time graph falls from its maximum at the start (S23/12 Q14). Change divided by a whole time interval gives an **average rate** (S23/34: 54 g in 10 min = 5.4 g min⁻¹). Variables: independent, dependent (the measured quantity in a stated time, e.g. volume of gas collected after one minute), standardised; a control with boiled enzyme or enzyme replaced by water. The specimen Paper 2 rubric for "outline how the progress is investigated": controlled variables, samples at timed intervals, determine substrate or product concentration, plot against time, rate of disappearance or appearance, determine initial rate.

**Exact mark-scheme keys:** [S22/33 Q1(a), MS p6](pastpapers/2022/June/9700_s22_ms_33.pdf#page=6): "oxygen is released (causing the discs to rise) ;", table headings pH then time/seconds, "records three times for each pH ;", "records a mean time for each pH ;", "results recorded as whole seconds ;", control "use boiled enzyme ;", "contaminated with enzyme" / "use fresh hydrogen peroxide for each test". [S21/33 Q1(b)(iii), MS p5](pastpapers/2021/June/9700_s21_ms_33.pdf#page=5). [Specimen P2 Q3(c), MS p13](cambridge-official/9700-specimen-2022-ms2.pdf#page=13): "take samples at timed intervals ; A regular intervals" … "determine initial rate ;". [S23/12 Q14, key C](pastpapers/2023/June/9700_s23_ms_12.pdf#page=2). [S23/34 Q1(c)(i–ii), QP p8, ER p33](cambridge-official/9700-examiner-report-june2023.pdf#page=33): 54 g and 5.4 g min⁻¹; **the printed MS (p7) figures "5.4" and "5.8" are erroneous** and are never shown.

**Scope ceiling and boundaries:** "investigate" means show the two designs and how to read them; hands-on time is the learner's own ([p51](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=51)). Factor effects belong to 3.2.1; the colorimeter to 3.1.4; Km/Vmax to 3.2.2.

**Chemistry on screen (both specified here; replayed elsewhere by reference):**

- **amylase:** recall the checked 2.2.6 internal α-1,4 hydrolysis graph (`Hydrolyse`). Og is bridge oxygen, Cp the partner glucose C4, Ow water oxygen, Ht the transferred water hydrogen and Hr its retained hydrogen. Delete Og–Cp and Ow–Ht; create Og–Ht and Cp–Ow. Retain C1–Og, Ow–Hr and every spectator bond. Switch the complete graph in one rendered frame, with no crossfade or overlapping old/new bond sets; show "net reaction; not a stepwise mechanism" throughout. Bond-order sums before → after: Cp 4→4, glucose C1 4→4, Og 2→2, Ow 2→2, Ht 1→1, Hr 1→1. Both product hydroxyls are complete even though only one new O–H edge is made.
- **catalase:** 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂ as a two-keyframe net transformation with atom mapping. Name the atoms of the two peroxide molecules O1–O2 (H1 on O1, H2 on O2) and O3–O4 (H3 on O3, H4 on O4). **Delete:** O1–O2 (single), O3–O4 (single), O2–H2, O3–H3. **Create:** O1–H2, O4–H3, and the O2=O3 double bond. **Retain:** O1–H1, O4–H4. Valence audit both keyframes: every O has bond order 2 (before: O1 = O2 + H1; O2 = O1 + H2; O3 = O4 + H3; O4 = O3 + H4. After: O1 = H1 + H2; O4 = H3 + H4; O2 = O3 double; O3 = O2 double), every H 1. Products: two H₂O (O1, O4) and one O₂ (O2=O3). One-frame switch, no crossfade, caption throughout; no radical intermediates are drawn or narrated. Bubbles collecting, discs rising and iodine fading are motion.

### 3.1.4

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> outline the use of a colorimeter for measuring the progress of enzyme-catalysed reactions that involve colour changes

**Commands / content type:** outline. **PROCEDURE**. Budget **3:15**, 5 beats, **0 errors**.

**What we teach:** When the substrate or product is coloured, a colorimeter measures the absorbance (or transmission) of one wavelength through the sample, giving a quantitative, objective reading instead of a by-eye judgement. Procedure: select a suitable fixed filter/wavelength that the coloured species absorbs, then zero the colorimeter with the appropriate reaction blank. Keep the filter and optical path fixed. Read absorbance at stated times and plot absorbance against time; its initial gradient measures change in absorbance per unit time. Where a concentration rate is required, use a calibration curve made with known concentrations under the same assay conditions. Re-zero if the filter or relevant blank conditions change. Demonstrate one supplied coloured-substrate or coloured-product assay (the S21/51 ONPG → yellow ONP assay is the chosen demonstration); its chemical names are context, not an additional recall list. "Outline" caps it: no instrument optics, no Beer–Lambert law. Not every enzyme reaction has a colour change; a colorimeter is not appropriate for every colour observation (June 2023 ER p58).

**Exact mark-scheme keys / limits:** [S21/51 Q1(b)(i), MS p7](pastpapers/2021/June/9700_s21_ms_51.pdf#page=7): "idea that (result / it, is) quantitative / AW". [March 2023 ER, Paper 52 Q1(a)(i), p14](cambridge-official/9700-examiner-report-march2023.pdf#page=14): calibrated, zeroed with distilled water, coloured filter, "the filter used should not be the same as the colour of the solution being tested". [June 2024 ER, Paper 22 Q4(b)(ii), p15](cambridge-official/9700-examiner-report-june2024.pdf#page=15): accuracy versus subjective judgement; quantitative results; calibration curves; "intensity" not "different colours"; absorbance at one wavelength, not different wavelengths. No enzyme-sense mark in the fixed sample.

**Scope ceiling and boundaries:** outline only; reuses 2.1.2's standard-curve idea by label; distinguish an absorbance rate from a concentration rate.

**Visual needs:** `ColorimeterModel`; reuses `RateGraph` (absorbance–time; calibration curve).

**Recorded examiner evidence:** colorimeter misconceptions are reported in June 2024 /22 Q4(b)(ii), ER p15, and are addressed in normal teaching; no separate error beat is allocated to them in this plan.

### 3.2.1

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> investigate and explain the effects of the following factors on the rate of enzyme-catalysed reactions:
>
> - temperature
> - pH (using buffer solutions)
> - enzyme concentration
> - substrate concentration
> - inhibitor concentration

**Commands / content type:** investigate and explain. **CAUSAL / DATA / PRACTICAL**. Budget **15:45**, 24 beats, **3 errors (E38–E40)**. **Delivered as two linked lessons: L4a (temperature and pH, 7:00, 11 teaching beats) and L4b (enzyme, substrate and inhibitor concentration, 8:45, 10 teaching beats and E38–E40).** L4a ends on its own recap and real question; L4b opens on a labelled recall of L4a's grammar and model.

**What we teach:** One explanation grammar, applied five times on the same enzyme model and the same graph component: rate depends on how often substrate meets a working active site and forms an enzyme–substrate complex **per unit time**, and on whether the active site remains functional.

*Temperature (L4a):* below the optimum, warming increases kinetic energy and the rate of effective collisions, so more complexes form per unit time. Above the optimum, increasing denaturation reduces functional active sites: hydrogen and ionic bonds between R groups (2.3.3 recall) are disrupted, tertiary structure and active-site shape change, substrate is no longer complementary, fewer complexes form. Particles do not lose kinetic energy because the rate falls; low activity in the cold is not itself evidence of denaturation. In the standard model, disrupt appropriate non-covalent interactions without cutting the peptide backbone.

*pH, using buffer solutions (L4a):* an optimum at which the active site is most complementary; either side, charges on R groups change, ionic and hydrogen bonds are altered, active-site shape changes, fewer complexes; extreme pH can denature. A modest pH change need not mean irreversible unfolding. Optima vary by enzyme (protease ~6.1 in S24/32; lipase pH 8 in W20/21).

*Enzyme concentration (L4b):* more active sites available, more successful collisions, more complexes per unit time; rate proportional to enzyme concentration while substrate is in excess (a straight line through the origin, Learner Guide p28).

*Substrate concentration (L4b):* rate rises as collisions increase while active sites are available, then levels off as active sites become saturated; substrate concentration is limiting at first, enzyme concentration at the plateau; complexes still form and break at the maximum rate. The graph's x-axis is concentration, not time.

*Inhibitor concentration (L4b):* more inhibitor, fewer available or fewer working active sites, lower rate; mechanism detail belongs to 3.2.3.

### 3.2.2

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> explain that the maximum rate of reaction (Vmax) is used to derive the Michaelis–Menten constant (Km), which is used to compare the affinity of different enzymes for their substrates

**Commands / content type:** explain. **DATA / DEFINITION**. Budget **7:00**, 10 beats, **2 errors (E36–E37)**.

**What we teach:** On the initial-rate against substrate-concentration graph, Vmax is the maximum rate, the plateau. Km is the substrate concentration at which the rate is half of Vmax (the S21/51 stem's wording). Construction: draw the horizontal at Vmax, halve it, read across to the curve, drop to the concentration axis: that reading is Km. A lower Km means the enzyme reaches half its maximum rate at a lower substrate concentration, so it has a higher affinity for its substrate; Km compares affinities of different enzymes, each read from **its own** half-Vmax, never a common horizontal across different maxima. Vmax and Km are different quantities in different units; the initial gradient is neither. For this graphical construction, use a defensible Vmax from the plateau or a supplied value; do not call the highest plotted point Vmax when the rate is still rising (S23/51 Q1(d)(iii)). No algebraic derivation, no reciprocal plot.

**Exact mark-scheme keys:** [S23/31 Q1(b)(ii), MS p7](pastpapers/2023/June/9700_s23_ms_31.pdf#page=7): "shows Vmax on graph ; shows ½ Vmax on graph ; 18 ;". ### 3.2.3

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> explain the effects of reversible inhibitors, both competitive and non-competitive, on enzyme activity

**Commands / content type:** explain. **MECHANISM / DATA**. Budget **6:45**, 10 beats, **1 error (E41)**.

**What we teach:** The comparison changes here from different enzymes to **the same enzyme with and without inhibitor under otherwise fixed conditions**. A competitive inhibitor has a shape similar to the substrate, binds reversibly in the active site and competes with the substrate; increasing substrate concentration can overcome it, so Vmax is unchanged and Km increases (curve to the right, merging with the uninhibited curve at the plateau). A non-competitive inhibitor binds reversibly at a site other than the active site (the credited vocabulary "allosteric site" names that site; no allosteric-regulation lesson), changing the shape of the active site so it is no longer complementary; in the simplified non-competitive model drawn here, Vmax decreases, increasing substrate concentration does not restore the uninhibited Vmax, and Km is unchanged. Do not generalise that unchanged Km to every inhibitor that binds away from the active site; other inhibition classes are not taught. Both are reversible: activity returns when the inhibitor leaves. Product inhibition (specimen P2 Q3(b)) illustrates product removal in 3.2.4; it does not make an end-product feedback pathway compulsory. Irreversible inhibitors are not taught.

**Exact mark-scheme keys:** [S24/23 Q5(b), MS p9](pastpapers/2024/June/9700_s24_ms_23.pdf#page=9): "(non-competitive inhibitor) binds to, allosteric site / site other than active site / AW ; (binding causes) change in shape of active site ; active site no longer complementary to the substrate / AW ;". [S21/51 Q1(c)(ii), (iv), MS p7](pastpapers/2021/June/9700_s21_ms_51.pdf#page=7). Keys: S24/12 Q10 A, S20/12 Q13 B, M23/12 Q16 B ([QP p8](pastpapers/2023/March/9700_m23_qp_12.pdf#page=8)).

### 3.2.4

**Syllabus verbatim — [p20](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=20):**

> investigate the difference in activity between an enzyme immobilised in alginate and the same enzyme free in solution, and state the advantages of using immobilised enzymes

**Commands / content type:** investigate; state. **PRACTICAL / STATEMENT**. Budget **6:00**, 12 beats, **0 errors**.

**What we teach:** Immobilising: mix the enzyme with sodium alginate solution and drip it into calcium chloride solution; calcium alginate beads form with the enzyme trapped inside ([syllabus p58](syllabus/664560-2025-2027-syllabus.pdf#page=58) lists both reagents). **The chosen comparison (one complete investigation):** amylase on starch, read by the L2 timed iodine sampling. Use equal measured aliquots of the same amylase stock: entrap one in a counted batch of standardised alginate beads and keep the other free. This matches nominal starting enzyme input; it does not by itself prove equal retained active enzyme after bead formation and rinsing. Keep final aqueous reaction volume, starting starch concentration, buffered pH, maintained temperature, mixing and sampling conditions comparable. Standardise bead size, preparation and rinsing, include an enzyme-free bead control, and compare the measured operational activities of the two preparations. Do not attribute every difference solely to diffusion or claim equal retained activity without checking preparation losses. Sample both at the same stated regular times into iodine; record time to the same loss-of-blue-black endpoint and compare 1/t; do not derive an initial rate from by-eye colour intensity. Report the measured activities side by side. Entrapment can reduce the measured rate by limiting substrate diffusion into the bead; compare the preparations' measured activities rather than promising that result. A separate run, retrieving, rinsing and re-using the beads with fresh starch, demonstrates re-use and is kept distinct from the activity comparison. Advantages, at "state" strength: the enzyme is easily separated from the product, so the product is not contaminated and the enzyme can be re-used; the process can run continuously (substrate passed through a column of beads); immobilisation can improve operational stability over a range of temperature and pH; in a flow-through arrangement that removes product from the enzyme, product inhibition can be reduced. Immobilisation alone does not continuously remove product from a closed batch, and neither a lower rate nor a wider operating range is guaranteed.

**Exact mark-scheme keys / limits:** [Specimen P2 Q3(b)(ii), MS p12](cambridge-official/9700-specimen-2022-ms2.pdf#page=12): "products and enzyme kept separated / AW ; product removed immediately ;"; (b)(iii): "inert / unreactive / cannot be digested by lactase / AW ; non-toxic ; insoluble ; long shelf-life ;". [W20/21 Q2(b)](pastpapers/2020/November/9700_w20_qp_21.pdf#page=6): "Lipase was immobilised in alginate beads" as context (no separate mark). No fixed-sample marking point tests the comparison or the advantages directly; the specimen and Learner Guide (p18) set the content.

**Scope ceiling and boundaries:** alginate entrapment only; no other methods, no industrial case studies.

**Chemistry on screen:** bead formation and substrate diffusing into a bead are motion; the amylase reaction inside the bead is the L2 `Hydrolyse` replay if shown.

**Visual needs:** `AlginateBeadRig`; side-by-side free/bead tubes with the `AmylaseIodineSampler`; a column with beads and flowing substrate; reuses `RateGraph`.
