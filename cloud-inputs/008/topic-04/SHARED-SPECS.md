- **REAL-WORLD SAMPLES rule (Durai, 26 Sep; binding).** Whenever a lesson uses a real material (potato, beetroot, red onion, blood cells, Visking tubing and what it holds, agar and its indicator or dye), the narration says **what in that material the method responds to** and **whether it fits the method** (range, clarity, interferences) — at least once, in an explain beat, with an on-screen note. In exam-question beats the **mark-scheme answer comes first**; any real-world extra is spoken as beyond the scheme ("the scheme doesn't need this, but…") and shown on a panel labelled **beyond the mark scheme** (dashed border, no tick, no MS tab), never looking like a marking point. - **No text-only frames.** A model or apparatus is on screen in every frame. The **first item of every beat's Visual action starts "From the first frame, …"** (or "From the beat's first frame, …") and names the model/apparatus on screen. Objectives surfaces: own styled surface, not the lesson diagram, each line beside a flat authored pictogram. Error beats: the relevant model held dimmed beside the card. Exam close: the familiar lesson layout stays on screen beside the forms surface.

- **Distinguish measured, calculated, supplied and illustrative quantities by labels; calculated variables may be plotted when appropriate** (plan check). Set start values: open circle, *set, not a reading*. Interpolations: construction lines within the tested range, never plotted points. Means labelled means; invented data *our illustrative data*; W20/51 Table 1.1 values *supplied data: W20/51 Table 1.1*. No fabricated values labelled 'illustrative'.

State "rendered still-frame verification pending" in Assets.
- **Colour changes only through real colours** (plan's Visual-integrity paragraph): Benedict's blue → green → yellow → orange → brick red, only as far as the sample goes; iodine blue-black or stays yellow-brown, one-frame switch; thymolphthalein agar blue → paler blue at the moving boundary → colourless; methylene blue at decreasing intensity towards the edge; beetroot water only in the pigment's red-violet at increasing depth; red onion sap stays pink-red. No RGB tween between different hues.
- **Molecular movement is MOTION** (plan's motion paragraph). ATP is a token: *ATP* → *ADP* + *Pi* in one rendered frame with the caption *ATP hydrolysed; energy used to change the carrier's shape; not a mechanism*. No atom-resolved reactions in this topic. No phospholipid flip-flop. Carbohydrate chains on the external face only.
- **Scope:** the plan's ceilings and calibration §4 DO-NOT-ADD; 4.2.6 exclusion (never name solute potential, pressure potential, ψs, ψp); "concentration of water" never used; osmosis always in water-potential terms.
- **Typicality sweep** on every sentence containing *all, every, always, never, only, no, nothing, cannot, because, must, needs* and causal *so/since*, recorded in the absolutes sweep.
- **Each lesson stands alone:** own hook (a real question, answered by the end), context (20–35 s), objectives (2–3), explanation with the framing sentence (why the mechanism exists), handle converted at once into the creditworthy sentence, recap **on the same diagram** (static, points faded in in place; "No new slide."), exam close (forms, cited; a reject card captioned as ours unless it is a verbatim reject line; callback to the hook).
- **Length:** runtime = words ÷ 120. Report honestly against the plan budget; if over, give a cut list that never touches an error beat.

## 4. Shared models — identical in every lesson

Orientation fixed across the topic: **outside the cell at the top, cytoplasm at the bottom**; in membrane-crossing scenes the gradient runs **across the membrane, along its normal**; left-to-right compartment layouts explicitly rotate and relabel the membrane (carbohydrate chains still face the outside) (plan check should-fix 2). Captions: molecular models *schematic; not to scale*; particle fields *particles drawn schematically; not to scale; far fewer than real*. Colour **roles** (the builder maps them to the house palette; the same role is always the same colour): phospholipid head = warm amber; tails = mid grey; proteins = teal; cholesterol = ochre; carbohydrate chains = green bead chains; water tokens = small pale blue circles; glucose tokens = orange hexagons; sucrose tokens = larger orange double-hexagons; ion tokens = small violet circles with + or −; O₂ tokens = two joined red circles (a token, not a bond diagram); ligand shapes = magenta; ATP token = yellow rounded tag. Error/contrast treatment = terracotta (house style).

### `PhospholipidToken` (4.1.1-2)
Round head + two tails (one straight, one with a single kink). First-use labels *hydrophilic head (phosphate-containing)*, *hydrophobic fatty-acid tails*; tag `recall: Topic 2 lipids`. Heads always face water; tails never in water.

### `FluidMosaicMembrane` (4.1.1-2; component layers for 4.1.2 published there)
Bilayer section, 12 phospholipids per leaflet visible. Component ids and positions (left → right): `glycolipid` (outer leaflet, position 2, carbohydrate chain of 4 beads above the surface); `intrinsic-channel` (spanning, positions 4–5, central pore lined by hydrophilic R groups, drawn as a light core); `cholesterol` (one in each leaflet at positions 6 and 7, OH end at head level, ring among tails); `receptor-glycoprotein` (spanning, positions 8–9, binding site cup on the outer face, carbohydrate chain of 3 beads beside it); `intrinsic-carrier` (spanning, position 10–11, a binding-site notch facing outside in its rest state); `glycoprotein` (spanning, position 12, chain of 5 beads); `extrinsic` (on the cytoplasmic face under positions 3–4). Region labels *outside the cell (watery)*, *cytoplasm (watery)*, *hydrophobic core*. States: `assemble` (scattered tokens in a `WaterField` drift into the bilayer over ~3 s, motion), `full`, `highlight:<id>` (the component brightens, others dim to 50%). Motion contract: phospholipids jitter and drift sideways (≤ 1 token width per 2 s), proteins drift more slowly; nothing crosses between leaflets; chains stay on the external face.

Cholesterol (4.1.3): may be animated as a clearly labelled qualitative schematic (*schematic; not measured data*): restrained motion at higher temperatures, prevention of close packing at lower temperatures; no speeds, temperatures or numbers. Proteins: intrinsic proteins are embedded in the bilayer; the channel, carrier and receptor drawn here span it and are transmembrane proteins.

### `WaterField` (4.1.1-2) / `DiffusionField` (4.2.1a)
Water tokens with random motion. `DiffusionField` adds solute tokens with a left-high/right-low distribution, a membrane or open boundary as the lesson needs, a **net-movement arrow** and a **crossing counter** (*left → right: n · right → left: m*). At equilibrium motion continues both ways, the counter's two numbers run level and the net arrow fades.

### `TransportProteinSet` (4.1.3 previews `channel-open`, `carrier-bind`; 4.2.1a full passive states; 4.2.1b `pump-ATP`)
`channel-open`: ion/polar tokens enter the pore and pass through, down the gradient; the channel never changes shape. `carrier-bind` → `carrier-flip` → `carrier-release` → `carrier-reset`: the solute seats in the notch (0.6 s), the protein changes shape over 0.8 s (motion, silhouettes interpolate; no cut) so the notch faces the other side, the solute leaves (0.5 s), the protein returns (0.8 s). `pump-ATP` (a simplified directly ATP-driven carrier model; its transported ion stays **generic**, never labelled nitrate or any named transporter; no co-transport): as the carrier cycle, with particle density higher on the release side (against the gradient); an ATP token docks on the cytoplasmic face; **on the frame the shape change starts, the token switches in one rendered frame to *ADP* + *Pi*** (caption *ATP hydrolysed; energy used to change the carrier's shape; not a mechanism* on screen throughout the cycle); ADP and Pi drift away. Labels **channel protein**, **carrier protein**, **binding site**.

### `ReceptorLigand` (4.1.3 basic; 4.1.4 adds `wrong-ligand-fail`, `seat`, `response-uptake`)
The `receptor-glycoprotein` of the membrane with its **binding site** labelled (never "active site"). Ligand A (complementary, magenta wedge) and ligand B (non-complementary, magenta square). `wrong-ligand-fail`: B approaches, touches the rim, rocks, fails to seat, drifts away (1.5 s). `seat`: A approaches and seats flush (0.8 s). `response-uptake` (replaces a bare pulse; MF4): the named response is shown as a labelled increase in uptake — in the insulin example, more glucose tokens crossing into the muscle cell, label *increased glucose uptake: the specific response* — with no intracellular pathway drawn.

### `SignallingScene` (4.1.4)
Left: secreting cell — a pancreatic beta cell with insulin vesicles (label *our example*), secreting by exocytosis (this example's mechanism, not a definition for every ligand); centre: blood-vessel segment with flow arrows carrying ligand tokens widely, ligands leaving into tissue fluid before reaching cells (never drawn as blood touching every cell); right: a target muscle cell (receptors complementary to A), a second target cell type sharing the same receptor, and a non-target cell (receptors of a different shape). Stage labels *secretion*, *transport*, *binding*, *specific response*.

### `VesicleTransport` (4.1.4 `exocytosis`; 4.2.1b `phagocytosis`, `pinocytosis`)
`exocytosis`: vesicle travels to the membrane, membranes fuse, contents released outside (motion). `phagocytosis`: membrane extends around a rod-shaped bacterium, encloses it, pinches off into a vesicle (motion). `pinocytosis`: membrane dips around a small droplet of fluid with dissolved tokens, pinches off into a small vesicle. Each carries an *energy from ATP* tag (no token reaction for bulk transport).

### `WaterPotentialModel` (4.2.1a; `cell-vs-solution` added by 4.2.6)
Two compartments separated by a partially permeable membrane strip (drawn as a short bilayer segment with gaps only water passes in this model); left compartment fewer solute tokens, right more. Beside it a vertical **water-potential scale**: **0 kPa — pure water at atmospheric pressure (reference)** at the top, "more negative" downward for the solutions in the comparison (same temperature and pressure), no other numbers unless the lesson supplies them. Labels *higher water potential (less negative)* on the left, *lower water potential (more negative)* on the right. Water tokens cross both ways; the counter shows more left → right; net arrow left → right, labelled *net movement of water by osmosis*; at equal water potentials crossings continue both ways, the counter runs level and the net arrow fades. `cell-vs-solution`: the right compartment becomes a cell outline (plant or animal per lesson), the left the surrounding solution; the comparison label reads *initially: solution's water potential higher / equal / lower than the cell's*. For a plant cell taking up water the net arrow fades as the cell's water potential rises to equal the solution's while molecules keep crossing both ways; never a permanent influx arrow beside a fully turgid cell.

### `UptakeGraph` (4.2.1b)
Axes *rate of uptake* against *external concentration* (no numbers); simple diffusion straight and rising; carrier-mediated rising then levelling off; caption *schematic model; not measured data*; inset of four carrier proteins filling one by one as concentration rises.

### `CellOsmosisSet` (4.2.6)
Plant cell: cell wall (thick outline), cell surface membrane, cytoplasm, large vacuole (tonoplast), nucleus. States `plant-turgid` (protoplast pressed on the wall, wall bulging very slightly; reached by net uptake until the cell's water potential equals the solution's, net arrow faded), `plant-equal` (no net movement; may be turgid; never labelled necessarily flaccid or as equal solute concentrations), `plant-flaccid` (protoplast no longer pressing), `plant-plasmolysed` (protoplast withdrawn from the fixed wall at the corners and sides; gap filled with external solution tokens that entered through the wall; the coloured vacuole's edge is not the cell-surface membrane). Red blood cell: `rbc-normal` (biconcave disc, side and face views; shown unchanged in the particular equal-potential comparison, never as a rule that equality determines shape), `rbc-swelling` (spherical), `rbc-haemolysed` (membrane tear, contents dispersing, schematic; "haemolysis" is specific to red blood cells), `rbc-crenated` (shrunken, spiky outline). Each state labelled with the *initial* comparison of water potentials. No component-potential names or equations.
