## Proposed checkpoints and progression

1. **Transport discrimination:** select a route for a specified molecule and explain the membrane property, gradient and energy requirement relevant to it.
2. **Mechanism transfer:** explain a plateau in a new uptake graph, then distinguish it from an osmosis equilibrium.
3. **Water-potential/data gate:** calculate percentage mass change, locate the zero crossing and interpret a supplied water-potential table. Separate arithmetic from biological meaning.
4. **Planning gate:** produce a reproducible osmosis method and critique one inadequately controlled method. Require operational detail, not a list of variable names.

**Common progression rule — proposal, not a Cambridge pass standard:** require each named core relationship in the particular checkpoint to be demonstrated; do not allow unrelated recall points to compensate for a missing mechanism. Give feedback on the failed relationship, then use a changed example. Record identification, explanation, data handling and practical evidence separately. A successful multiple-choice answer alone does not establish explanatory precision. Neither a fixed percentage nor a specified number of retries is established by these schemes.

Accept scientifically equivalent wording where the scheme permits it. Check the biological agent, action, target, direction and relevant condition together. Do not demand a particular conjunction if an equivalent causal relationship is explicit. The science marking principles allow unambiguous spellings and equivalent expression, but withhold a point when a correct statement is contradicted in the same part; irrelevant error is not a universal cancellation rule. Slash-separated alternatives share a point. Calculations may receive full credit from a correct answer unless working is specifically requested. Asking for visible reasoning in a learning gate is our deliberate additional diagnostic requirement. See [m24_22 science marking principles and notation, MS PDF p. 2–4](pastpapers/2024/March/9700_m24_ms_22.pdf#page=2). Plain text does not preserve all underlining; no mandatory-underlined-word claims are inferred from extraction alone.

## Revision-note coverage not established by this sample

The descriptions distinguish plant and animal osmotic outcomes, surface-area investigations and several membrane components. The citations do not supply a complete rubric for every named component, cell state or lysis/plasmolysis case. In particular, the water-potential task does not establish a requirement to derive osmotic-pressure equations or calculate water potential from thermodynamic constants.

**No universal “not examined” claim follows.** Absence here means no demonstrated marked demand within the stated sample/boundary, not permission to remove a syllabus requirement. Do not create compulsory gates from every note heading, add extra named examples, or require university-level detail to make a short answer appear more rigorous.

## Component 5: a separate design-and-evidence gate

Score **question/variables → operational method → comparability → replication → analysis → warranted conclusion** separately. Do not substitute subject recall for a feasible experiment. [w20_51 Q1(a–c), MS PDF p. 7–9](pastpapers/2020/November/9700_w20_ms_51.pdf#page=7) credits range/dilution, controls and percentage-change interpretation. [m24_52 Q1(b)(ii), MS PDF p. 5](pastpapers/2024/March/9700_m24_ms_52.pdf#page=5) credits limitations including untested intermediate concentrations, variation among cells and missing uncertainty information. A conclusion about this tissue, duration and dataset must not become a universal physiological law. The exact combination of optional planning marks remains task-specific; our checkpoint may target one missing design relationship at a time.
