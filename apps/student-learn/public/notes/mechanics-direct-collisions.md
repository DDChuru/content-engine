# Direct collisions — revision notes

## What you will learn

- Choose signed velocities.
- Conserve the pair's momentum.
- Solve separate and sticking outcomes.

A **direct collision** occurs when two bodies meet while moving along the same straight line. They may approach in opposite directions, a moving body may hit a stationary one, or a faster body may catch a slower one ahead. After impact they may separate, one may stop, or they may **coalesce**: stick together and move with one common velocity.

## When momentum is conserved

Take the **two colliding particles together** as the system. On a smooth horizontal surface, with negligible external horizontal force during the short impact, the system's total horizontal momentum is conserved. Weight and the normal reaction balance vertically. The contact forces between the particles are internal to this system and act equally in opposite directions.

**Total momentum before = total momentum after.**

\(m_Au_A+m_Bu_B=m_Av_A+m_Bv_B\)

Here \(m_A,m_B\) are masses, \(u_A,u_B\) are velocities immediately before impact, and \(v_A,v_B\) are velocities immediately afterwards. Momentum has units \(\text{kg m s}^{-1}\).

Each particle's momentum can change during the collision. It is the **total for the pair** that is conserved. If a significant external horizontal force acts during impact, this conservation equation cannot simply be assumed for the pair.

## Choose the signs before substituting

Choose one positive direction and keep it throughout. In this lesson, **right is positive**: rightward velocities are positive and leftward velocities are negative. Mass remains positive.

Draw both the before and after states, showing every mass, velocity and direction. If an after direction is unknown, draw a tentative arrow and label the unknown. A negative answer means the actual motion is opposite to the chosen positive direction. **Speed** is the nonnegative magnitude; **velocity** includes direction.

## One question, two separate trials

Particles \(A\) and \(B\), of masses \(2\text{ kg}\) and \(3\text{ kg}\), approach each other along the same straight line on a smooth horizontal surface. Immediately before impact, \(A\) moves right at \(4\text{ m s}^{-1}\) and \(B\) moves left at \(1\text{ m s}^{-1}\). External horizontal forces are negligible during impact.

Two **independent trials** start from these same initial conditions:

- **Trial 1:** \(A\) rebounds left at \(2\text{ m s}^{-1}\). Find \(B\)'s velocity immediately after impact, including its direction.
- **Trial 2:** The particles stick together. Find their common velocity immediately after impact.

Trial 2 is a fresh start. It does not use the velocities produced by trial 1.

| State | Particle \(A\), mass \(2\text{ kg}\) | Particle \(B\), mass \(3\text{ kg}\) |
|---|---|---|
| Before either trial | \(u_A=+4\text{ m s}^{-1}\), right → | \(u_B=-1\text{ m s}^{-1}\), left ← |
| After trial 1 | \(v_A=-2\text{ m s}^{-1}\), left ← | \(v_B\) unknown; tentatively right → |
| After trial 2 | Common velocity \(v\), unknown | Same common velocity \(v\), unknown |

### Trial 1: the particles separate

With negligible external horizontal force, **total momentum before = total momentum after**:

\(m_Au_A+m_Bu_B=m_Av_A+m_Bv_B\)

Substitute signed velocities, including both leftward velocities:

\(2(4)+3(-1)=2(-2)+3v_B\)

\(8-3=-4+3v_B\)

\(5=-4+3v_B\)

Add \(4\) to both sides, then divide by \(3\):

\(9=3v_B\)

\(v_B=+3\text{ m s}^{-1}\)

The positive sign means that \(B\) moves **right**, with speed \(3\text{ m s}^{-1}\). It has reversed direction. The after diagram now has \(A\) moving left at \(2\text{ m s}^{-1}\) and \(B\) moving right at \(3\text{ m s}^{-1}\), so the particles move apart.

### Trial 2: the particles coalesce

Reset to the original before velocities. The particles now stick, so \(v_A=v_B=v\). Their total after momentum is \(m_Av+m_Bv=(m_A+m_B)v\).

With the same negligible-external-force condition, **total momentum before = total momentum after**:

\(m_Au_A+m_Bu_B=(m_A+m_B)v\)

\(2(4)+3(-1)=(2+3)v\)

\(8-3=5v\)

\(5=5v\)

\(v=+1\text{ m s}^{-1}\)

Both particles move **right at the common speed** \(1\text{ m s}^{-1}\). They remain together. The after state uses both masses and one velocity; it cannot use two different after velocities for particles that have stuck.

## Check your understanding

Return to the condition of negligible external horizontal force during impact. In trial 1, \(A\) reverses direction. Does that mean the pair's total momentum is not conserved? Pause before reading on.

**Answer:** No. The reversal changes \(A\)'s individual momentum, while \(B\)'s momentum has the equal and opposite change. The pair's total is conserved. Both particles must be included in the system.

## Common slips

- Substituting a leftward speed as a positive velocity after choosing right as positive.
- Conserving the momentum of just one particle instead of the pair.
- Omitting the external-force condition before applying conservation.
- Giving a signed answer without stating its direction, or calling a negative velocity a negative speed.
- Using only one mass when the particles coalesce.
- Carrying trial 1's final velocities into the separate trial 2.

Start with the conditions, draw the before-and-after states, write the general momentum equation, and only then substitute the signed values.
