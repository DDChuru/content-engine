# F = ma

Newton’s second law links the **resultant force** on one particle to its acceleration:

\[
F_{\text{net}}=ma.
\]

Use force in newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg), and acceleration in metres per second squared (m s⁻²). Here the mass is constant. Combine all forces in the chosen direction before applying the law.

## Direction matters

Choose a positive direction and keep it throughout the equation. With right positive, a driving force \(D\) to the right and resistance \(R\) to the left give

\[
D-R=ma.
\]

Acceleration describes how velocity changes. A car moving right but slowing down has acceleration to the left: \(a<0\). A zero resultant means zero acceleration; a moving particle continues at constant velocity.

Newton’s third-law pair acts on **different bodies**. Those two forces do not cancel in the force diagram of one body.

## Mass, weight and vertical motion

Mass is measured in kg. Weight is the gravitational force, measured in N:

\[
W=mg.
\]

Consider a particle hanging from a taut string and accelerating upwards. Take up as positive. Tension \(T\) acts upwards; weight \(mg\) acts downwards. Applying the law vertically gives

\[
T-mg=ma.
\]

At constant velocity, \(a=0\), so \(T=mg\). With upward acceleration, \(T>mg\). Apply the law separately in horizontal and vertical directions.

## Worked example: a car starting from rest

Model a car as a particle of constant mass **1200 kg**, on a straight, horizontal road. Its driving force is a constant **3000 N to the right** and its total resistance is a constant **600 N to the left**. The car starts from rest. Find its acceleration and the distance travelled in the first **4 s**.

Given: \(m=1200\), \(D=3000\), \(R=600\), \(u=0\), \(t=4\). Unknown: \(a\) and \(s\). Choose right as positive.

### 1. Find the acceleration

\[
F_{\text{net}}=ma
\]
\[
D-R=ma
\]
\[
3000-600=1200a
\]
\[
2400=1200a
\]
\[
a=\frac{2400}{1200}=\boxed{2\text{ m s}^{-2}}.
\]

The acceleration is to the right. Velocity increases by 2 m s⁻¹ each second. The force in \(F=ma\) is **2400 N**, the resultant, not the driving force alone.

### 2. Find the distance

Constant forces and constant mass give constant acceleration, so suvat applies. Choose the equation containing \(s,u,a,t\):

\[
s=ut+\frac12at^2
\]
\[
s=0\times4+\frac12\times2\times4^2
\]
\[
s=0+1\times16=\boxed{16\text{ m}}.
\]

Since the car travels only to the right, its distance equals its positive displacement. Acceleration is the link between the force equation and suvat.

If resistance increased but remained below the same driving force, the positive resultant would be smaller. From the same rest condition over the same 4 s, acceleration and distance would both decrease.

## Check yourself

A moving car has equal forward and backward forces. Must it stop?

**No.** Its resultant force and acceleration are zero, so its existing velocity stays constant.

## Remember

- Use the resultant force.
- Choose signs for acceleration.
- Link forces to distance travelled.
