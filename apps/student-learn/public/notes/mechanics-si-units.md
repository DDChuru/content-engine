# S.I. Units for Mechanics

## What S.I. units are and why they matter

S.I. is the shared measurement system used in science. It lets everyone interpret a value in the same way and makes equations consistent. In mechanics, convert the data to one coherent set of units before substituting into a formula. The usual set is metres, seconds and kilograms.

## The three fundamental units used in mechanics

- **Metre** (\(\mathrm{m}\)): the S.I. base unit of length. Use it for distance, displacement, height and position. Recall it as: **metre measures how far**.
- **Second** (\(\mathrm{s}\)): the S.I. base unit of time. Use it for durations and time intervals. Recall it as: **second measures how long**.
- **Kilogram** (\(\mathrm{kg}\)): the S.I. base unit of mass. It measures the amount of matter—and therefore inertia—in an object. Recall it as: **kilogram measures how hard motion is to change**.

Length, time and mass are the **base quantities**. Metre, second and kilogram are their **base units**.

## Mass is not weight

Mass is a scalar: it has size but no direction, and it is measured in \(\mathrm{kg}\). Weight is the gravitational force on a mass. It is a vector directed towards the attracting body and is measured in newtons, \(\mathrm{N}\).

Near Earth’s surface,

\(W = mg\)

where \(W\) is weight, \(m\) is mass and \(g\) is gravitational acceleration. A mass does not disappear when gravity changes, but its weight does.

## Prefixes and conversions

Think of a prefix as a scale factor attached to a unit.

- kilo means \(10^3\): \(1\,\mathrm{km}=1000\,\mathrm{m}\). Multiply by \(1000\) to go from \(\mathrm{km}\) to \(\mathrm{m}\); divide by \(1000\) to go back.
- centi means \(10^{-2}\): \(1\,\mathrm{cm}=0.01\,\mathrm{m}\).
- milli means \(10^{-3}\): \(1\,\mathrm{g}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}\) and \(1\,\mathrm{mg}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{kg}\).
- nano means \(10^{-9}\): \(1\,\mathrm{nm}=10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}\).
- Time is not decimal: \(1\,\mathrm{min}=60\,\mathrm{s}\), so multiply minutes by \(60\).
- A tonne is \(1000\,\mathrm{kg}\): \(1\,\mathrm{tonne}=1000\,\mathrm{kg}\).

Derived units combine base units. Speed uses \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), also written \(\mathrm{m/s}\). Acceleration uses \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), also written \(\mathrm{m/s^2}\). Convert both the numerator and denominator: for example, \(1\,\mathrm{km/min}=1000/60\,\mathrm{m/s}\).

## Worked examples

### Example A — distance and speed

A cyclist travels \(2.4\,\mathrm{km}\) in \(8\,\mathrm{min}\). Find the average speed in \(\mathrm{m/s}\).

\(2.4\,\mathrm{km}=2.4\times1000=2400\,\mathrm{m}\)

\(8\,\mathrm{min}=8\times60=480\,\mathrm{s}\)

\(\text{speed}=\frac{2400}{480}=5\,\mathrm{m/s}\)

### Example B — grams, kilograms and weight

A parcel has mass \(750\,\mathrm{g}\). Find its weight when \(g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\).

\(750\,\mathrm{g}=\frac{750}{1000}=0.75\,\mathrm{kg}\)

\(W=mg=0.75\times9.8=7.35\,\mathrm{N}\)

### Example C — tonnes to kilograms

A lorry has mass \(3.2\,\mathrm{tonnes}\). Express the mass in kilograms.

\(3.2\,\mathrm{tonnes}=3.2\times1000=3200\,\mathrm{kg}\)

### Example D — acceleration

A car’s speed rises from \(6\,\mathrm{m/s}\) to \(24\,\mathrm{m/s}\) in \(4\,\mathrm{s}\). Find its average acceleration.

\(\Delta v=24-6=18\,\mathrm{m/s}\)

\(a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}=\frac{18}{4}=4.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\)

## Quick self-check

- **Question A:** Convert \(5.6\,\mathrm{km}\) to metres.  
  **Answer:** \(5.6\times1000=5600\,\mathrm{m}\).
- **Question B:** Convert \(3.5\,\mathrm{min}\) to seconds.  
  **Answer:** \(3.5\times60=210\,\mathrm{s}\).
- **Question C:** Convert \(420\,\mathrm{g}\) to kilograms.  
  **Answer:** \(420/1000=0.42\,\mathrm{kg}\).
- **Question D:** Which is a vector: mass or weight?  
  **Answer:** Weight. Mass is scalar; weight is a force with direction.
- **Question E:** A runner covers \(150\,\mathrm{m}\) in \(30\,\mathrm{s}\). Find the speed.  
  **Answer:** \(150/30=5\,\mathrm{m/s}\).

## Common exam slips

- Substituting kilometres, minutes, grams or tonnes directly into a formula that expects metres, seconds and kilograms.
- Multiplying when the conversion needs division, or vice versa. Write the unit equality first.
- Treating \(\mathrm{g}\), the gram, as though it were \(g\), gravitational acceleration.
- Giving mass in \(\mathrm{N}\) or weight in \(\mathrm{kg}\).
- Writing speed in \(\mathrm{m/s^2}\) or acceleration in \(\mathrm{m/s}\).
- Converting only one part of a compound unit.
- Dropping the direction or sign when reporting a vector quantity.
