# Coefficient of Friction

Friction acts parallel to a contact surface and opposes actual or impending relative sliding. A rough contact can supply friction, but it does not always supply the maximum possible amount.

The coefficient of friction, μ, depends on the two materials in contact and has no units. If R is the normal reaction and f is the magnitude of actual friction,

$$f \leq \mu R, \qquad f_{\max}=\mu R.$$

For a body initially at rest, friction matches the horizontal demand up to this limit. At the limit, the body is in **limiting equilibrium**: it is on the point of moving but remains stationary in the ideal model. If the pull exceeds the limit, the body begins to slide. This lesson uses the ideal model in which sliding friction also has magnitude μR.

## One box, three independent trials

A 5 kg box rests on a rough horizontal floor. The coefficient of friction is 0.4 and g = 10 m s⁻². Contact is maintained. In each independent trial the box starts from rest and a constant horizontal pull acts to the right:

- A: 12 N
- B: 20 N
- C: 25 N

Find the normal reaction, limiting friction, actual friction and acceleration in each trial.

There is no vertical acceleration, and the pull is horizontal. Therefore,

$$R-mg=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad R=mg=5\times10=50\text{ N}.$$

The limiting friction is

$$f_{\max}=\mu R=0.4\times50=20\text{ N}.$$

For horizontal motion, taking right as positive,

$$\text{resultant force}=ma,\qquad P-f=ma.$$

| Trial | Pull right | Actual friction left | Acceleration | What happens? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A | 12 N | 12 N | (12−12)/5 = 0 m s⁻² | Stays at rest below the limit |
| B | 20 N | 20 N | (20−20)/5 = 0 m s⁻² | Stays at rest in limiting equilibrium |
| C | 25 N | 20 N | (25−20)/5 = 1 m s⁻² right | Slides and speeds up right |

In A, writing f = 20 N would be wrong. Only 12 N is needed to balance the pull. In B, reaching the limit still leaves zero resultant force. In C, friction cannot match 25 N; the remaining 5 N resultant accelerates the 5 kg mass.

Each trial is reset to rest. These are not consecutive force changes acting on one continuously moving box.

## Check your understanding

Return to trial A, starting from rest. What if the pull is removed altogether?

Actual friction is zero because there is no horizontal force tending to move the box. The available limit is still 20 N. Rough contact alone does not imply nonzero friction.

You can now distinguish actual friction from its limit, and find friction and acceleration in three independent trials.
