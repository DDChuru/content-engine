# Momentum — five-minute revision

## What this lesson covers

Cambridge 9709, section 4.3, page 32 — exact syllabus excerpt:

> use the definition of linear momentum

By the end you can calculate momentum, give its units and interpret its direction.

## Momentum is mass times velocity

**Linear momentum** is the product of a particle's mass and its velocity:

\[
\text{momentum}=\text{mass}\times\text{velocity},
\]

\[
\mathbf{p}=m\mathbf{v}.
\]

Here \(m\) is mass in \(\text{kg}\), and \(\mathbf{v}\) is velocity in \(\text{m s}^{-1}\). Multiplying these units gives

\[
\text{unit of momentum}=\text{kg}\times\text{m s}^{-1}
=\text{kg m s}^{-1}.
\]

You can also write this as \(\text{kg m/s}\).

Momentum is a **vector**: it has both magnitude and direction. Since mass is positive, multiplying velocity by mass preserves its direction. Momentum points in the same direction as the motion.

## Choose a positive direction before calculating

For motion along a straight line, choose a positive direction and use signed velocities in

\[
p=mv.
\]

Here \(p\) and \(v\) are signed components in that chosen direction. If right is positive, rightward velocity and momentum are positive; leftward velocity and momentum are negative.

The negative sign describes direction. It does not mean negative mass or negative speed. The magnitude of momentum is non-negative.

## Worked comparison — two separate tracks

Trolleys \(A\) and \(B\) move on separate parallel straight tracks, so they do not collide. Trolley \(A\) has mass \(2\text{ kg}\) and moves right at \(6\text{ m s}^{-1}\). Trolley \(B\) has mass \(3\text{ kg}\) and moves left at \(4\text{ m s}^{-1}\).

**Take right as positive. Find each trolley's signed momentum, then compare their magnitudes and directions.**

| Trolley | Mass | Motion on its own track | Signed velocity | Find |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(2\text{ kg}\) | \(6\text{ m s}^{-1}\) right \(\rightarrow\) | \(+6\text{ m s}^{-1}\) | \(p_A\) |
| \(B\) | \(3\text{ kg}\) | \(\leftarrow\) \(4\text{ m s}^{-1}\) left | \(-4\text{ m s}^{-1}\) | \(p_B\) |

### Trolley A

Momentum equals mass times velocity:

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_A&=m_Av_A\\
&=2\times(+6)\\
&=+12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Its momentum has magnitude \(12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}\) and points right, the chosen positive direction.

### Trolley B

Momentum equals mass times velocity. Because \(B\) moves left while right is positive, its velocity is negative:

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_B&=m_Bv_B\\
&=3\times(-4)\\
&=-12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Its momentum has magnitude \(12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}\) and points left. The negative sign belongs to the velocity and momentum, not to the mass.

### Equal magnitudes, different vectors

The magnitudes are equal:

\[
|p_A|=|+12|=12\text{ kg m s}^{-1},
\qquad
|p_B|=|-12|=12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}.
\]

Their momentum vectors are different because their directions are opposite. Equal magnitudes alone do not make two vectors equal: equal vectors must also have the same direction.

## Self-check — change the positive direction

Use the same two separate tracks and the same motion: \(A\), of mass \(2\text{ kg}\), still moves right at \(6\text{ m s}^{-1}\); \(B\), of mass \(3\text{ kg}\), still moves left at \(4\text{ m s}^{-1}\).

This time, **take left as positive**. Find \(p_A\) and \(p_B\). Do the trolleys actually reverse direction?

Pause and choose the sign of each velocity before reading the answer.

### Answer

With left positive, rightward motion is negative. Thus \(v_A=-6\text{ m s}^{-1}\) and \(v_B=+4\text{ m s}^{-1}\).

Momentum equals mass times velocity:

\[
p=mv.
\]

For \(A\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_A&=m_Av_A\\
&=2\times(-6)\\
&=-12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(B\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_B&=m_Bv_B\\
&=3\times(+4)\\
&=+12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Only the chosen positive direction changed. Neither trolley reversed: \(A\)'s momentum still points right and \(B\)'s still points left. Their magnitudes remain \(12\text{ kg m s}^{-1}\); their signed components reverse because we now describe direction using the opposite convention.

## Common exam slips

- Substituting speed without first assigning the velocity's sign.
- Giving a signed momentum without stating the positive direction.
- Using weight instead of mass in \(p=mv\).
- Writing momentum in \(\text{kg}\) or \(\text{m s}^{-1}\) alone; multiply the units to obtain \(\text{kg m s}^{-1}\).
- Treating negative momentum as a negative magnitude.
- Calling momentum vectors equal when only their magnitudes are equal.
- Changing the sign convention during a calculation without redefining every signed quantity.
