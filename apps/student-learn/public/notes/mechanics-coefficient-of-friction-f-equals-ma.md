# Coefficient of Friction — F = ma

An angled pull changes both the horizontal driving force and the normal reaction. Since the friction limit depends on reaction, resolve the angled force before calculating friction.

For a box staying on a horizontal floor, vertical acceleration is zero. An upward component shares the support with the floor; a downward component increases the floor's support.

## Worked example

A 4 kg box starts at rest on a rough horizontal floor. The coefficient of friction is 0.25 and g=10 m/s². Two constant pulls act together: P=9 N horizontally right and Q=8 N at 30° above the horizontal right. Contact is maintained, with no other applied forces. In the ideal model used here, sliding friction equals the limiting value. Find the normal reaction, whether the box moves, its actual friction and acceleration.

1. Resolve Q. H=Q cosθ=8 cos30°=4√3 N right. V=Q sinθ=8 sin30°=4 N up. These components replace Q; do not add Q again.
2. Apply vertical balance. R+V−mg=0, so R+4−4×10=0 and R=36 N. R>0 is consistent with floor contact.
3. Find the limit, then compare. fmax=μR=0.25×36=9 N. Horizontal demand before friction is D=P+H=9+4√3 N, which exceeds 9 N. Friction cannot hold the box at rest, so it slides right. Actual friction is therefore 9 N left.
4. Apply Newton's second law horizontally. Resultant=ma; P+H−f=ma; 9+4√3−9=4a; 4√3=4a; a=√3 m/s² to the right. Constant forces give constant acceleration, so speed increases from rest.

## What changes for a downward push?

Keep the horizontal component the same, but reverse the vertical component. Vertical balance is now R−V−mg=0, so R=mg+V. Reaction and the friction limit increase. Compare the horizontal demand with the new limit again; do not carry over the previous actual friction or acceleration.

## Remember

- Find reaction with an angled pull.
- Decide the actual friction.
- Calculate the acceleration.

At rest, friction adjusts to the required value up to μR. Equality f=μR applies at limiting equilibrium or during sliding in this ideal model; it is not the automatic friction force for every rough surface.
