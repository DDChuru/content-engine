# Scalars and vectors — five-minute revision

## The core distinction

A **scalar** is completely described by its **magnitude**: its size together with a unit. A **vector** needs both **magnitude and direction**.

- “The car travels at \(12\text{ m s}^{-1}\)” gives a speed, so it is scalar.
- “The car travels at \(12\text{ m s}^{-1}\text{ east}\)” gives a velocity, so it is vector.

Direction may be stated in words, by a bearing, or by a sign after a positive direction has been chosen.

## Distance, displacement, speed and velocity

- **Distance** is the total length of the route travelled. It is scalar.
- **Displacement** is the change in position from start to finish, including direction. It is vector.
- **Speed** is the rate at which distance is travelled. It is scalar.
- **Velocity** is the rate of change of displacement. It is vector.

Maya walks \(5\text{ m}\) east, then \(2\text{ m}\) west, taking \(7\text{ s}\) altogether.

\(\text{distance}=5+2=7\text{ m}\)

Taking east as positive,

\(\text{displacement}=5-2=3\text{ m east}\)

Therefore,

\(\text{average speed}=\dfrac{7}{7}=1\text{ m s}^{-1}\)

\(\text{average velocity}=\dfrac{3}{7}\text{ m s}^{-1}\text{ east}\)

The route controls distance; only the start and finish control displacement.

## Acceleration and force are vectors

Acceleration measures how velocity changes:

\(\mathbf{a}=\dfrac{\Delta \mathbf{v}}{\Delta t}\)

Because velocity includes direction, acceleration does too. An object can accelerate by changing speed, direction, or both.

Force also has a direction. Newton’s second law is

\(\mathbf{F}=m\mathbf{a}\)

Weight and momentum are vectors as well:

\(\mathbf{W}=m\mathbf{g}\)

\(\mathbf{p}=m\mathbf{v}\)

## Signs on a straight line

Choose one direction as positive and keep that choice throughout. For example, if right is positive, then leftward displacement, velocity, acceleration and force have negative components.

A negative component does **not** mean a negative magnitude. The velocity \(v=-6\text{ m s}^{-1}\) means a speed of \(6\text{ m s}^{-1}\) in the negative direction.

## Classification table

| Scalars | Vectors |
|---|---|
| speed | velocity |
| distance | displacement |
| mass | acceleration |
| time | force |
| energy | weight |
| — | momentum |

## Worked examples

### Negative velocity

East is positive. A train travels west at \(12\text{ m s}^{-1}\) for \(8\text{ s}\).

\(v=-12\text{ m s}^{-1}\)

\(\Delta x=vt=(-12)(8)=-96\text{ m}\)

So its displacement is \(96\text{ m}\) west. Its speed is \(12\text{ m s}^{-1}\).

### Round trip

A runner goes \(3\text{ m}\) right and then \(3\text{ m}\) left.

\(\text{distance}=3+3=6\text{ m}\)

\(\text{displacement}=3-3=0\text{ m}\)

The runner moved \(6\text{ m}\), but finished where they started.

### Acceleration

A car’s eastward velocity rises from \(4\text{ m s}^{-1}\) to \(16\text{ m s}^{-1}\) in \(6\text{ s}\).

\(a=\dfrac{v-u}{t}=\dfrac{16-4}{6}=2\text{ m s}^{-2}\text{ east}\)

### Resultant force

Right is positive. A \(5\text{ kg}\) box has acceleration \(a=-3\text{ m s}^{-2}\).

\(F=ma=5(-3)=-15\text{ N}\)

The resultant force has magnitude \(15\text{ N}\) and acts left.

## Quick self-check

1. Is energy scalar or vector?
2. A walker goes \(8\text{ m}\) north and \(3\text{ m}\) south. Find distance and displacement.
3. East is positive. Write a westward velocity of \(20\text{ m s}^{-1}\) as a signed component.
4. Velocity changes from \(-4\text{ m s}^{-1}\) to \(8\text{ m s}^{-1}\) in \(3\text{ s}\). Find the acceleration.
5. Is momentum scalar or vector, and why?

### Answers

1. Scalar.
2. Distance \(=8+3=11\text{ m}\); displacement \(=8-3=5\text{ m north}\).
3. \(v=-20\text{ m s}^{-1}\).
4. \(a=\dfrac{8-(-4)}{3}=4\text{ m s}^{-2}\).
5. Vector, because \(\mathbf{p}=m\mathbf{v}\) and it follows the direction of velocity.

## Common exam slips

- Adding a direction to a speed: that makes the quantity velocity.
- Adding journey lengths when displacement is required.
- Using displacement divided by time for average speed; use total distance instead.
- Changing the positive direction partway through a calculation.
- Treating a negative component as a negative magnitude; it indicates the opposite direction.
- Giving a vector magnitude but omitting its direction or sign.
- Assuming every scalar must be positive. Scalars lack direction, but “scalar” does not by itself guarantee a positive value.
- Omitting or mixing units, especially \(\text{m s}^{-1}\), \(\text{m s}^{-2}\), \(\text{kg}\) and \(\text{N}\).
