- Length, time and mass as the three base quantities used in the lesson, with metre, second and kilogram as their units.
- Distance and displacement as length quantities.
- Mass as a scalar; weight as a vector force measured in newtons.

- The exam habit of converting quantities into coherent mechanics units before calculation.

## Accuracy and wording checks

- All five numerical conversions shown are correct.
- The source sometimes uses “fundamental units” when it is naming the quantities length, time and mass. More precisely, these are base quantities; metre, second and kilogram are the corresponding S.I. base units.
- The summary can be read as treating prefixed units such as centimetres, milligrams and nanometres as non-S.I. units. They are S.I. units with prefixes. The more accurate exam instruction is to convert data to a coherent set of units—usually metres, seconds and kilograms—before substituting into a mechanics formula.
- The tonne and minute are not S.I. units, although both are accepted for use with S.I.; converting them to kilograms and seconds is still the safest mechanics workflow.
- The mass-versus-weight distinction and the newton as the unit of weight are correct. A full definition would also state that weight is the gravitational force, commonly calculated using `W = mg`.
