# Storyboard: S.I. Units for Mechanics

Format: 16:9 Remotion explainer. Target duration: about 3 minutes 52 seconds. The visual language is an original “measurement lab”: dark navy space, warm white cards, cyan measurement lines and amber force arrows. Each scene is a separate voiceover sequence whose final duration should be set from the generated ElevenLabs audio. Cue phrases are exact phrases from the narration; the named visual enters when that phrase is spoken.

## S01 — The unit trap

**Duration:** 14 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A clean equation workspace. Three mismatched input cards—kilometres, minutes and grams—drop into a formula machine. The machine jams, then resets to metres, seconds and kilograms. Title resolves: “S.I. Units in Mechanics”.

**Narration:** “A mechanics calculation can be perfectly arranged and still go wrong. The usual culprit is a hidden unit mismatch. Let’s make every quantity speak the same measurement language before we calculate.”

**Cue keywords:** “unit mismatch”; “same measurement language”; “calculate”

## S02 — One shared language

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** Three laboratories on a stylised globe send measurement cards toward one central grid. Differently coloured cards snap to matching S.I. rulers and clocks, and their values become directly comparable.

**Narration:** “S I is the shared system of measurement used in science. It removes ambiguity. A metre means the same thing in every lab, classroom and exam. In mechanics, that common language also keeps the units inside an equation consistent.”

**Cue keywords:** “shared system”; “removes ambiguity”; “metre”; “consistent”

## S03 — Metre: how far

**Duration:** 24 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A horizontal ruler grows across the frame. It measures a short displacement arrow, then stretches into a road. At the cue for one kilometre, the ruler telescopes outward and a counter rolls from one metre to one thousand metres.

**Narration:** “The metre is the base unit of length. Think: metre means how far. Distance, displacement, height and position are all lengths. For a large distance, remember that one kilometre contains one thousand metres.”

**Cue keywords:** “metre”; “how far”; “displacement”; “one kilometre”

## S04 — Second: how long

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A circular clock hand sweeps once while a motion trail crosses a track. A sixty-segment ring builds around the clock, then condenses into a single “1 minute” card paired with “60 seconds”.

**Narration:** “The second is the base unit of time. Think: second means how long. Everyday questions may give minutes, but mechanics formulas usually need seconds. One minute contains sixty seconds.”

**Cue keywords:** “second”; “how long”; “minutes”; “sixty seconds”

## S05 — Kilogram: resistance to change

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** Two identical carts receive the same push. The cart carrying a one-kilogram block accelerates less than the empty cart. A mass ladder then links grams, kilograms and tonnes.

**Narration:** “The kilogram is the base unit of mass. Mass tells us how strongly an object resists a change in motion. One thousand grams make one kilogram. And one tonne is one thousand kilograms.”

**Cue keywords:** “kilogram”; “resists”; “one thousand grams”; “one tonne”

## S06 — Mass versus weight

**Duration:** 28 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** Split screen. On the left, a one-kilogram mass sits on a balance and keeps the same label. On the right, the same mass moves from Earth to the Moon; a downward amber arrow shrinks. A compact formula card for weight equals mass times gravitational acceleration appears.

**Narration:** “Mass and weight are not interchangeable. Mass is a scalar measured in kilograms. Weight is the pull of gravity on that mass. It is a force, so it has direction and is measured in newtons. Move the object to the Moon: its mass stays the same, but its weight becomes smaller.”

**Cue keywords:** “Mass”; “Weight”; “newtons”; “Moon”

## S07 — The conversion ladder

**Duration:** 30 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A vertical decimal ladder with metres and kilograms at the centre. Prefix tiles slide into place: kilo above, then centi, milli and nano below. The decimal point travels left or right while power-of-ten labels pulse. A separate clock lane shows that minutes use groups of sixty.

**Narration:** “Prefixes are scale factors. Kilo means one thousand. Centi means one hundredth. Milli means one thousandth. Nano means one billionth. So choose the target unit, then multiply or divide by the scale factor. Time needs its own rule: minutes convert in groups of sixty, not ten.”

**Cue keywords:** “Kilo”; “Milli”; “Nano”; “groups of sixty”

## S08 — Worked conversion: cyclist speed

**Duration:** 36 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A cyclist travels along a distance bar. The problem card reads “2.4 km in 8 min”. As each number is spoken, that number appears large, then moves into the working line. The distance line converts first, the time line second, and both feed a speed dial that lands on “5 m/s”.

**Narration:** “A cyclist covers two point four kilometres in eight minutes. First, turn kilometres into metres. Two point four times one thousand gives two thousand four hundred metres. Then turn minutes into seconds. Eight times sixty gives four hundred eighty seconds. Speed is distance divided by time. Two thousand four hundred divided by four hundred eighty gives five metres per second.”

**Cue keywords:** “two point four kilometres”; “two thousand four hundred metres”; “four hundred eighty seconds”; “five metres per second”

## S09 — Derived units tell a story

**Duration:** 18 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** Base-unit tiles combine like magnetic blocks. Metres and seconds form a speed readout. A second time tile joins the denominator and the display morphs into an acceleration gauge with evenly spaced velocity arrows.

**Narration:** “Derived units describe relationships. Metres per second means distance gained each second. Metres per second squared means velocity changes by that amount every second. The unit tells you what the quantity is doing.”

**Cue keywords:** “Metres per second”; “Metres per second squared”; “velocity changes”; “unit”

## S10 — Twenty-second recap

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** Three bold cards lock into a triangle: metre for length, second for time, kilogram for mass. A weight arrow appears beside—not on—the mass card. A final conversion checklist ticks through “convert”, “calculate”, “unit”.

**Narration:** “Quick recap. Use metres for length, seconds for time, and kilograms for mass. Mass is not weight; weight is a force in newtons. Convert every value into a coherent set of units, calculate carefully, then check that the final unit matches the quantity. That habit saves marks.”

**Cue keywords:** “metres”; “Mass is not weight”; “Convert”; “saves marks”
