# Storyboard: Types of Force

Format: 16:9 Remotion explainer, approximately 3 minutes 38 seconds. Original “mechanics lab” visual language: dark navy field, warm white instrument cards, cyan construction lines and amber force arrows. Objects are simple geometric lab models, not source images. Each scene is a separate narration sequence; arrow reveals are timed to spoken cue words with frame-driven Remotion animation, and final scene lengths should be conformed to the recorded voiceover.

## S01 — Forces are invisible actors

**Duration:** 12 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A warm white block sits alone on a cyan lab bench. Nothing moves until four faint amber arrows flicker around it like detected signals. The title resolves above a small “force scanner” reticle.

**Narration:** “Forces are invisible, but their effects are everywhere. A pull starts motion. A push changes it. In mechanics, the first skill is knowing which forces are present.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “invisible” — scanner sweeps the block
- “pull” — rightward arrow flashes
- “push” — leftward arrow answers
- “which forces” — title locks in

## S02 — A force needs size and direction

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** One amber arrow expands from a glowing point. A cyan ruler measures its length while a compass ring tracks its direction. A newton unit chip and a particle-model card settle beside it.

**Narration:** “A force is a vector. That means it has a size and a direction. We measure force in newtons. In a particle model, every force is shown as acting at one point.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “vector” — arrowhead forms
- “size” — ruler extends
- “direction” — compass ring rotates
- “one point” — object contracts to a particle dot

## S03 — Weight and reaction

**Duration:** 25.678 seconds (measured MP3)

**Viewer sees:** Return to the block on the bench. An amber weight arrow grows vertically down. The bench glows at the contact, then an amber reaction arrow grows perpendicular to it. The bench tilts briefly and the reaction rotates with the surface while weight stays vertical. Keep “W = mg” above a compact diagram label “g = 10 m s⁻² · Paper 4”, and ring 10 at its spoken cue.

**Narration:** “Weight is gravity pulling the object vertically down. Its size is mass times gravity. For Cambridge Paper Four, use g equals ten metres per second squared. The bench pushes back with a normal reaction. Normal means perpendicular to the surface. Tilt the surface, and the reaction tilts too. Weight still points straight down.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “weight” — downward arrow appears
- “mass times gravity” — compact formula card resolves
- “ten metres” — ring the diagram’s gravity value
- “normal reaction” — contact pulse launches upward arrow
- “tilt” — bench rotates while arrows separate in direction

## S04 — String or rod: tension or thrust?

**Duration:** 24 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Split lab bay. Left: a taut string pulls a block and amber arrows point away from the block along the string. Right: a rigid rod first pulls, then compresses; a central toggle flips from tension to thrust as the force arrows reverse. The string sags when the animation tries to push with it.

**Narration:** “A taut string pulls, so it carries tension. Tension acts along the string, away from the object. A rod can pull or push. Pulling means tension. Pushing means thrust. A string cannot provide thrust. It simply goes slack.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “tension” — string straightens and pull arrow appears
- “rod” — second bay opens
- “thrust” — rod compresses and arrows flip
- “slack” — string droops

## S05 — Friction chooses a direction

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The same sled glides across two horizontal strips labelled by texture alone: glossy ice and granular sand. Its cyan motion trace points right; the amber friction arrow points left and grows much longer on sand. A limiting-friction gauge fills but does not always reach maximum.

**Narration:** “Friction acts along a rough surface. It opposes motion, or the motion that is about to happen. On ice it may be small. On sand it may be larger. Before slipping begins, friction only grows as much as needed, up to its limit.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “opposes” — friction arrow points against the trace
- “ice” — short arrow
- “sand” — longer arrow
- “limit” — gauge stops at its cap

## S06 — Driving, braking and air resistance

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A geometric lab car travels through a cyan wind tunnel. A driving arrow builds forward; air resistance and braking arrows build backward. The car first accelerates, then moves steadily when the forward and backward stacks balance.

**Narration:** “A driving force propels a vehicle. Braking force acts against its motion. Air resistance also acts backwards through the surrounding air. If the forward and backward forces balance, the car can keep a constant velocity even while the engine is working.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “driving” — forward arrow grows
- “braking” — first backward arrow appears
- “air resistance” — wind lines create a second backward arrow
- “balance” — force bars become equal

## S07 — A book on a slope

**Duration:** 28 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A warm white book rests on a cyan incline. Weight drops vertically. Reaction rises at right angles to the slope. A translucent weight rectangle splits into components, revealing a down-slope tendency; friction then appears up the slope and a small balance indicator settles at zero.

**Narration:** “Now place a book on a rough slope. Weight is still vertical. Reaction is perpendicular to the slope. Part of the weight tries to slide the book downhill, so friction acts uphill. If the required friction is below its limiting value, the book remains at rest.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “vertical” — weight drops
- “perpendicular” — right-angle marker draws in
- “downhill” — component glides down the plane
- “friction” — uphill arrow holds the book

## S08 — Build the force diagram

**Duration:** 24 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A five-kilogram block is pulled to the right by a horizontal string on a rough table. The surrounding person and scenery fade away, leaving one block. As each force is spoken, a labelled amber arrow appears: weight down, reaction up, tension along the string, friction left. A red reject stamp removes a force drawn on the person.

**Narration:** “Build one force diagram. Isolate the block. Add its weight, vertically down. Add the reaction, perpendicular to the table. Add tension along the string. The pull tends to move the block right, so friction acts left. Stop there. Include only forces acting on the block.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “isolate” — scene collapses to one block
- “weight” — downward arrow appears
- “reaction” — upward arrow appears
- “tension” — inclined arrow appears, followed by friction

## S09 — From arrows to an equation

**Duration:** 34.273 seconds (measured MP3)

**Viewer sees:** The built diagram docks beside two balance rails. On the vertical rail, weight and reaction cancel. On the horizontal rail, a twenty-two-newton tension bar exceeds a fifteen-newton friction bar. The difference is 7 N and becomes a rightward acceleration trace labelled 1.4 m/s². Lead the working with “R = W = mg”, “Fnet = T − F” and “Fnet = ma” before the substitutions. Keep the block, its givens and the balance bars visible. Ring each numerical source at its word cue, including repeated substitutions.

**Narration:** “The diagram now writes the equations for us. Vertically, reaction balances weight, which is mass times gravity. Five times ten gives fifty newtons. Horizontally, resultant force is tension minus friction. Tension is twenty-two newtons and friction is fifteen newtons. The remaining force is seven newtons to the right. Force equals mass times acceleration. So the five-kilogram block accelerates at one point four metres per second squared.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Vertically” — vertical rail balances
- “Horizontally” — force bars compare
- “mass times gravity” — general weight formula
- “five times”, “ten gives”, “fifty newtons” — weight substitution and source rings
- “resultant force” — general horizontal formula
- “twenty-two newtons”, “fifteen newtons” — tension and friction rings
- “remaining force” — difference bar detaches
- “seven newtons” — net-force result ring
- “Force equals mass times acceleration” — general acceleration formula
- “five-kilogram”, “one point four” — mass and acceleration rings
- “accelerates” — block moves right

## S10 — Twenty-second recap

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Five rapid mechanics-lab callbacks fill a clean grid: weight and reaction, taut string and compressed rod, friction on sand, car force balance, and the isolated four-arrow block. Final card reads “One body. Every force on it.”

**Narration:** “Recap. Forces are vectors measured in newtons. Weight is down. Reaction is perpendicular to the surface. Tension pulls. Thrust pushes. Friction and resistance oppose motion or intended motion. Driving force propels. For every diagram, choose one body, then draw every force acting on it.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Weight” — flash weight and reaction tile
- “Tension” — flip string and rod tile
- “Friction” — flash resistive-force tiles
- “one body” — resolve final card
