# Types of force — five-minute revision

## What a force is

A force is a vector: it needs magnitude and direction. Its unit is the newton, \(\text{N}\). A model often replaces a body by a particle, with every force drawn from one representative point. Arrow direction shows force direction; the label gives its name or size.

Newton’s second law connects the resultant force to acceleration:

\(\mathbf{F}_{\text{resultant}}=m\mathbf{a}\)

## The force family to recognise

### Weight

Weight is the gravitational force on a body:

\(W=mg\)

It acts vertically down. Questions commonly use \(g=9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}\) or a supplied value. Mass is a scalar measured in kilograms; weight is a vector measured in newtons.

### Normal reaction

The normal reaction \(R\) is a surface’s push on an object, perpendicular to the surface. It is vertical only on a horizontal surface. Do not assume \(R=mg\): slopes and other vertical force components change \(R\).

### Tension and thrust

Both act along a connector.

- Tension is a pull. Its arrow points away from the object along the connector.
- Thrust, or compression, is a push. Its arrow points from the connector into the object.
- A light string carries tension, not thrust; if pushed, it goes slack.
- A rod may carry either. Ask whether it pulls or pushes.

The letter \(T\) may be used for either tension or thrust; the direction and physical situation tell you which it is.

### Friction

Friction acts along a rough surface, opposing actual or impending motion. It need not equal its maximum:

\(0\leq F\leq\mu R\)

At limiting equilibrium, and in the usual simplified sliding model, \(F=\mu R\). Before the limit, static friction takes only the value needed for equilibrium.

### Driving, braking and fluid resistance

A driving force propels a vehicle; a braking force opposes its motion. Air and water resistance oppose relative motion through a fluid. A driving force need not cause acceleration: resistance can balance it.

## How to draw a force diagram

1. Isolate one body as a box or point.
2. Draw every force acting **on it** as a labelled arrow.
3. Use the physical directions: weight vertical, reaction perpendicular, friction along the surface, and tension or thrust along the connector.
4. Draw nothing else—no scenery, motion arrows or forces on other bodies.

## Worked examples

### 1. Rough horizontal table and a string

A \(5\text{ kg}\) block is pulled horizontally by a string with tension \(22\text{ N}\). The coefficient of friction is \(0.30\), and \(g=9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}\).

Force diagram in words: on the block, draw \(W\) down, \(R\) up, \(T\) right and \(F\) left. Do not include the block’s force on the table.

There is no vertical acceleration, so

\(R-W=0\)

\(R=mg=5(9.8)=49\text{ N}\)

Limiting friction is

\(\mu R=0.30(49)=14.7\text{ N}\)

Since \(22>14.7\), the block moves. Using the simplified sliding model,

\(F=14.7\text{ N}\)

Horizontally,

\(22-14.7=5a\)

\(a=1.46\text{ m s}^{-2}\)

Acceleration is to the right.

### 2. A book at rest on a rough slope

A \(4\text{ kg}\) book rests on a plane inclined at \(30^\circ\). The coefficient of friction is \(0.70\), and \(g=9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}\).

Resolve weight perpendicular to the plane:

\(R=mg\cos 30^\circ=4(9.8)\cos 30^\circ=33.9\text{ N}\)

The component trying to move the book down the plane is

\(mg\sin 30^\circ=4(9.8)\sin 30^\circ=19.6\text{ N}\)

Equilibrium needs \(19.6\text{ N}\) of friction up the plane. Check the limit:

\(\mu R=0.70(33.9)=23.7\text{ N}\)

Because \(19.6<23.7\), the book can rest. Friction is \(19.6\text{ N}\), not \(23.7\text{ N}\).

### 3. A rod pushing a trolley

A horizontal rod pushes a \(600\text{ kg}\) trolley. Resistance is \(100\text{ N}\), and the trolley accelerates at \(0.50\text{ m s}^{-2}\).

The rod pushes, so its force is thrust. Taking forward as positive,

\(T-100=600(0.50)\)

\(T=400\text{ N}\)

If the rod pulled the trolley, this force would be tension.

### 4. Driving and braking forces

A \(1200\text{ kg}\) car has a driving force of \(2400\text{ N}\). Air and rolling resistance total \(600\text{ N}\), and the brakes add a further backward force of \(900\text{ N}\).

Taking forward as positive,

\(F_{\text{resultant}}=2400-600-900=900\text{ N}\)

\(a=\dfrac{F_{\text{resultant}}}{m}=\dfrac{900}{1200}=0.75\text{ m s}^{-2}\)

The car still accelerates forward because the driving force is larger than the two opposing forces combined.

## Quick self-check

1. A book rests on a horizontal table. Which two forces act on the book, and in which directions?
2. A string runs up and right from a crate. In which direction does the string’s tension act on the crate?
3. A rod between two vehicles is being compressed. Is its force tension or thrust?
4. A rough surface has \(\mu R=18\text{ N}\), but only \(7\text{ N}\) of friction is needed to prevent motion. What is the friction?
5. A car moves at constant speed against \(500\text{ N}\) of resistance. What is its driving force?

### Answers

1. Weight acts vertically down and the normal reaction acts vertically up.
2. Up and right, along the string and away from the crate.
3. Thrust, because the rod is pushing.
4. \(7\text{ N}\). Static friction has not reached its limiting value.
5. \(500\text{ N}\) forward, because the resultant force is zero.

## Common exam slips

- Drawing forces that the chosen body exerts on other objects instead of forces acting on the chosen body.
- Forgetting the normal reaction whenever an object touches a surface.
- Drawing the reaction vertically on a slope instead of perpendicular to the slope.
- Making friction equal to \(\mu R\) before checking whether it is limiting.
- Drawing friction opposite a force arrow rather than opposite actual or impending motion.
- Calling every force in a rod thrust. A rod in a pulling state is in tension; it is in thrust only when it pushes.
- Giving a string a thrust. An ideal string goes slack instead.
- Treating weight and normal reaction as a Newton’s third-law pair. They act on the same body, so they cannot be such a pair.
