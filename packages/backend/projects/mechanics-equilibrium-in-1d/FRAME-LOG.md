## Source-review flags

- At f014 and f100, “overall force” is informal shorthand. For syllabus precision, the resultant is the vector sum of all forces; along one line it is their signed algebraic sum.
- At f072, “equating both sides” is ambiguous wording. The valid step is to equate the total upward force to the total downward force, or equivalently to write the signed resultant as zero. The equation used, \(10+(18+x)=5x\), is correct.
- At f038-f051 and f109-f121, the Newton’s-first-law account correctly allows both rest and constant non-zero velocity. Learners should not infer from the word “equilibrium” alone that a particle must be stationary.
- No \(g\ne10\text{ m s}^{-2}\) use occurs in the supplied video or PDF. The video example contains no mass-to-weight conversion; the PDF explicitly uses \(g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}\) in its equilibrium example, consistent with Cambridge 9709’s normal convention.
