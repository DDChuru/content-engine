# Storyboard: Displacement-Time Graphs

Format: 16:9 Remotion explainer, approximately 3 minutes 36 seconds. Original “mechanics lab” visual language: dark navy field, warm white instrument cards, cyan graph lines and amber direction arrows. All diagrams are newly built geometric assets. Animate from `useCurrentFrame()` and the composition frame rate; draw graphs as frame-driven SVG paths, premount every sequence and avoid CSS animations. Spoken cue words trigger the principal reveals, with final scene lengths conformed to the recorded voiceover.

## S01 - Position leaves a trace

**Duration:** 12 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A warm white particle sits at the origin of a cyan laboratory track. A vertical scanner links it to an empty displacement-time grid. The title resolves as the particle begins to glow.

**Narration:** “A displacement-time graph is a record of position. It shows where a particle is, relative to a fixed origin, as time passes.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “record” - scanner line connects track and graph
- “particle” - particle pulses
- “origin” - zero marker locks onto the track

## S02 - The graph draws with the motion

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The particle slides right along the track while a cyan graph path draws upward in exact sync. A vertical guide joins the moving particle to the growing endpoint. Warm white chips label time horizontally and displacement vertically.

**Narration:** “Time runs across the graph. Displacement runs upward. Move the particle in the positive direction, and the line rises. Every new point matches the particle's position at that instant.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Time” - horizontal axis illuminates
- “Displacement” - vertical axis illuminates
- “positive direction” - amber track arrow points right
- “matches” - guide line snaps to the graph endpoint

## S03 - Gradient is velocity

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The graph and track remain paired. The particle completes one gentle straight climb, then a steeper one. A small laboratory inclinometer measures each graph segment while a velocity readout increases.

**Narration:** “The gradient tells us the velocity. A rising line gives positive velocity. A falling line gives negative velocity. And the steeper the line, the greater the speed.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “gradient” - rise-and-run triangle appears
- “positive” - rising segment glows cyan
- “negative” - segment tilts downward and track arrow flips
- “steeper” - readout climbs

## S04 - Straight, flat, curved

**Duration:** 24 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Three warm white cards enter like lab samples. Card one has a straight sloping line and a particle moving steadily. Card two has a horizontal line and a parked particle. Card three has a curve and a particle whose speed changes.

**Narration:** “A straight sloping line has constant gradient, so velocity is constant. A horizontal line has zero gradient, so the particle is at rest. A curve has a changing gradient, so velocity is changing.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “constant” - first velocity readout freezes
- “zero” - second readout settles at zero
- “curve” - third path bends
- “changing” - third readout rolls through new values

## S05 - Instantaneous velocity

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**Viewer sees:** One curve expands to fill the bench. An amber tangent line pivots around a cyan point as that point travels along the curve. The live gradient readout moves from small positive, to larger positive, then back toward zero. At the chosen instant, the tangent freezes and a rise-and-run triangle appears.

**Narration:** “On a curve, velocity changes from moment to moment. Draw a tangent at the instant you need. Its gradient is the instantaneous velocity. As the tangent pivots, the velocity readout changes with it.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “tangent” - amber line lands on the curve
- “instant” - cyan point pulses
- “gradient” - triangle measures the tangent
- “pivots” - line sweeps through the curve

## S06 - Crossing the origin

**Duration:** 28 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The particle moves left from a positive track position, crosses the glowing origin and continues left. In sync, the graph falls through the time axis into a shaded negative-displacement region. The amber displacement arrow shrinks to zero, then flips direction and grows leftward.

**Narration:** “Now move back toward the origin. The graph falls. At zero displacement, the particle is at the origin. Continue past it, and displacement becomes negative. The arrow flips because the particle is now on the negative side. Distance itself has not become negative.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “toward” - arrow contracts
- “origin” - particle crosses the zero beacon
- “negative” - lower graph region illuminates
- “flips” - amber displacement arrow reverses

## S07 - Worked journey

**Duration:** 30 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A fresh graph and track build together. Numbers appear only as they are spoken: start at two metres; reach ten metres at four seconds; rest until six seconds; finish at minus six metres at ten seconds. The last line crosses the origin. Velocity chips attach to each segment.

**Narration:** “Try one journey. The particle starts at two metres. It reaches ten metres after four seconds, so its velocity is two metres per second. It rests until six seconds. Then it reaches minus six metres at ten seconds. That final velocity is minus four metres per second.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “two metres” - starting value appears
- “four seconds” - first endpoint and gradient triangle appear
- “rests” - horizontal segment draws
- “minus six metres” - final line crosses the axis

## S08 - Distance versus displacement

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The journey continues behind two instrument panels. Left is a displacement readout with a reversible amber arrow. Right is a cyan distance odometer that only counts upward. Segment lengths eight and sixteen enter the odometer; the signed endpoint change appears on the left.

**Narration:** “Displacement compares the finish with the start. Here it is minus eight metres. Distance adds the ground covered on every leg. Eight metres out, then sixteen metres back, gives twenty-four metres in total.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “finish” - endpoint difference highlights
- “minus eight” - signed readout settles
- “every leg” - both graph segments pulse
- “twenty-four” - odometer completes

## S09 - Two different averages

**Duration:** 16 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The two readouts divide by the same ten-second clock. The displacement panel produces negative zero point eight metres per second; the odometer produces two point four metres per second. Direction arrow remains only on the velocity side.

**Narration:** “Divide by ten seconds. Average velocity is minus zero point eight metres per second. Average speed is two point four metres per second. One keeps direction. The other uses total distance.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “ten seconds” - shared clock docks between panels
- “average velocity” - signed result appears
- “average speed” - odometer result appears
- “direction” - velocity arrow pulses

## S10 - Twenty-second recap

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Four fast lab callbacks form a clean grid: particle and self-drawing graph, steep-versus-shallow gradients, pivoting tangent, and odometer beside displacement. The final card reads “Position gives the point. Gradient gives the motion.”

**Narration:** “Recap. A displacement-time graph shows signed position against time. Its gradient is velocity. Straight means constant velocity. Horizontal means rest. A curve needs a tangent for velocity at one instant. Add absolute position changes for distance. Use endpoint change for displacement. Then keep their averages separate.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “gradient” - steep and shallow lines flash
- “tangent” - amber tangent pivots once
- “distance” - odometer tile lights
- “separate” - final two-column lockup resolves
