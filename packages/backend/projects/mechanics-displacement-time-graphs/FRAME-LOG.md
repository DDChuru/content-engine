## Source-review flags

- The arithmetic on the worked journey is consistent: net displacement is `-11 m`, average velocity is `-0.44 m s^-1`, total distance is `35 m`, and average speed is `1.4 m s^-1`.
- The “in front of” and “behind” language at f014 and f042 is not general enough. Positive and negative displacement refer to the chosen positive and negative directions from the origin; a coordinate sign is not inherently front or back.
- The average-velocity wording at f155 and f218 is easy to misread. The numerator should be the final position coordinate minus the initial position coordinate, equivalently the net displacement over the interval—not “final displacement” minus “initial displacement” as two separate journey totals.
- Calling every curved section simply accelerating or decelerating (f126, f131 and f211) needs care. A curve guarantees changing velocity because its gradient changes; whether speed rises or falls also depends on the signs of velocity and acceleration.
- “Distance from origin” and “total distance travelled” are both scalar quantities, but they are not interchangeable. The three side-by-side graphs correctly show the distinction; exam answers should name the intended quantity.
- The supporting PDF says average speed can only be positive. It can also be zero for a particle that remains at rest, so “non-negative” is the precise statement.
