# Teaching standard for explainers (Durai, 2026-09-06)

The outcome is a student UNDERSTANDING the syllabus point. Not a stylish recap, not a "mechanics lab".
Durai's words on the first paced video: "we need clarity and sticking to the syllabus context; the content is
vibey instead of being clear; some things we need to dwell on and explain well; the video appeared too busy."

This standard sits on top of PACING.md (two tempos, holds, hand-written working). Where they conflict, this wins.

## 1. Syllabus is the spine

- Every video opens with the Cambridge 9709 syllabus statement(s) it covers, quoted, on a plain card:
  "Syllabus 4.1: ... " (see SYLLABUS-9709-MECHANICS.md for the exact statements per topic).
- Then one line in the student's language: "By the end you can ..." (the outcome), with at most three bullets.
- Every scene maps to one of those bullets. If a scene doesn't serve a bullet, cut it.
- The recap returns to the same bullets and ticks them. Same words, not new ones.
- Use the syllabus's own vocabulary (particle, light, inextensible, smooth, rough...) exactly as the exam does.

## 2. Plain, not vibey

- No metaphor chrome: no "model bay", "reality scanner", "connector bench", "rig", "console", "lab test".
  Headers say what the idea is: "What a particle model ignores", "Why a light string has one tension".
- No decorative micro-labels (tiny uppercase tags, "channel v", "signals detected"). If a label doesn't teach, delete it.
- Narration is a teacher talking to one student: say the idea, say why, give the example, say what to remember.
- Explain the WHY every time a rule appears ("we ignore its size because every point of it moves the same way").
- Prefer one concrete, ordinary example (a box on a table, a lift, a ball thrown up) over a stylised object.

## 3. Not busy

- Hard cap: ≤ 3 visual regions on screen (e.g. one diagram + one card + one line of working). One header line.
- One new element per cue. Nothing appears that isn't being spoken about at that moment.
- Text on screen ≤ 12 words per card. Working is written by hand, line by line; never a wall of typeset maths.
- Empty space is fine. A diagram alone with the narration is fine. Density is not a feature.
- Colour: one accent for "the thing we're talking about", neutral for everything else. No rainbow of chips.

## 4. Dwell where understanding happens

- Definitions: say it, show it, then show what happens when it is NOT true (the contrast is the understanding).
- Worked examples: state the goal, then one line at a time, pausing after each line to say what it means.
- After the answer: "what would change if..." beat (drop one assumption, change one number).
- Check understanding: one short question to the student with a 3 s hold before the answer.

## 5. Builder checklist (before committing)

- [ ] syllabus card + outcome bullets at the top; recap ticks the same bullets
- [ ] every scene serves a bullet; no lab/rig/console words anywhere in headers, labels or narration
- [ ] ≤ 3 regions and ≤ 12 words per card on every still; one new element per cue
- [ ] two slow scenes per PACING.md, working hand-written, holds after key numbers
- [ ] one "when it is not true" contrast and one "what would change if" beat
- [ ] 3.5–5 min total; fewer scenes are fine
