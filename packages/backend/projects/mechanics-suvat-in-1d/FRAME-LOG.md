## Source-review flags

- **Incorrect substitution (f081):** the bicycle example writes \(v^2=100^2+2(4)(48)\), although the stated and listed initial velocity is \(u=10\text{ ms}^{-1}\). The following value \(v^2=484\) is obtained from the intended \(10^2\), not \(100^2\).
- **Sign-dependent claim (f132):** “Deceleration is negative acceleration” is only true when the chosen positive direction is the direction of motion. In general, deceleration means acceleration opposite to velocity, so its signed value depends on the convention.
- **Terminology after rest (f200, repeated f369):** with the same constant acceleration, the particle reverses direction after reaching rest, but it then speeds up. Calling the acceleration “continued deceleration” beyond the turning instant is misleading.
- **Beyond §4.2 (f205-f215):** the force used to motivate the reversal belongs to Newton’s laws/dynamics in syllabus §4.4. The resulting constant-acceleration kinematics are within §4.2.
- **Required vertical-motion coverage omitted:** the recording contains no vertical-motion example and never uses \(g\). Cambridge 9709 §4.2 work for this task must include vertical motion with \(g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}\); the accompanying PDF does cover this convention, and the independent notes below supply a clean example.
- **Unit typography throughout:** the recording writes forms such as “ms⁻¹” and “ms⁻²”. Standard SI presentation is \(\text{m s}^{-1}\) and \(\text{m s}^{-2}\).
- **No \(g\ne10\) calculation occurs:** the recording does not use gravity at all. The accompanying PDF mentions the measured mean \(9.80665\text{ m s}^{-2}\) only as context and explicitly directs students to use \(g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}\) unless told otherwise.
