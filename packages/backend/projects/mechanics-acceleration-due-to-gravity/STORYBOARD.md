### s02 — Gravity keeps acting downwards

tempo: brisk; speed: 1.0; source: f008–f051; f061–f078

- **weight**: Weight is gravitational force. The symbol g is acceleration.
- **gravity**: Cambridge 9709 Paper 4 uses g equals ten metres per second squared. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **sign**: Upward is positive throughout, so acceleration is minus ten metres per second squared. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **top**: At the highest point, zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration. Hold 2 s after pen finish.

### s03 — A stone dropped from rest

tempo: slow; speed: 0.9; source: f150–f179; requested dropped variant of 15 m diagram

- **story**: Consider a stone released from rest. It falls faster and faster. Find its velocity before impact.
- **height**: It falls fifteen metres: displacement minus fifteen metres.
- **initial**: Initial velocity is zero; acceleration, minus ten. Ignore air resistance. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **formula**: Let us calculate. Final velocity squared equals initial velocity squared plus twice acceleration times displacement. Write `v² = u² + 2as`. Hold 1 s after pen finish.
- **substitute**: Zero squared, plus two times minus ten, times minus fifteen. Write `v² = 0² + 2(−10)(−15)`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **square**: Velocity squared is three hundred. Which sign for velocity? Write `v² = 300`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **result**: Minus seventeen point three metres per second. Negative means downward velocity before impact. Write `v = −17.3 m s⁻¹`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **change**: Thrown down instead? Initial velocity would be negative.

### s04 — Returning to the launch height

tempo: brisk; speed: 1.0; source: f057–f147

- **story**: Picture a ball thrown up, turning, and returning.
- **initial**: Launch velocity: twelve metres per second upward.
- **graph**: Now the velocity time graph: constant acceleration, straight line.
- **top**: At the top, velocity is zero; then negative. Up remains positive.
- **return**: Returning velocity: minus twelve metres per second. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **equal**: Areas cancel. Time up equals time down only when returning to launch height, without air resistance. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **lower**: Landing lower takes longer. The cliff is not symmetric.

### s05 — A stone thrown from a cliff

tempo: brisk; speed: 1.0; source: f180–f210

- **story**: Consider a stone thrown up from a cliff. It rises, returns past the edge, then hits the sea. Find its total flight time.
- **initial**: It leaves at eight metres per second upward.
- **height**: The cliff is twenty metres high: displacement to sea, minus twenty metres.
- **acceleration**: Freely under gravity, acceleration is minus ten; upward positive. Hold 2 s after pen finish.

### s06 — Time until the stone reaches the sea

tempo: slow; speed: 0.9; source: f211–f234

- **setup**: Find the unknown time. Start with the displacement equation.
- **formula**: Displacement equals initial velocity times time, plus half acceleration times time squared. Write `s = ut + ½at²`. Hold 1 s after pen finish.
- **substitute**: Minus twenty equals eight t, plus half times minus ten t squared. Write `−20 = 8t + ½(−10)t²`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **rearrange**: Rearrange: five t squared minus eight t minus twenty equals zero. Write `5t² − 8t − 20 = 0`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **roots**: Solving this quadratic gives minus one point three five four, or two point nine five four seconds. Write `t = −1.354... or 2.954...`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **positive**: Reject the negative root: before projection. Time must be positive. Write `t > 0`.
- **result**: The total time of flight is two point nine five seconds, to three significant figures. Write `t = 2.95 s`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **meaning**: This includes the extra fall below the cliff edge.

### s07 — Maximum height above the sea

tempo: slow; speed: 0.9; source: f235–f270

- **setup**: Find maximum height above sea. First, the rise above the cliff.
- **top**: At the top, velocity is zero. Initial velocity eight; acceleration minus ten. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **formula**: Final velocity squared equals initial velocity squared plus twice acceleration times displacement. Write `v² = u² + 2as`. Hold 1 s after pen finish.
- **substitute**: Zero squared equals eight squared plus two times minus ten times s. Write `0² = 8² + 2(−10)s`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **simplify**: So zero equals sixty four minus twenty s. Write `0 = 64 − 20s`. Hold 1 s after pen finish.
- **rise**: The rise is three point two metres above the launch point. Write `s = 3.2 m`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **height-formula**: Height above sea equals cliff height plus rise. Write `h = cliff height + rise`. Hold 1 s after pen finish.
- **result**: Twenty plus three point two gives twenty three point two metres above the sea. Write `h = 20 + 3.2 = 23.2 m`. Hold 2 s after pen finish.

### s08 — Check the highest point

tempo: brisk; speed: 1.0; source: f076–f108; f271–f308

- **question**: At the highest point, is the acceleration zero? Hold 3 s after pen finish.
- **answer**: No. Velocity is zero, but acceleration is still minus ten metres per second squared. Hold 2 s after pen finish.
- **outcome1**: You can distinguish weight from acceleration.
- **outcome2**: Keep one positive direction.
- **outcome3**: And solve vertical motion problems.
