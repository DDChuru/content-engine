## Source-review flags

- At f014-f051, the Earth-diagram banner repeatedly displays \(g\approx9.8\text{ ms}^{-2}\). This is a reasonable real-world approximation, but every production derived from these sources must replace it with the Cambridge 9709 Paper 4 convention \(g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}\). No displayed value of exactly \(9.81\) was found in the 316 frames.
- At f023-f051, the slide also displays the more precise average \(g\approx9.80665\text{ ms}^{-2}\). The supplied PDF repeats \(9.80665\) on page 10. Retain it only as optional real-world context; do not use it in 9709 calculations, where \(g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}\) unless a question states otherwise.
- Throughout the recording, the units are typeset as “ms⁻¹” and “ms⁻²”. Standard 9709 notation should separate the units as \(\text{m s}^{-1}\) and \(\text{m s}^{-2}\), since “ms” otherwise denotes milliseconds.
- At f008-f051, “Gravity is a force” is followed by the useful distinction that \(g\) is not a force. For precision, describe the gravitational force on a particle as its weight and describe \(g\) as the acceleration due to gravity.
- At f132-f143, the upward-positive SUVAT list writes \(v=\text{“speed of impact”}\). Because \(v\) is a signed velocity and the particle is moving downwards, \(v\) should be negative; the non-negative speed of impact is \(|v|\). The corresponding downward-positive case at f167-f171 does not have this sign conflict.
- At f103-f121, “\(t=2\times\text{‘max height’ time}\)” is valid only for return to the launch height under constant gravity with no resistance. It should not be presented as a general time-of-flight rule for landing at a different height.
