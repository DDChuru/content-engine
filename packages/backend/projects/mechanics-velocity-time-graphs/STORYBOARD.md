# Storyboard: Velocity-Time Graphs

Format: original 16:9 Remotion explainer, approximately 3 minutes 46 seconds. Visual language: a dark graphite “mechanics lab” with an ivory plotting table, cyan instrument lines, amber measurement marks, teal positive-area tiles and coral negative-area tiles. A simple geometric car, road rig and analogue-digital speedometer are built from original shapes. No source branding, wording or imagery is reused. All motion is frame-driven and deterministic; graph drawing, car position, speedometer needle and readouts share the same progress value.

## S01 - Put motion on the bench

**Duration:** 14 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A small lab car rolls onto a marked road strip. Above it, an empty plotting screen powers up. A speedometer needle wakes, and the title is stamped onto an instrument plate.

**Narration:** “A journey can look complicated on the road. On a velocity-time graph, the same journey becomes a line we can measure. Let’s learn to read that line.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “journey” - car enters the lab
- “graph” - plotting screen powers up
- “measure” - grid and crosshair lock in

## S02 - Road, speedometer, graph

**Duration:** 24 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The car drives from left to right while the speedometer and graph draw in sync. Velocity is on the vertical axis; time is on the horizontal. The car reverses briefly as the plotted line drops below the axis.

**Narration:** “Time runs across the bottom. Velocity runs up the side. Above zero, the car moves in our chosen positive direction. Below zero, it moves the other way. The speedometer and the graph tell the same story.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Time” - horizontal axis scans on
- “Velocity” - vertical axis rises
- “Above zero” - car moves right
- “Below zero” - car reverses and line crosses the axis

## S03 - Read the line before calculating

**Duration:** 21 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Four specimen cards slot into a lab rack: horizontal line, rising straight line, falling straight line, curved line. A tiny car icon demonstrates steady motion, changing velocity and variable acceleration on each card.

**Narration:** “A flat line means constant velocity. A straight slope means constant acceleration. Rising and falling slopes give opposite acceleration signs. A curve means the acceleration itself is changing.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “flat” - horizontal specimen illuminates
- “straight slope” - ruler clicks onto a line
- “opposite signs” - plus and minus lamps alternate
- “curve” - rigid ruler flexes into a spline

## S04 - Measure the gradient

**Duration:** 25 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A graph segment rises from four metres per second to sixteen metres per second in six seconds. A bright gradient triangle slides along the segment. Its rise and run arms telescope open; the acceleration readout counts to two metres per second squared.

**Narration:** “Acceleration is the gradient. Here, velocity rises from four to sixteen metres per second. That is a rise of twelve over a run of six seconds. Twelve divided by six gives an acceleration of two metres per second squared.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “gradient” - triangle docks to the slope
- “twelve” - vertical arm extends
- “six seconds” - horizontal arm extends
- “two” - acceleration display settles

## S05 - Signed area is displacement

**Duration:** 24 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Teal square tiles fill the region beneath a positive graph segment. With every tile, a displacement odometer climbs and the car advances. The line then crosses zero; coral tiles fill below the axis while the displacement readout falls and a separate distance counter keeps rising.

**Narration:** “Area between the line and the time axis gives displacement. Area above zero is positive. Area below zero is negative. The negative tiles reduce displacement, but they still add to total distance.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Area” - teal tiles begin filling
- “displacement” - signed odometer climbs
- “below zero” - coral tiles fill downward
- “distance” - second counter continues upward

## S06 - Build a journey from shapes

**Duration:** 31 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A complete journey forms a trapezium: accelerate from rest to twelve metres per second in four seconds, hold that velocity for five seconds, then stop in three seconds. As each number is spoken it appears beside the matching edge. The shape separates into a central rectangle and two side triangles, mounted like parts on an exploded technical drawing.

**Narration:** “Now find the distance for this journey. The car reaches twelve metres per second in four seconds. It holds that velocity for five seconds, then stops in three. Split the trapezium into two triangles and a rectangle. Their areas are twenty-four, sixty and eighteen metres. The total distance is one hundred and two metres.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “twelve” - height label appears
- “four, five, three” - time labels appear in sequence
- “split” - trapezium explodes into three shapes
- “one hundred and two” - pieces snap into the total counter

## S07 - Direction changes the averages

**Duration:** 28 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A positive triangle is tagged twenty-four metres; a smaller coral triangle below the axis is tagged negative eight metres. A balance rail computes signed displacement on one side and unsigned distance on the other. The car finishes to the right of its start despite reversing once.

**Narration:** “Suppose the forward area is twenty-four metres and the backward area has magnitude eight. Displacement is sixteen metres. Distance is thirty-two. Over ten seconds, average velocity is one point six metres per second. Average speed is three point two.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “forward area” - teal region weighs in
- “backward area” - coral region weighs in
- “Displacement” - signed rail shows sixteen
- “average speed” - unsigned rail shows three point two

## S08 - The suvat bridge

**Duration:** 23 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A straight graph segment is projected onto a second lab display. Its gradient becomes an acceleration chip; its trapezium area becomes a displacement chip. Those chips slide into a compact suvat console. At a graph corner, the console splits the journey into two trays.

**Narration:** “A straight sloping section has constant acceleration, so suvat applies. The velocity change gives the same acceleration as the gradient. The suvat displacement matches the trapezium area. At a corner, start a new section.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “constant acceleration” - gradient chip ejects
- “suvat” - equation console opens
- “displacement” - area chip matches the result
- “corner” - journey splits into trays

## S09 - Three exam checks

**Duration:** 16 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Three inspection lamps test a candidate answer: graph type, area sign and gradient unit. A red warning briefly catches a displacement-time graph being misread as velocity-time, then all lamps turn green.

**Narration:** “Before you finish, run three checks. Is this really a velocity-time graph? Did you keep below-axis area negative for displacement? Is acceleration in metres per second squared?”

**Cue keywords:**

- “velocity-time” - graph-type lamp
- “negative” - sign lamp
- “squared” - unit lamp

## S10 - Twenty-second recap

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The lab bench resolves into a clean dashboard: flat line, gradient triangle, teal and coral area tiles, axis crossing and two average readouts. The car completes one final synced run and parks on the zero line.

**Narration:** “Recap. Height tells velocity. Gradient tells acceleration. Signed area tells displacement. Absolute area tells distance. A flat line means constant velocity. Crossing zero changes direction. Divide displacement by time for average velocity, and distance by time for average speed.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Gradient” - slope triangle flashes
- “Signed area” - teal and coral tiles pulse
- “Crossing zero” - car reverses once
- “average speed” - final dashboard locks
