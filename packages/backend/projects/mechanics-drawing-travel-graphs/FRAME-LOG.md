## Source-review flags

- The cyclist calculation is internally consistent: the key times are \(10,22,27,31,53\text{ s}\); the signed areas are \(+60,+36,0,-8,-88\text{ m}\), giving final displacement \(0\text{ m}\).
- “Acceleration is a curved line” on a displacement-time graph is imprecise. A curve means velocity is changing; constant acceleration produces a parabolic section, and the concavity indicates the acceleration sign. Both the forward deceleration and the reverse acceleration in the worked journey are curved.
- The tip about “uniformly” should be read in context. It indicates a constant rate: “uniformly decelerates” means constant deceleration, while “uniformly accelerates” means constant acceleration.
- “Area under the curve” is convenient shorthand, but signed area between the velocity-time graph and the time axis is safer. Below-axis area is negative displacement.
- The supporting PDF’s claim that average speed can only be positive overlooks the stationary case; average speed is non-negative and may equal \(0\).
- The PDF cross-check has \(12\) rendered pages, despite one file-identification utility reporting \(8\). All \(12\) pages opened correctly; the relevant drawing guidance on pages \(10\)-\(12\) agrees on axes, units, key points, gradients and velocity-time area.
