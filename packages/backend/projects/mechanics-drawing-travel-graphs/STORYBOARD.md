# Storyboard: Drawing Travel Graphs

Format: original 16:9 Remotion explainer, approximately **4 minutes 10 seconds (250 seconds)** across eight scenes. Content source: this project's `NOTES.md`; pacing standard: `../mechanics-syllabus-map/PACING.md`. All narration, illustrations and layouts are original; do not reuse source-recording wording, branding, audio or imagery.

Keep the “mechanics lab”: dark graphite surroundings, an ivory plotting bench, cyan axes, amber time gates, teal positive motion and coral negative motion. Use geometric vehicles, lift hardware and instrument traces. Paired displacement-time and velocity-time graphs share one horizontal time scale.

**Timing contract:** Durations include narration, thinking pauses, reading holds and transitions. Cue times are local and aligned to the quoted spoken keyword. Brisk scenes retain short statements and a cue every 3–5 seconds. Each slow scene has three cue keywords, separated by at least eight seconds. Between them, draw or inspect one element at a time; these are not additional keyword reveals. Each question's ellipsis reserves a one-second thinking pause. Bracketed directions are silent, not TTS text. Say each key result, explain its meaning, then hold for two seconds (60 frames), with an emphasis ring on the value and nothing new appearing. The pen, playhead, graph, lift/ball and panels all freeze during the hold; the ring is in place at its start. Previously established givens remain readable, rather than being reanimated as results.

## S01 — Put the journey on the bench: the recipe

**Duration:** 15 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** A route card and stopwatch dock beside two plotting panels. A positive-direction arrow pins above the bench. Phase durations accumulate into shared amber time gates. Axis plates identify `t / s` horizontally and `s / m` or `v / (m s⁻¹)` vertically; endpoint pins wait for values.

**Narration:** “A travel graph turns a journey into shapes. Choose positive. Split the story into phases and add their durations to mark key times. Choose each phase's shape, label axes and units, then mark key values and sign changes.”

**Cue keywords:**

- 01 s — “journey” — route card unfolds
- 04 s — “positive” — direction arrow clamps in
- 09 s — “key times” — cumulative time gates stamp both axes
- 12 s — “axes and units” — axis plates lock in

## S02 — Words become segments

**Duration:** 20 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** The original scenario card reads: “Moves away steadily for 4 s. Slows uniformly to rest in 2 s. Waits for 3 s. Returns at constant speed.” Build a positive horizontal velocity segment, a straight fall to zero, a zero segment, then a negative horizontal return. Mark cumulative gates `0, 4, 6, 9` seconds; the return endpoint remains symbolic because its speed is unspecified. Keep away from the start positive.

**Narration:** “Read the verbs as drawing instructions. Steadily gives a horizontal velocity line. Uniformly slows gives a straight slope to zero. Waits sits on the axis. Returns belongs below zero, because away is positive. Add durations; the phase times are cumulative.”

**Cue keywords:**

- 03 s — “Steadily” — positive segment grows
- 07 s — “slows” — straight segment descends to zero
- 11 s — “Waits” — zero rail glows
- 15 s — “Returns” — coral segment draws below the axis

## S03 — The gradient-area bridge

**Duration:** 20 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** Displacement-time sits left, velocity-time right. A slope triangle carries “gradient → velocity” across a bridge; signed area tiles carry “area → displacement change” back. A shared playhead compares velocity height with displacement gradient at the same instant. Above-axis tiles add to displacement; below-axis tiles subtract from it, starting from the initial displacement.

At the last cue, settle on a lift reference card for S04: “Upwards positive; starts from rest; acceleration 1.5 m s⁻² for 2 s; constant velocity for 4 s; slows uniformly to rest in 2 s.” Keep these givens visible through S04. They are reference inputs, not calculated readouts.

**Narration:** “Displacement-time gradient gives velocity; steeper means faster. Velocity-time height must match that gradient at the same instant. Going back, accumulate signed areas from the starting displacement. Positive area adds; negative area subtracts. Now use that bridge to build both graphs for this lift.”

**Cue keywords:**

- 01 s — “gradient” — slope triangle traces its measurement
- 06 s — “same instant” — shared playhead aligns the two graphs
- 11 s — “signed areas” — positive and negative tiles cross the bridge
- 16 s — “bridge” — lift reference card settles beside blank axes

## S04 — Worked build: lift in a shaft

**Duration:** 75 seconds
tempo: slow
voiceSpeed: 0.9

**Viewer sees:** Keep a single layout: a geometric lift and stacked compact graphs on the left, with an ivory **lined-paper panel beside the diagram** on the right. Retain S03's printed givens. Every equation below is **handwritten strokes appearing line by line at pen pace**, in the ink-tutor style: a hand/pen tip follows the active stroke, lifts between symbols and lines, and leaves completed working visible. No typeset working pops in. Finish each calculation before drawing its corresponding graph segment; do not build both graphs simultaneously.

| Local time | Narration, including silent pause directions | Working, graph consequence and mandatory holds |
|---|---|---|
| 00–12 s | “What speed follows the acceleration? ... Three metres per second. That is the first velocity corner's height. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `v = 0 + 1.5 × 2 = 3 m s⁻¹`. Draw the straight velocity rise to `(2, 3)`. Ring `3 m s⁻¹`; freeze 10–12 s. |
| 12–22 s | “When does the cruise end? ... Six seconds. That is elapsed time from the start. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `t = 2 + 4 = 6 s`; extend the horizontal velocity segment to `(6, 3)`. Ring `6 s`; freeze 20–22 s. |
| 22–30 s | “When does the lift stop? ... Eight seconds. It is now at rest. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `t = 6 + 2 = 8 s`; draw the straight velocity fall to `(8, 0)`. Ring `8 s` and its linked zero-velocity marker; freeze 28–30 s. |
| 30–39 s | “What does the first triangle give? ... Three metres. This is the first rise. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `s₁ = ½ × 2 × 3 = 3 m`; shade the first triangle and draw displacement concave up to `(2, 3)`. Ring `3 m`; freeze 37–39 s. |
| 39–47 s | “What does the rectangle add? ... Twelve metres. This is the cruise's rise. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `s₂ = 4 × 3 = 12 m`; shade the rectangle. Ring `12 m`; freeze 45–47 s. |
| 47–55 s | “Where are we now? ... Fifteen metres. This is the accumulated height. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `s(6) = 3 + 12 = 15 m`; draw the straight displacement segment to `(6, 15)`. Ring `15 m`; freeze 53–55 s. |
| 55–64 s | “What does braking add? ... Three metres. The lift still moves upwards. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `s₃ = ½ × 2 × 3 = 3 m`; shade the final positive triangle. Ring this separate `3 m`; freeze 62–64 s. |
| 64–75 s | “So... where does it finish? ... Eighteen metres. That is above its starting floor. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `s = 3 + 12 + 3 = 18 m`; complete the concave-down displacement curve to `(8, 18)`, with a horizontal final tangent. Ring `18 m`; freeze 72–74 s and leave the finished page visible to the cut. |

**Cue keywords:**

- 01 s — “speed” — pen begins the velocity line on the paper
- 31 s — “triangle” — pen moves to the first area line
- 65 s — “finish” — pen moves to the total-displacement line

**Reading rhythm:** Number holds are quiet, not filled with another sentence. Each plotted key coordinate inherits its already explained time and value; do not reveal an extra numeric readout during a hold. Keep the displacement graph's changing gradient visible: increasing during acceleration, constant during the cruise, and decreasing to zero during braking. The velocity stays positive until the stop, so all three areas increase displacement.

## S05 — One journey, both graphs

**Duration:** 20 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** Return to S02's out-wait-return scenario. Its displacement graph rises straight, curves concave down to a horizontal tangent, stays level, then returns along a straight falling line. A vertical probe samples velocity while a tangent ruler docks to displacement. Coral return area drains the displacement to zero without making displacement negative. At the idealised switch from waiting to constant return velocity, show a dashed boundary marker on velocity: an instantaneous change is the scenario's simplification, not a finite sloping segment with invented duration. Displacement stays continuous.

**Narration:** “Positive velocity makes displacement rise. Zero velocity makes it level. Negative velocity makes it fall. On the return, velocity is negative, but displacement remains positive until the traveller reaches the start. Keep the whole return leg; arriving back does not erase it.”

**Cue keywords:**

- 01 s — “Positive velocity” — rising tangent illuminates
- 05 s — “Zero velocity” — level segment and zero rail align
- 09 s — “Negative velocity” — falling tangent and coral area link
- 14 s — “reaches the start” — displacement arrives at zero

## S06 — Why it matters: the ball and the examiner's sign check

**Duration:** 65 seconds
tempo: slow
voiceSpeed: 0.9

**Viewer sees:** A ball, vertical laser scale, paired graphs and a small lined-paper working panel remain in one fixed arrangement. State upwards positive. The reference card gives launch speed `14.7 m s⁻¹` and constant gravity `9.8 m s⁻²`, with no air resistance. Start with blank graph traces, labelled axes and the existing positive arrow. Reveal working as handwritten strokes, one line at a time. An examiner inspection strip is present from the opening, with spaces for “tangent”, “signed area” and “continuity”; illuminate existing words only at the three keyword cues.

| Local time | Narration, including silent pause directions | Working, explanation and holds |
|---|---|---|
| 00–13 s | “Why is the velocity line straight? ... With upwards positive, constant gravity makes velocity decrease uniformly. Its gradient is constant.” | Handwrite `v = 14.7 − 9.8t`; draw a straight descending trace from the given launch velocity. This is a line on velocity-time, not a straight displacement segment. Keep reference input labels static. |
| 13–25 s | “Where does direction change? ... One point five seconds. Velocity is zero at the highest point. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `0 = 14.7 − 9.8t`, then `t = 1.5 s`. Mark the zero crossing and the matching horizontal displacement tangent, without revealing a height yet. Ring the linked `1.5 s` and `v = 0` markers; freeze 23–25 s. |
| 25–39 s | “How high is that? ... Eleven point zero two five metres. The positive triangular area gives the maximum rise. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `h = ½ × 1.5 × 14.7 = 11.025 m`. Shade the positive triangle; draw the concave-down displacement rise to `(1.5, 11.025)`. Ring `11.025 m`; freeze 37–39 s. |
| 39–49 s | “When is it caught? ... Three seconds. Equal signed areas return it to hand height. [hold 2 s]” | Extend the line below zero to make an equal negative triangle. Handwrite `t = 2 × 1.5 = 3 s`; finish the displacement arch at `(3, 0)`. Ring `3 s` and the linked zero-displacement endpoint; freeze 47–49 s. Do not label the new negative velocity value yet. |
| 49–59 s | “What velocity is that? ... Minus fourteen point seven metres per second. The ball is moving downwards. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `v(3) = −14.7 m s⁻¹` and label the velocity endpoint `(3, −14.7)`. Ring `−14.7`; freeze 57–59 s. |
| 59–65 s | “So... what matters? ... Show turning, negative velocity and continuous displacement.” | Trace those existing features without adding a new card or numerical result. Keep the full concave-down displacement arch and both signed areas visible to the cut. |

**Cue keywords:**

- 01 s — “straight” — trace the velocity line's direction
- 15 s — “direction change” — illuminate the existing tangent inspection word
- 43 s — “signed areas” — illuminate the area inspection word and outline the two existing regions

**What the examiner wants:** A zero velocity at the top means a horizontal displacement tangent, not a missing descent or a jump in position. The velocity graph goes below zero; the displacement graph comes back to zero. The same constant negative acceleration slows the rising ball and speeds the falling ball: “decelerating” describes decreasing speed, not a universal sign for acceleration. Show this with a tangent ruler that flattens on ascent and steepens downward on descent, outside the numerical holds. Normally velocity also joins continuously, as it does here; the idealised jump in S05 is explicitly part of that different scenario.

## S07 — Sketch versus accurate plot: final inspection

**Duration:** 15 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** A loose sketch and a measured graph-paper plot sit under inspection lamps. Both receive checks for axis labels, units, key times and values, phase order, sign and straight/curved shape. The accurate plot also gets calculated coordinate pins and consistent scales; draw smooth displacement curves through the appropriate endpoints. Highlight gradient-triangle dimensions and area widths/heights. A missing negative return region is restored on a small faulty sketch.

**Narration:** “A sketch needs correct shapes, signs and phase order. An accurate plot also needs calculated coordinates and consistent scales. Check both for labelled axes, units, key times and values. Show the negative region whenever motion reverses.”
