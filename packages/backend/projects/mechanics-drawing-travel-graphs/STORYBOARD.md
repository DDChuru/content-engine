# Storyboard: Drawing Travel Graphs

Format: original \(16{:}9\) Remotion explainer, approximately \(3\) minutes \(40\) seconds. Visual language: a dark graphite “mechanics lab” with an ivory plotting bench, cyan axes, amber time gates, teal positive motion and coral negative motion. Journeys are represented by original geometric vehicles, lift hardware and instrument traces. No source wording, branding, audio or imagery is reused.

## S01 - Put the journey on the bench

**Duration:** 12 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A route card, stopwatch and two blank plotting panels slide onto a lab bench. The title locks between the panels while a cyan scan line links the real journey to its graphs.

**Narration:** “A travel graph is a journey translated into shapes. The safest way to draw one is to split the story into phases, then make every line agree with the motion.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “journey” - route card unfolds
- “phases” - time gates divide the bench
- “agree” - both plotting panels power on

## S02 - The drawing recipe

**Duration:** 23 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A six-position rotary instrument advances through direction, key times, phase shape, axes and units, key values, and sign checks. A small plus arrow pins the chosen direction above the bench.

**Narration:** “First choose a positive direction. Add the phase durations to mark every key time. Decide whether each section is straight, horizontal or curved. Then label both axes with units, add the important values, and mark where velocity is zero or changes sign.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “positive direction” - plus arrow clamps in
- “key time” - amber gates stamp the time line
- “shape” - ruler, level and curve templates appear
- “changes sign” - a marker crosses the zero rail

## S03 - Words become segments

**Duration:** 28 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A worded scenario card reads: “Moves away steadily for \(4\text{ s}\). Slows uniformly to rest in \(2\text{ s}\). Waits for \(3\text{ s}\). Returns at constant speed.” Each phrase lights up in turn and physically spawns its velocity-time segment: positive horizontal, straight fall to zero, zero horizontal, then negative horizontal. Matching time labels land below the joins.

**Narration:** “Read the verbs as drawing instructions. ‘Steadily’ makes a horizontal velocity line. ‘Uniformly slows’ makes a straight slope to zero. ‘Waits’ sits on the axis. ‘Returns’ belongs below zero, because we chose away from the start as positive.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “steadily” - positive segment grows
- “slows” - ruler draws to zero
- “waits” - zero rail glows
- “returns” - coral segment appears below

## S04 - The gradient-area bridge

**Duration:** 26 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The same journey is shown with \(s\)-\(t\) on the left and \(v\)-\(t\) on the right. An animated bridge between them carries a slope triangle from the left labelled “gradient \(\rightarrow v\)” and signed area tiles from the right labelled “area \(\rightarrow\Delta s\)”. A shared playhead proves that the graphs describe the same instant.

**Narration:** “These graphs are two views of one motion. The gradient of displacement gives velocity. Going back the other way, signed area under velocity gives the change in displacement. At every time gate, the height on the right must match the slope on the left.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “gradient” - slope triangle crosses the bridge
- “signed area” - teal and coral tiles return
- “same instant” - shared playhead snaps into alignment

## S05 - Worked build: lift in a shaft

**Duration:** 31 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A lift moves beside two compact graphs. As the narration names each phase, the graph is built segment by segment: straight velocity rise from \((0,0)\) to \((2,3)\), horizontal to \((6,3)\), then straight fall to \((8,0)\). The displacement graph simultaneously curves to \((2,3)\), rises straight to \((6,15)\), and curves to \((8,18)\). Area tiles count \(3+12+3=18\text{ m}\).

**Narration:** “Here is a three-phase lift journey. It accelerates upwards for two seconds and reaches three metres per second. It holds that velocity for four seconds, then slows to rest in two. So the velocity graph rises, stays level, and falls. Its areas are three, twelve and three metres. The lift finishes eighteen metres above its start.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “accelerates” - lift and first segments rise together
- “holds” - middle segments extend
- “slows” - final curve and slope settle flat
- “eighteen” - floor readout locks

## S06 - One journey, both graphs

**Duration:** 26 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The out-wait-return scenario from S03 returns in full. At each time gate, a vertical probe samples velocity on the right while a tangent ruler docks to the left. The return region fills coral; its magnitude drains the displacement readout back to zero without sending the displacement graph below zero.

**Narration:** “Now check the whole journey both ways. Positive velocity makes displacement rise. Zero velocity makes it level. Negative velocity makes it fall. Notice the distinction: the velocity graph goes negative on the return, but the displacement stays positive until the traveller actually reaches the start.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “rise” - positive tangent illuminates
- “level” - both zero indicators align
- “negative velocity” - coral region fills
- “reaches the start” - displacement lands on zero

## S07 - Ball: the sign change matters

**Duration:** 25 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A ball launches through a vertical laser scale. Beside it, a straight velocity trace falls from \(14.7\) through zero at \(1.5\text{ s}\) to \(-14.7\) at \(3\text{ s}\). The linked displacement trace is a concave-down arch peaking at \(11.025\text{ m}\). Equal positive and negative area triangles balance on the bridge.

**Narration:** “For a thrown ball, choose upwards as positive. Gravity makes velocity fall in a straight line. At one point five seconds the line crosses zero: the ball is momentarily at its highest point. Then velocity is negative as it falls. Equal signed areas bring the displacement back to zero when it is caught.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “upwards” - positive arrow climbs the scale
- “crosses zero” - ball freezes at the top
- “negative” - trace turns coral
- “caught” - area balance returns to zero

## S08 - Sketch versus accurate plot

**Duration:** 16 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A loose sketch and a graph-paper plot sit under inspection lamps. The sketch passes shape, order and sign tests without a scale; the accurate plot receives coordinates, evenly spaced scales and a smooth curve through fixed endpoints.

**Narration:** “A sketch does not need a measured scale, but its shapes, signs and phase order must be right. An accurate plot needs calculated coordinates and consistent scales. Both need clear labels.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “sketch” - freehand trace passes three lamps
- “accurate” - coordinate pins click onto a grid
- “labels” - axis plates slide into place

## S09 - Examiner inspection

**Duration:** 13 seconds

**Viewer sees:** An examiner’s checklist ticks in a close-up instrument panel: axes, units, key values and shape. A fifth warning catches a missing negative region and inserts it before the answer is stamped complete.

**Narration:** “Before you stop, inspect the answer. Axes? Units? Key times and values? Correct straight or curved shape? And if the object returns, have you shown the negative velocity region?”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Axes” - first tick
- “units” - unit plates glow
- “shape” - ruler tests the trace
- “negative” - missing return segment drops in

## S10 - Twenty-second recap

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The lab resolves into one clean dashboard: direction arrow, time gates, word card, paired graphs, gradient triangle, signed area tiles and the completed checklist. A final playhead runs through all phases once.

**Narration:** “Recap. Choose positive. Mark the key times. Turn each phrase into the right segment. On displacement-time, gradient gives velocity. On velocity-time, signed area gives displacement change. Label axes, units and key values. Show every stop and every sign change. Then run the examiner’s shape check.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “key times” - all gates flash
- “gradient” - slope bridge pulses
- “signed area” - area bridge pulses
- “shape check” - dashboard locks complete

## Remotion implementation notes

- Compose at \(1920\times1080\), \(30\text{ fps}\), for \(6600\) frames. Use premounted scene sequences and allow only brief lab-light wipes between major bench layouts.
- Drive every lift position, playhead, tangent, area fill and SVG path reveal from `useCurrentFrame()` and the composition frame rate. Clamp interpolations at both ends; use no CSS or third-party chart animations.
- Build graphs from SVG paths and reveal them with one shared progress value per phase. Straight motion segments should draw linearly; reserve heavily damped springs for plates, markers and checklist ticks.
- Keep the paired graphs on one data model so velocity height, displacement gradient, lift position and accumulated area cannot drift apart during seeking or rendering.
