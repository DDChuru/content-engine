# Storyboard: Scalars and Vectors

Format: 16:9 Remotion explainer, approximately 3 minutes 45 seconds. Visuals use an original clean, high-contrast style with simple geometry and character animation. Each scene is a separate voiceover sequence; final scene lengths should be conformed to the generated ElevenLabs audio.

## S01 — Size is not the whole story

**Duration:** 12 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A dashboard dial shows a large reading. It slides left and becomes a glowing dot labelled “size”. A compass rose appears beside it, then an arrow joins the dot. Title resolves from the two ideas.

**Narration:** “Mechanics uses two kinds of quantity. Some tell us only how much. Others also tell us which way. Those are scalars and vectors.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “how much” — reveal the size dot
- “which way” — reveal the compass and arrow
- “scalars” — place the scalar label
- “vectors” — place the vector label

## S02 — Magnitude and direction

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Split screen. Left: a bar grows to twelve with no arrow. Right: the same bar becomes a twelve-unit arrow pointing east. Compact definition cards lock into place below.

**Narration:** “A scalar is complete once you know its magnitude, meaning its size. A vector needs magnitude and direction. Twelve metres per second is a speed. Twelve metres per second east is a velocity.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “scalar” — illuminate the left panel
- “magnitude” — grow the bar
- “direction” — add the arrowhead and compass
- “velocity” — replace the speed label on the right

## S03 — One journey, two measurements

**Duration:** 28 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A person walks along a curved park path, five metres east then two metres west. A route trace accumulates behind them. A straight start-to-finish arrow updates separately. Distance and displacement counters stay side by side.

**Narration:** “Maya walks five metres east, then two metres west. Distance counts every part of her route, so it reaches seven metres. Displacement compares only her finish with her start. She finishes three metres east, so that is her displacement.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “five metres east” — animate the outward walk
- “two metres west” — reverse the walker
- “distance” — route counter reaches seven
- “displacement” — straight arrow settles at three metres east

## S04 — Speed and velocity

**Duration:** 24 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The journey becomes two animated fraction cards. The top uses total route length over time; the bottom uses straight-line displacement over time. A speedometer icon sits above; a directional arrow sits below.

**Narration:** “Speed is distance travelled per unit time. Velocity is displacement per unit time. If Maya’s journey takes seven seconds, her average speed is one metre per second. Her average velocity is three sevenths of a metre per second east.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Speed” — reveal the distance-over-time card
- “Velocity” — reveal the displacement-over-time card
- “one metre per second” — resolve the speed result
- “east” — point and colour the velocity arrow

## S05 — The round-trip test

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A runner travels three metres right, then three metres left. The distance odometer climbs continuously to six. The displacement arrow first grows to three, then shrinks smoothly to zero as the runner returns to the start marker.

**Narration:** “Here is the quickest test. A runner goes three metres right and three metres back. The distance is six metres because the whole route counts. The displacement is zero because the runner finishes at the start.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “right” — runner moves out and displacement arrow grows
- “back” — runner reverses
- “six metres” — distance odometer stops at six
- “zero” — displacement arrow collapses into the start point

## S06 — Acceleration and force

**Duration:** 26 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A car’s velocity arrow lengthens toward the right. A second panel shows a box with a rightward force arrow and matching acceleration arrow. A brief curved-road inset shows that turning can change velocity even at steady speed.

**Narration:** “Acceleration describes a change in velocity, so it is a vector. Speeding up changes velocity. Slowing down changes it too. Even turning changes velocity. Force is also a vector. A resultant force gives an object acceleration in the force’s direction.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Acceleration” — lengthen the car’s velocity arrow
- “Slowing down” — shorten a second arrow
- “turning” — bend the path and rotate the arrow
- “Force” — reveal the force and acceleration arrows on the box

## S07 — Signs encode direction

**Duration:** 25 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A horizontal number line declares right positive. A velocity arrow moves right, flips, and moves left while its label changes from positive to negative. A large magnitude badge remains unchanged during the flip.

**Narration:** “On a straight line, choose a positive direction before you calculate. If right is positive, a leftward velocity has a negative component. Negative does not mean a negative speed. It tells you the object moves opposite to your chosen positive direction.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “positive direction” — highlight the right arrow on the number line
- “leftward” — flip the velocity arrow
- “negative component” — change the signed label
- “opposite” — pulse the unchanged magnitude badge

## S08 — Classification table: scalars

**Duration:** 18 seconds

**Viewer sees:** A two-column table begins empty. The scalar column fills one row at a time. Each row gets a simple icon: speedometer, route, balance, and clock.

**Narration:** “Now build the table. Speed is scalar. Distance is scalar. Mass is scalar. Time is scalar. Each needs a size, but no direction.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Speed” — add speed to the scalar column
- “Distance” — add distance
- “Mass” — add mass
- “Time” — add time

## S09 — Classification table: energy and motion vectors

**Duration:** 18 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The existing table remains. Energy completes the scalar column. Velocity, displacement and acceleration then enter the vector column one by one with arrow icons.

**Narration:** “Energy is scalar as well. Velocity is vector. Displacement is vector. Acceleration is vector. For each vector, direction is part of the answer.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Energy” — add energy to the scalar column
- “Velocity” — add velocity to the vector column
- “Displacement” — add displacement
- “Acceleration” — add acceleration

## S10 — Classification table: force family

**Duration:** 14 seconds

**Viewer sees:** The final three vector rows fill. Force gets a push arrow, weight gets a downward gravity arrow, and momentum gets a moving-object arrow. The completed table briefly enlarges.

**Narration:** “Force is vector. Weight is vector because gravity acts in a direction. Momentum is vector because it follows velocity. That completes the classification.”

**Cue keywords:**

- “Force” — add force
- “Weight” — add weight with a downward arrow
- “Momentum” — add momentum with a motion arrow
- “completes” — expand and centre the finished table

## S11 — Twenty-second recap

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Viewer sees:** Four fast visual callbacks in a single clean grid: magnitude dot versus arrow, route versus shortcut, speedometer versus velocity arrow, and signed number line. Final card asks, “Size only, or size and direction?”

**Narration:** “Recap. A scalar has magnitude only. A vector has magnitude and direction. Distance and speed are scalars. Displacement and velocity are vectors. Acceleration, force, weight and momentum are vectors too. Choose a positive direction, keep your signs consistent, and always ask: size only, or size and direction?”

**Cue keywords:**

- “scalar” — flash the magnitude-only tile
- “vector” — flash the arrow tile
- “positive direction” — show the number line
- “always ask” — resolve the final question card
