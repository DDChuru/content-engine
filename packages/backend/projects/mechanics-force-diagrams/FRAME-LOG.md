## Source-review flags

- No numerical value of \(g\) is used anywhere in f001-f396. The video writes weights symbolically as \(mg\), \(m_1g\), or \(m_2g\); there is therefore no \(g\ne10\) substitution to flag. The supplied PDF states the Cambridge convention \(g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}\) unless told otherwise.
- At f055-f087, f139-f177, f202-f224, f253-f265, f302-f344, and again in the recap at f351-f370, the presenter calls a direction marker an “acceleration arrow” but keeps it pointing in the chosen direction of initial motion even when \(a<0\). This is a signed positive-axis convention, not the acceleration vector when the object is decelerating. It should be labelled as the chosen positive direction, or an actual acceleration arrow should point in the physical direction of acceleration.
- The recap at f358-f370 says \(a>0\) means “accelerates” and \(a<0\) means “decelerates”. That is true only under the video’s local convention that the positive axis follows the current/initial velocity. In general, the sign of \(a\) alone does not determine whether speed increases: acceleration and velocity with the same sign speed a body up; opposite signs slow it down.
- At f120-f125, “Questions will not tell you add weight” is missing “to”. The mathematical advice—to remember the unmentioned weight—is sound.
- At f148-f152 and f161-f168, the car diagram uses \(F\) for combined friction/air resistance and then adds a separate braking force \(B\). In a real braking model, braking force is normally transmitted through tyre-road friction, so treating “friction” and “braking force” as independent arrows can double-count unless \(F\) is explicitly only non-braking resistance such as drag/rolling resistance.
- At f204-f224, the negative-\(a\) claim for a block projected up a smooth slope is correct only with the video’s up-slope positive-axis convention. The important force-diagram point is sound: the initial push is not a continuing “projection force”.
- At f267-f285, \((mg=0)\) for the pulley means the *light pulley model* neglects pulley mass/weight; it does not mean that gravitational acceleration is zero. The two perpendicular tensions give a resultant of magnitude \(T\sqrt2\), so the balancing support reaction \(R_p\) is correctly shown up-right at \(45^\circ\), assuming equal tension in the smooth-pulley string.
- The supplied PDF states that friction acts “in the opposite direction to motion”. More precisely, friction opposes relative sliding or the tendency to slide at the contact; it need not oppose the body’s overall motion (for example, static friction can act forwards on a driven wheel).
- The supplied PDF’s worked-example premise says the block is heavier than the hanging mass “and so” the block would slide down the plane. Mass comparison alone does not establish that direction: the block’s down-slope component \(52\sin\theta\), the hanging weight \(mg\), and the possible direction and magnitude of friction all matter.
- Scope: force identification, the particle model, arrow directions, normal contact and equilibrium in f010-f125 are directly relevant to 9709 §4.1. The acceleration/constant-speed claims and vehicle braking in f126-f177, projected motion in f178-f224, connected-particle/string/pulley constraints in f225-f285, and towing dynamics in f286-f344 extend into §§4.2 and 4.4. The pulley-resultant construction also uses the §4.1 resultant/equilibrium ideas, but the light-string/smooth-pulley model and shared acceleration are §4.4 material.
