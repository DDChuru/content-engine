## Visual and timing contract

Eight scenes, preserving the recording's six sequences between opening and check.
One plain header, one rich diagram, at most one compact caption/problem card.
Captions ≤8 words; problem question ≤3 short lines and ≤12 words. No caption-only
frames. Every situation begins with its object moving before the particle view.
Givens are on the diagram before any force: symbolic masses, surface, motion,
string/rod and pulley assumptions, and the source's slope/string angles.
The question “Which forces act?” precedes every construction, with real silence.
Force arrows grow from the particle sequentially, followed by stroke handwriting
of force name and symbol. Named arrows/figures get local Whisper word-timed loose
rings. Every handwriting stroke completes before the next arrow/hold.
Motion and actual acceleration are dashed and away from the force origin;
positive direction is stated separately. Actual acceleration points downhill or
backward while slowing, correcting the source's ambiguous positive-axis marker.
No numerical calculation or force components. The only formula is weight = mg,
introduced verbally before the symbolic label. No invented continuing push.

## S04 — Driving and braking
Source: f126–f177 and source-review double-counting/sign flags. Outcome: check assumptions.
tempo: slow
voiceSpeed: 0.9
**Duration:** 49 s
**Visual:** Car drives right at steady speed; wheels turn. Givens: mass m, level
road, moving right, engine driving. Build mg, R, D, air resistance A. State right
positive beside separate dashed motion marker. Brake story slows wheels, switches
engine off. Remove D, retain air drag A, add B left. B includes tyre friction:
the collinear drag and braking arrows share the particle origin but use distinct
lengths, separate labels and individual word-timed rings. No extra tyre-friction
arrow. Actual acceleration dashed left, away from the particle, labelled a < 0.
**Narration:** “A car travels right on a level road, engine driving. Which forces act? [hold 2 s] Weight acts down. The normal reaction acts up. Driving force acts right. Air resistance acts left. At steady speed, these horizontal forces balance. Take right as positive. Now brake and stop driving. [pause 1 s] Remove the driving arrow. Add braking force left; air resistance remains. Braking includes tyre friction: do not count it twice. Draw acceleration separately, pointing left. It is negative because right is positive. [hold 2 s]”

Inextensible string: equal accelerations, right and down. [pause 1 s] Isolate the pulley. Light means massless. Tension pulls left; tension pulls down. The support reaction balances both, upwards and right. [hold 2 s] What if the table is rough? Add friction left on the sliding particle. [hold 1 s]”

## S07 — Tension and thrust
Source: f286–f344. Outcome: check changed assumptions.
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

Draw T right on trailer then T left on car. During braking replace with thrust:

**Narration:** “A car pulls a trailer through a light rod. Which connector forces act? [hold 2 s] Tension pulls the trailer right, and the car left. Now the car brakes; the trailer compresses the rod. [pause 1 s] Thrust pushes the trailer left, and the car right. Thrust means compression. Both arrows still start at their particles. What if a string replaces the rod? [hold 1 s] A string cannot push: it goes slack. [hold 1 s]”
