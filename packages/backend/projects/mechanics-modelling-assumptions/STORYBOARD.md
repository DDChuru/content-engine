# Storyboard: Modelling Assumptions

Format: original 16:9 Remotion explainer, approximately **4 minutes (240 seconds)** across eight scenes. Content source: this project's `NOTES.md`; pacing standard: `../mechanics-syllabus-map/PACING.md`. Write and draw everything afresh; do not reuse source-recording wording, audio, images, layouts or branding.

The “mechanics lab” remains: dark navy field (`#081521`), warm-white instrument cards (`#F7F1E6`), cyan construction lines (`#56D6E5`) and amber force or motion arrows (`#F5A623`). Geometric lab props have subtle paper grain. A right-hand index tray collects modelling terms with their consequences; related cards can enter together on one cue.

**Timing contract:** Scene times include narration, thinking time and holds. Cue times are local and align to the quoted spoken keyword. Brisk scenes use short statements and cues every 3–5 seconds. Slow scenes have only three cue keywords, at least eight seconds apart; the working between them is continuous pen movement, not extra keyword/card reveals. Each question gets a one-second thinking pause at its ellipsis. Bracketed directions are silent, not TTS text. After each key numerical input or result is spoken and its meaning explained, hold for two seconds (60 frames): an emphasis ring is already on the value as the hold starts, and nothing else appears or moves. Freeze the pen, diagram, cards and readouts; no transitions during a hold.

## S01 — Reality becomes a particle model

**Duration:** 20 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** A loading bay contains a crate, rope fibres, a flexing trolley and wind ribbons. A cyan scanner strips away irrelevant details. The crate shrinks to a force point, but its mass badge stays lit. Shape and rotation dials switch off. A separate air-resistance switch controls the amber drag arrow. File “particle” and “air resistance neglected” independently.

**Narration:** “A mathematical model keeps the effects that matter and sets other details aside. A particle keeps its mass, but loses size, shape and rotation. Forces meet at one point. Neglecting air resistance is a separate choice. The answer is useful only while those choices remain reasonable.”

**Cue keywords:**

- 02 s — “model” — scanner isolates the essentials
- 07 s — “particle” — crate becomes a point; mass remains
- 12 s — “one point” — force arrows meet
- 16 s — “separate choice” — independent drag switch illuminates

## S02 — Light and inextensible do different jobs

**Duration:** 20 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** String, rod and pulley stations lose their own weight badges. Their inertia indicators fade, including the pulley's flywheel ring. A rough contact strip stays visible: “light” has not removed friction. Below, an elastic connector stretches with unequal endpoint motion; beside it a taut inextensible string keeps its length. Equal travel markers and acceleration magnitudes follow different directions around a pulley. File separate light-string, light-rod, light-pulley and inextensible-string cards.

**Narration:** “Light means negligible mass: the string and rod add no weight; the pulley adds no rotational inertia. Light does not mean smooth. A stretchy string lets its ends move differently. A taut inextensible string keeps its length, giving equal travel and acceleration magnitudes along the string.”

**Cue keywords:**

## S03 — Smooth ice, rough sand

**Duration:** 20 seconds
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0

**Viewer sees:** A block crosses an icy cyan plate, then amber sand. The smooth surface supplies only a perpendicular normal reaction. On sand, friction opposes the indicated actual or impending slide. Above, a light string runs around a smooth pulley and a smooth peg; the tension gauges agree on either side. File both contact cards and both redirector cards.

**Narration:** “A smooth surface supplies no friction, only a normal reaction. A rough surface may supply friction, opposing sliding or the slide about to begin. A smooth pulley or peg removes contact friction there. With a light string, the tension is the same on both sides.”

**Cue keywords:**

## S06 — Worked lab test: what the assumptions let us calculate

**Duration:** 75 seconds
tempo: slow
voiceSpeed: 0.9

**Viewer sees:** Keep one layout throughout: a block on a horizontal bench and a hanging block at left; a warm-white **lined-paper panel beside the diagram** at right. The particles are connected by a light, taut, inextensible string over a smooth, light pulley; the bench is smooth and air resistance is neglected. These assumption cards are present from the start. Draw both force diagrams, including the bench block's weight and normal reaction, before writing the equations. Positive acceleration arrows follow each block's path.

All working is original **handwritten strokes appearing line by line at pen pace**, with visible pen lifts and a hand/pen tip following the active stroke, in the ink-tutor style. Do not pop in typeset equations or whole lines. Keep completed lines visible. Do not move the blocks while the viewer reads a result.

| Local time | Narration, including silent pause directions | Working and holds |
|---|---|---|
| 00–10 s | “Which mass is supported? ... Three kilograms. This is the bench block. [hold 2 s]” | Establish `3 kg`; ring it and freeze 08–10 s after the spoken meaning. |
| 10–19 s | “Which mass hangs? ... Two kilograms. This block drives the motion. [hold 2 s]” | Establish `2 kg`; ring it and freeze 17–19 s. |
| 19–33 s | “What gravity value will we use? ... Nine point eight metres per second squared. This is gravitational acceleration. [hold 2 s]” | Establish `g = 9.8 m s⁻²`; ring it and freeze 31–33 s. |
| 33–43 s | “So... what equations follow? ... Tension accelerates the bench block; weight minus tension accelerates the hanging block.” | Trace the force arrows, then handwrite `T = 3a` and, on the next line, `2g − T = 2a`. The light string and smooth pulley give one `T`; taut and inextensible gives one acceleration magnitude `a`, with different directions. |
| 43–51 s | “What mass moves? ... Five kilograms. That is the combined mass. [hold 2 s]” | Add the equations by handwriting `2g = (3 + 2)a = 5a`; ring `5` and freeze 49–51 s. |
| 51–63 s | “What acceleration follows? ... Three point nine two metres per second squared. Both blocks share this magnitude. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `a = 2(9.8)/5 = 3.92 m s⁻²`. Complete the strokes before naming the result; ring `3.92` and freeze 61–63 s. |
| 63–75 s | “What is the tension? ... Eleven point eight newtons. That is the pull on either block. [hold 2 s]” | Handwrite `T = 3(3.92) ≈ 11.8 N`, with a small “3 s.f.” note completed before the hold. Ring `11.8` and freeze 73–75 s; keep the unrounded acceleration in the substitution. |

**Cue keywords:**

- 01 s — “supported” — attend to the bench block's input label
- 35 s — “equations” — trace the existing assumption links, then begin the working

## S07 — Why it matters: justify the shortcut, then test it

**Duration:** 50 seconds
tempo: slow
voiceSpeed: 0.9

**Viewer sees:** Retain the worked rig and paper. The index becomes a consequence sorter, working through existing cards one at a time. No new numerical results are introduced. In the final beat, sand replaces the smooth bench and an opposing friction arrow is drawn; handwrite `2g − F = 5a` below the earlier equation. The old acceleration stays labelled “smooth bench only”; a qualitative downward indicator shows that friction reduces acceleration for the same driven motion, without inventing a new value.

**Narration:** “So... why do the assumption words earn marks? ... They justify the shortcut. Smooth removes friction; light plus a smooth redirector gives equal tension. What fixes the other choices? ... A taut inextensible string gives equal acceleration magnitudes. Uniform gives midpoint weight for a straight rod. Particle gives one force point, but says nothing about drag. What changes on a rough bench? ... Friction reduces the driving force, so acceleration falls. Check the answer's size, sign, units and plausibility. Refine the model if those checks fail.”

**Cue keywords:**

- 07 s — “shortcut” — route “no friction” and “equal tension” to the correct assumptions over the first beat
- 17 s — “other choices” — route equal acceleration, midpoint weight and one force point over the second beat; reject the particle/no-drag pairing
- 34 s — “rough bench” — introduce friction, then write the revised force balance at pen pace

**Reading rhythm:** Leave each matched consequence visible while explaining it. A fixed-length string only locks motion while taut; its two acceleration vectors can point in different directions. Keep that diagram visible through the explanation. Reserve the last five seconds for the spoken checks and the settled rig; no new card sequence. Existing numerical labels and equation coefficients are not reintroduced as fresh results.
