## S03 — Model a falling stone

**Duration:** 76 seconds (00:52–02:08)
tempo: slow
voiceSpeed: 0.9
**Maps to:** O2. **Source:** f017–f049; correct the f042 reading using the displayed model.

**Narration:** “A stone at a cliff: two or three dimensional motion; air and wind; size, shape and spin; gravity pulls down. Assume a vertical fall. Ignore air and wind. Use a particle: omit dimensions. Keep gravity constant, simplifying height. Our model is h equals twenty minus five t squared, from zero to two seconds. At zero, height is twenty metres. [hold 2 s] At one second, height is fifteen metres. [hold 2 s] Two seconds means the stone reaches the water. Is one metre at one second reasonable? [hold 3 s] No: that misreads this model, which gives fifteen. To improve realism, include air resistance; keep the stone's size and mass; or allow motion in two or three dimensions.”

**Main anchors:** real situation → model and readings → critique and refine.

**Viewer sees:** Cliff and curved path; a side card successively identifies real motion, air/wind, size/shape/spin and gravity. As each simplification is spoken, the same path becomes vertical, air/wind marks disappear, the stone becomes a point, then the gravity arrow becomes constant. The card changes to lined paper at the model cue. Handwrite `h = 20 - 5t²`, then `0 ≤ t ≤ 2` at the domain cue; replace the domain line with the two readings `h(0) = 20 m` and `h(1) = 15 m` as spoken. Complete and ring each result before its 60-frame hold. At `water`, move the point to the waterline, retaining the model. ## S04 — What the modelling words mean

**Duration:** 49 seconds (02:08–02:57)
tempo: brisk
voiceSpeed: 1.0
**Maps to:** O1. **Source:** f050–f083.

**Narration:** “A particle ignores dimensions: size is unimportant here. Smooth means no friction. Rough allows friction, opposing sliding. A rod or beam keeps length and rigid shape. Uniform means evenly spread mass: weight acts at its centre; uneven mass shifts it. Light means negligible mass: omit string weight. A heavy string adds weight. Inextensible means no stretching. Taut over this fixed pulley, it links equal acceleration magnitudes in different directions. A stretching string breaks that link.”

**Viewer sees:** Reuse the box/table/pulley diagram, replacing it temporarily with the existing rod detail. One term card at a time: `Particle`, `Smooth surface`, `Rough surface`, `Rod or beam`, `Uniform rod`, `Light string`, `Inextensible string`. The current term or feature alone uses the accent. The rough cue introduces the opposing friction arrow in the existing diagram. The rod cue restores retained length; uniform cue distributes mass evenly, centre cue adds its weight, uneven cue moves it. Light cue removes string weight, heavy cue restores it. Inextensible cue restores a thin taut string; acceleration cue adds the linked directional indicator; stretching cue shows unequal motion. No extended force calculation, extra glossary, or numeric labels.

## S05 — Match words to the situation

**Duration:** 64 seconds (02:57–04:01)
tempo: slow
voiceSpeed: 0.9
**Maps to:** O3, supported by O1. **Source:** f084–f103, with the particle/air-resistance distinction corrected.

**Narration:** “A box on a desk connects over a pulley to a hanging sphere. Match five simplifications. Ignoring air resistance: which body model is paired here? [hold 2 s] Particle. But neglecting air resistance is a separate assumption; a point model can still have drag. Ignore string mass: which word? [hold 2 s] Light, because the string's mass is negligible. Same tension on both sides: which pulley? [hold 2 s] Smooth pulley, with our light string and ideal pulley. Ignore desk friction: which surface? [hold 2 s] Smooth surface: no friction. Same acceleration magnitudes: which string? [hold 2 s] Inextensible, while taut, because its fixed length links the movements.”

**Main anchors:** setup and particle → light and smooth pulley → smooth surface and inextensible.
