## Source-review flags

- At f042-f049, substituting (t=1) into the displayed (h=20-5t^2) gives (h=15\text{ m}), not the stated (1\text{ m}).
- At f092-f103, and in the PDF answer on page 15, particle dimensions are used to justify ignoring air resistance. That does not follow: a particle model removes size and rotational detail but may still include a drag force. “Air resistance is neglected” is a separate assumption.
- At f066-f071, “long, rigid particle” is internally contradictory. A particle has negligible dimensions; a rod or beam retains its length so that force positions and moments can matter. “Slender rigid body” would be safer wording.
- At f076-f083 and f117, “inextensible” is broadened to an object that cannot be stretched or compressed. For a string, the useful statement is that its length does not increase while it is taut. A string does not transmit compression; it goes slack.
- At f078-f083 and f100-f103, “same acceleration” should be read as equal magnitudes along a taut string. The two acceleration vectors in the shown pulley system point in different directions.
- The stone equation (h=20-5t^2) also assumes zero initial vertical velocity and uses (g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}); those choices are not stated explicitly.
- Uniformity makes the centre of mass coincide with the relevant geometric centroid; it does not put that point at the colloquial “centre” of every shape. The midpoint result shown is correct for the uniform straight rod, but the recap wording is broader than the example.
