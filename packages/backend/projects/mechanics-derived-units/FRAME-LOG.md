## Concepts covered by the source

- Derived units as combinations of S.I. units.
- Speed and velocity in `m s^-1`, including the scalar/vector distinction.
- Acceleration in `m s^-2` as change of velocity per second.
- Force as a vector in newtons, with `F = ma` and `1 N = 1 kg m s^-2`.
- Weight as the gravitational force, with `W = mg` and location-dependent `g`.
- Separate numerator-and-denominator conversions for `km h^-1` and `km h^-2`.
- Converting grams before using `F = ma`.
- Converting pressure from `N cm^-2` to `N m^-2`.
- Momentum, work and energy are not covered.

## Accuracy and wording checks

- The numerical conversions for `45 km h^-1`, `48 km h^-1`, `324 km h^-2`, `225 g` with `3 m s^-2`, and `0.004 N cm^-2` are correct.
- In the first acceleration example, the working starts from `12.96 km h^-2` and correctly reaches `0.001 m s^-2`, but the final line changes the input to `129.6 km h^-2`. That displayed equality is wrong: `129.6 km h^-2 = 0.01 m s^-2`.
- Acceleration should be read as “metres per second squared”, or understood as “metres per second per second”. The source's phrases “metres per square second squared” and “metres per square second” are malformed.
- In `W = mg`, `g` is the local acceleration due to gravity, or gravitational field strength. It is not the universal gravitational constant; that distinct constant is normally written `G`.
- The summary contains a duplicated word in “Derived units units”.
- The source capitalises the written unit name “Newtons”. Standard S.I. style uses lowercase “newton” or “newtons”, while the symbol remains uppercase `N`.
