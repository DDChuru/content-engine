# Storyboard: Derived Units for Mechanics

Format: 16:9 Remotion explainer. Target duration: about 3 minutes 38 seconds. The visual language continues the original “measurement lab”: dark navy space, warm white instrument cards, cyan construction lines and amber force arrows. Scenes should be separate Remotion sequences, with exact frame lengths adjusted to the final voiceover. Cue-driven reveals should use frame-based timing so each number and unit arrives on the spoken beat.

## S01 — Units that reveal the equation

**Duration:** 12 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A dark workbench holds three glowing base-unit tiles: kilogram, metre and second. A speed dial, rocket gauge and force sensor sit unpowered behind them. The title “Derived Units” etches itself in cyan across a warm white card.

**Narration:** “A unit can tell you more than the scale of an answer. It can reveal how the quantity was built. Let’s assemble the units used in mechanics.”

**Cue keywords:** “unit”; “reveal”; “assemble”; “mechanics”

## S02 — The base-unit builder

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** Metre, second and kilogram tiles slide along cyan rails into a magnetic assembly bay. A multiplication joint and a division slot appear. Several faint silhouettes preview the derived-unit shapes they can make.

**Narration:** “Metres, seconds and kilograms are base units. A derived unit combines them by multiplication or division. The way they combine follows the equation for the quantity.”

**Cue keywords:** “base units”; “combines”; “multiplication or division”; “equation”

## S03 — Speed and velocity

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A cyan car marker moves along a measured track. One metre tile snaps above a fraction bar and one second tile below it. The assembly folds into an “m s⁻¹” readout. A direction arrow switches on beside velocity but not speed.

**Narration:** “Speed is distance divided by time. So its unit is metres per second. Velocity uses the same unit, but velocity also carries direction. That is why speed is scalar and velocity is vector.”

**Cue keywords:** “distance divided by time”; “metres per second”; “direction”; “vector”

## S04 — Per second, per second

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A velocity readout rises by the same amount at each one-second tick. A second denominator tile joins the speed unit, and two amber brackets label the two roles of time: velocity already contains one second; change happens over another second.

**Narration:** “Acceleration measures how quickly velocity changes. Start with metres per second, then divide by time once more. The result is metres per second squared. Think of it as metres per second, per second.”

**Cue keywords:** “velocity changes”; “divide by time”; “second squared”; “per second, per second”

## S05 — Convert the fraction, piece by piece

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A compound-unit card opens into a large fraction. The numerator travels through a cyan distance converter; the denominator travels through an amber clock converter. A superscript two duplicates the clock conversion when acceleration is selected.

**Narration:** “For a compound unit, separate the top from the bottom. Convert kilometres to metres in the numerator. Convert hours to seconds in the denominator. If the time unit is squared, square its conversion factor too.”

**Cue keywords:** “top”; “bottom”; “hours to seconds”; “squared”

## S06 — Worked conversion: seventy-two to twenty

**Duration:** 32 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A stylised car sits beside a digital speedometer reading “72 km/h”. Numbers appear exactly as they are spoken. First “72 × 1000 = 72 000 m/h” builds on the numerator lane. Then “72 000 ÷ 3600 = 20 m/s” builds on the denominator lane. The needle sweeps to a second highlighted mark labelled “20 m/s”.

**Narration:** “Convert seventy-two kilometres per hour. First handle the numerator. Seventy-two times one thousand gives seventy-two thousand metres per hour. Now handle the denominator. One hour is three thousand six hundred seconds. Seventy-two thousand divided by three thousand six hundred gives twenty metres per second.”

**Cue keywords:** “seventy-two”; “one thousand”; “three thousand six hundred”; “twenty metres per second”

## S07 — Build one newton

**Duration:** 26 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** A one-kilogram block rests on a low-friction lab rail. An amber arrow pushes it, and cyan timing gates show its speed increasing by one metre per second every second. The kilogram tile locks to the acceleration tiles and stamps “1 N”.

**Narration:** “Force equals mass multiplied by acceleration. Put kilograms beside metres per second squared, and you get kilograms metres per second squared. That combination has a shorter name: the newton. One newton accelerates one kilogram by one metre per second squared.”

**Cue keywords:** “mass multiplied by acceleration”; “kilograms”; “newton”; “one kilogram”

## S08 — Weight changes with location

**Duration:** 22 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** The same labelled mass stands first on an Earth platform and then on a Moon platform. Its mass card does not change. The downward amber weight arrow becomes shorter on the Moon, while a side card assembles “W = mg”.

**Narration:** “Weight is a force, so we measure it in newtons. It equals mass multiplied by the local acceleration due to gravity. Move the object to the Moon. Its mass stays the same, while its weight becomes smaller.”

**Cue keywords:** “Weight”; “newtons”; “local acceleration”; “Moon”

## S09 — Powers are part of the unit

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** Two quick lab checks share the frame. On the left, an hours-squared tile fans into two time-conversion layers. On the right, a one-centimetre square expands into a one-hundred-by-one-hundred grid, turning a pressure label from “N/cm²” into “N/m²”. Red warning lights flash when a power is omitted.

**Narration:** “Powers belong to the conversion as well as the unit. Hours squared needs the hours-to-seconds factor twice. Square centimetres needs the length factor twice. Missing that power can move an answer by thousands.”

**Cue keywords:** “Powers”; “factor twice”; “Square centimetres”; “thousands”

## S10 — Twenty-second recap

**Duration:** 20 seconds

**What the viewer sees:** The lab assembles three final cards in sequence: speed, acceleration and force. A fourth card drops an amber weight arrow beside the unchanged mass tile. The closing checklist illuminates: “build”, “convert each part”, “keep powers”, “check the unit”.

**Narration:** “Quick recap. Derived units combine base units. Speed uses metres per second. Acceleration uses metres per second squared. A newton is one kilogram metre per second squared, and weight is a force. Convert each part of a compound unit, keep every power, and let the final unit check your work.”

**Cue keywords:** “Derived units”; “Acceleration”; “newton”; “check your work”
